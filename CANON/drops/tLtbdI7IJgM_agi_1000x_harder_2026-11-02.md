## 1. What the source actually says

**The frame [POETIC].** A narrated cold open [0:00–0:35], then the thesis: today's AI "can learn from almost the entire internet" yet "still struggles with something your brain does every single day." **The thesis [HYP] — general intelligence as a STACK of learning levels, each learning from the level below it.** The whole 25 minutes argues that AGI is hard because humans do not just learn — they do five things AI mostly can't, each cheaper-in-experience for a human than for a machine:

1. **Continual learning / associative learning [EST for the phenomenon, HYP for the framing].** Anchored on the 1989 McCloskey & Cohen addition-network experiment [1:16] and **Catastrophic Forgetting** [1:50]: a net trained on the "ones facts" then the "two facts" forgets the ones, because *"the connections that support one skill are often the same ones that support another skill"* [~2:30]. (Note the transcript says "ones facts" / "twos" / "two facts" — the ASR splits "twos".) The proposed remedy is **Replay** [2:59] — mixing old data back in, which is what pretraining already does — but that *"incredibly expensive and time consuming"* [~4:00].
2. **Selective learning / certainty-weighted updating [EST for Bayesian inference, HYP for the neural mechanism].** The coffee-shop-closed vs gravity-stopped-working contrast [~5:30–6:00]: *"your brain doesn't just store what you know. It also keeps track of how certain you are about what you know"* [5:51]. This is **Bayesian inference** [~11:00], and the narrator notes *"Some neuroscientists even hypothesize that individual synapses keep track of how certain they are about their connection strength"* [~11:15] — flagged by the narrator himself as a hypothesis.
3. **Metacognition — questioning the frame [HYP].** The pivot is the Einstein/Le Verrier vignette [~9:00–11:00]: **Urbain Le Verrier** (1859) proposed the planet **Vulcan** to save Newton's theory rather than abandoning it, because *"he kept Newton's theory, and found something within that theory that explains the new evidence"* [10:53]; Vulcan was never found; Einstein's general relativity replaced the frame instead. The narrator draws the load-bearing distinction: *"Bayesian inference can tell you how likely each explanation is, given the evidence. But it can't tell you which explanations to consider in the first place"* [11:43]. This is the ability to *"step back and question your own thinking"* — **Metacognition** [12:23].
4. **Intuition — compressed experience [HYP].** A **1973** chess study [19:01] (unnamed) shows a master recalls most pieces from a 5-second glance of a real position but loses the advantage on random placements; **DeepMind's AlphaGo** combined learned intuition with search to beat **Lee Sedol in 2016** [20:00–20:10] — but Go is closed, the real world open-ended, so intuition doesn't transfer by self-play.
5. **Instincts — evolved priors [HYP].** *"pain, joy, hunger, fear, curiosity"* [~21:30], *"tuned over hundreds of millions of years of evolution"* [~21:40], the thing that tells a newborn what to attend to before any intuition exists.
