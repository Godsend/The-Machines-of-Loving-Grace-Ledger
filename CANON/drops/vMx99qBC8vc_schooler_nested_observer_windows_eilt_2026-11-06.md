## 5. External verification (CA46 / CA75 — nothing relayed as fact)

### 5.1 The paper the talk is based on — verified, with exact bibliographic data
The talk's own pointer (`[04:09]`, `[37:46]`) plus the channel description's link (PMID **38504828**) resolve to: **Riddle J, Schooler JW. **[EST-ext]** The case matching the talk's description (twins joined at the head, a **"thalamic bridge"** between their thalami, reports of seeing through each other's eyes and shared taste/touch, and a reported non-verbal internal communication channel) is **Krista and Tatiana Hogan** — the thalamic-bridge structure is the term used in the reporting, and the sharing claims are attributed to their neurosurgeon and to documentary reporting, with **no controlled study**. **[C] Caveat carried into the drop:** the popular account is explicitly hedged — *"there's no empirical test that can conclusively establish that for some sensations the twins share one token experience rather than two exactly matching token experiences."* So the talk's use of the case as evidence **for multiple streams of consciousness** borrows a case whose evidentiary status is contested by the very reporting that established it.

### 5.4 The theta claim — right band family, coarser than the literature
**[EST-ext]** Cortical tracking of speech is real and hierarchical: **theta (4-8 Hz) tracks the syllable rhythm** and **delta (1-4 Hz) tracks word/phrase-level structure**; Ding et al. (2015, *Nature Neuroscience* 19:158-164) showed cortical activity tracking linguistic structures **at different hierarchical levels** for connected speech (and their 2017 EEG follow-up gave peaks at ~4 Hz, ~2 Hz and ~1 Hz for words, phrases and sentences). The talk's *"all of them seem to … focus on this theta wave … period"* `[17:08]` is therefore **band-family correct but coarse**: the hierarchical-tracking literature divides the labour between theta *and* delta, and assigns the phrase/sentence levels to slower bands than theta. **[HYP]** The stronger reading he offers — theta as **"the carrier wave"** enabling cross-region crosstalk — is a framework claim, consistent with the literature but not established by it.

### 5.5 ★ The time-dilation illustration is contradicted by the experiment it evokes
**[EST]** On tape: *"when you're in some sort of very dangerous situation, the frame rate may go very fast, and that's why you experience a … time slowing down when … you perceive impending death"* `[15:34]`.
**[EST-ext] The counter-evidence is the canonical study of exactly that scenario:** Stetson C, Fiesta MP, Eagleman DM, "Does Time Really Slow Down during a Frightening Event?", *PLoS ONE* 2007;2(12):e1295 (doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0001295). Participants in free fall (31 m into a net) **showed no increase in temporal resolution** — they could not read a wrist-mounted display flashing at faster-than-normal rates — while retrospectively estimating their own fall as **~36 % longer** than others'. The authors' conclusion: **"time-slowing is a function of recollection, not perception"**; subjective time "is not a single entity that speeds or slows". This is the sharpest **`[DIVERGENCE]` that cuts at the source**: the frame-rate reading of fright-induced time dilation is the specific hypothesis the free-fall experiment tested and failed to support, and the talk presents it as illustration without flagging the conflict. *(Attribution discipline: the talk does not cite Stetson et al.; the counter-evidence was located by this drop, and the claim on tape is credited to the speaker, not to the record.)*
