## The setup (accurate background, `E`)

**Hubble tension:** two routes to the same number, H₀ (current expansion rate) — the **local distance ladder** (stars, supernovae) vs. inference from the **cosmic microwave background**. They disagree by **more than 5σ**.

The field's priors have shifted: it was assumed to be a measurement problem — systematics, calibration — and those were double-checked repeatedly. The tension **persisted and grew**. Live alternatives:
- **dynamical dark energy** (not a constant), which would also ease other minor tensions;
- a camp arguing the problem is in the **data analysis itself** — that the supernova results are wrong and the tension *"is just the tip of the iceberg."* Sabine notes the consequence bluntly: if that camp is right, *"the Nobel Prize in Physics from 2011 might have been awarded in error."*

## The new claim (`E`, as reported)

The paper's authors accept that the analysis may be wrong — but say it is **not the supernovae; it is the CMB**, and the reason is that **magnetic fields have been underestimated**.

- **Primordial magnetic fields** — the universe was **born with magnetic fields already in place.**
- **Mechanism:** in the pre-recombination plasma, magnetic fields move the charged particles, which *"helps the atomic nuclei to find atoms"* — so **the CMB light escapes earlier** than assumed. A small timing change alters how H₀ is **inferred** from the CMB.
- **Independent motivation:** **galactic magnetic fields of unknown origin** are observed. If these are primordial, then *"our universe was born being magnetic."*
