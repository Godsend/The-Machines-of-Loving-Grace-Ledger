## §2 — The actual argument (six moves)

1. **The shock.** Borcherds: AI math "was kind of amusing" two years ago — *"plausible looking stuff that was in fact complete garbage"* — and *"then suddenly about summer this year, they suddenly went from being mediocre undergraduates to being the best mathematicians on the planet,"* wiping out a series of deep conjectures. `[A]`
2. **Weinstein's refinement — the split.** Conjecture-solving is *"the cleanest place where we have gamified mathematics"* (the sword-in-the-stone). He concedes the problem-solving tsunami and adds the load-bearing distinction: *"the AI is as yet nowhere in what I would call theory building."* He flags that many conjectures have fallen to **counter-examples**, which he finds *"somewhat counter to my intuition."* `[A]`
3. **Borcherds' correction — selection bias and "glorified calculation."** Counter-examples are found because they are *"much easier … than to prove a conjecture"*; and *"a lot of what AI is doing at the moment is a sort of glorified version of a lot of intense calculation."* Example: the **complex structure on the six-sphere** — *"that could have been found by humans if they were prepared to spend five years doing endless calculations."* `[A]`
4. **The sociology.** Mathematicians *"are hiding their problems"*; credit is the real currency (Weinstein on grad school: *"mathematicians care tremendously about credit"*, and *"we have misrepresented ourselves as near saints"*); *"cherry topping"*; Perelman/Hamilton; by contrast, the funding case is being lost because mathematicians themselves advertise their work as useless (Borcherds: *"I am really puzzled about why anybody pays me"*; *"most of it was finished off about 100 or 200 years ago"*), while Weinstein calls that *"a suicidal aesthetic"* and argues from **public goods / market failure** (*"inexhaustible and inexcludable"*). `[A]`
5. **Verification vs understanding.** On the ~100-page AI-generated proof: *"It did come with a formal proof certificate in this language called Lean. So we do know it's correct. But … we've learned almost nothing mathematically from what they did."* Borcherds' general law: *"pioneering work is always clumsy"* — Hilbert later simplified 19th-c. invariant theory; Feynman thought renormalisation was a trick until **Ken Wilson**. Independently: in the classification of finite simple groups AI *"completely understands the classification, but it hasn't yet managed to sort of digest and simplify it to a form that humans can understand"*; the 26 sporadic groups still have no half-page explanation like the Lie groups do. `[A]`
6. **The stake (Weinstein, escalating).** Math as *"the fabric of existence"*; exceptional algebra as an unreached mainland (*"Octonionic 3-Space is at the heart of all of the large exceptional simple groups"*); physics *"right at the finish line"*; the chess trajectory ("humans will just get in the way"); **stop making the machine human-aligned**; and the closing meta-question — **what is the square root** as *"the psychedelic of mathematics."* `[A]/[POETIC]`
