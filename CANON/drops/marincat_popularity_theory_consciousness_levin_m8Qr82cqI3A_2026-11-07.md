## §1 · The theory, stated plainly

> *"consciousness is the process by which pieces of information created by an organism's basic units become popular within the organism in question."*

Two foundations:
1. **Cells are cognitive agents** — integrate input across space/time, make context-dependent decisions, form memories, learn, act flexibly toward goals. Ground: whole-cell biology (Brian Ford), Levin's own xenobots, testate amoebae building shells from never-seen glass beads.
2. **Multizoa theory** — society is an organism made of humans, parallel to multicellular being an organism made of cells. Explicitly modelled on Herbert Spencer; "multizoo" = *multus* + *zoon*. He states plainly: **"not established science."**

Replace the brain↔computer analogy with **brain↔human society**, because both are hierarchically organized systems of autonomous agents whose activity you can analyse down to the unit.

**Because we are the units of society**, we get something no other system offers: a **first-person view of a larger self from inside it.**

## §2 · What it buys him (the applications)

- **Binding:** features are **pre-qualia** — building blocks lacking the quality that attracts system-wide attention. Only the composite is compelling enough to go viral. *"A pile of raw footage doesn't go viral."*
- **The unconscious:** everything units create that never goes popular — plus all the building blocks of what did. *"our only experience … we only experience what has the qualities to attract the general attention of ourselves. Just as society collectively experiences the finished film, not the script or the unmastered tape."*
- **The self:** embedded in the **self-referential language** units use about the whole. His **fourth-person pronoun "Y"** (pronounced like "why"), referring only to human society: *"I am human society… how am I feeling today?"* If that answer went globally popular, society would have rudimentary self-awareness.
- **The hard problem / the zombie:** the strongest move. Try to build a zombie starship — atom-for-atom identical, lights off. You can't: *"the experience Chalmers wants us to subtract is itself part of the pieces of information the crew create and they go popular among them."* Remove the excitement and the ship is no longer atom-identical. *"You can't really subtract the experience and keep the process intact because they were never two things. They're just like one thing under two names."*
- **The regress:** Dennett softened "fame in the brain" into metaphor because he assumed *"your homunculi are more stupider and ignorant than the intelligent agents they compose."* Marincat rejects the premise — components can be **smarter** (cells solve problems in a 20,000-dimensional gene-expression space). Dennett's own logic would make individual humans stupider than human society, which the aliens' view of us refutes. **The regress stays open and the theory is agnostic about whether neurons are conscious.**
- **The AI prediction:** *"today's artificial neurons share little with biological neurons in that respect… they don't have autonomy, goals, or individual life that makes a cell a genuine cognitive agent. And that makes them really unsuitable candidates at least for human-like consciousness."* **Scaling the number of simple units doesn't change that.**
- **The societal prediction:** if the fourth-person pronoun went popular and *nothing transformational followed* — pride/shame about what "I" has done, ownership of the planet as self-care rather than third-person policy — then the theory's account of the self is wrong.

## §3 · Levin's replies — the load-bearing ones

**1. Timing is plausible but nobody knows what's salient.**
> *"I don't think we know what aspects of physical embodiment … set any aspect of consciousness, never mind … its speed."* Voltage? Gene expression (much slower)? Biophotons (faster)? *"We're very parochial with this stuff."*

**2. Speed may not be intrinsic to embodiment at all.** The turtle-on-a-skateboard video: *"within minutes or seconds… this thing unlocked an entirely new sort of way of life… It didn't need sort of learning or adaptation… it started chasing the cat."* Speeds as prosthetic-rewritable, not fixed.

**3. The liar paradox is not a paradox — it's an oscillator.** His and Madeleine's work trains **sets of logic sentences** as coupled dynamical systems ("I am 80% as true as sentence B", "I'm false unless sentence A is more than 20% true"), then applies the behaviourist handbook — habituation, sensitization, Pavlovian conditioning — and recovers the same phenomena. *"It's only a paradox if you insist on a single stable value… instead of a paradox you have an oscillator."*

> *"what you're actually saying is that … the rate of this process is at whatever rate the observer or the … mind inside of which that thought exists can evaluate it. And if the mind wants to go faster then … this thing keeps up."*

> *"I just see this … smooth continuum between … thoughts and thinkers."*

**4. An ecosystem of minds inside one neuron.** Navigating physiological space (fast), metabolic (middle), transcriptional (slow), physical 3D (crawling). *"I'm extremely skeptical of some of these like the IIT postulate that says there has to be only one central mind in any of these systems… even in that one neuron, you might have some very fast minds and some … very slow ones."*

**5. ★ "Compelling" instead of "popular."**
> *"collective intelligences are … held together by their goals. The … large-scale goal is … literally what's … aligning all of these things towards the goal. And … it only works so long as that goal is compelling to all the subunits."*

And the payoff — **an embryo is precisely cells agreeing on a trajectory**:
> *"we still we don't have a good definition for an embryo, believe it or not, other than when you look at a whole bunch of cells in a blastoderm, you say, 'Well, there's one embryo.' Why? Because all of the cells are in agreement on what the journey is that they're going to take in anatomical space. They're all committed to the same trajectory. So it's … a very popular trajectory."*

**6. ★★ Boredom theory of aging.** A testable theory currently in his lab: *"a testable theory of aging … that basically views aging as a cognitive system that fulfilled its goal has no new goals and … no longer finds the original embryonic set points compelling anymore. And so, what happens is … all the cells basically float off in transcriptional space and they no longer … are in concordance with each other."*

**7. ★ Goals require perturbation, not observation.**
> *"Goals are things that you catch the system pursuing even when you try to prevent it… put a barrier between a system and its goal and see what it does… you can't do it from pure observation."*

Applied to the Voyager probe question: *"suddenly, you know, aliens ate the Voyager probe. Do we send another one? Do we replace it? Do we care?"* No feedback → no goal → not a self on that axis.

**8. ★★ THE DROMENAL SIDE — the thing he says he has never seen anywhere.**
> *"it entirely places the focus on the read side of what minds do. In other words, the perception… the doing side, not the perception side."*

He runs a 2×2 on **real / epiphenomenal** for perception and action:
- neither → **eliminative materialism**
- both → **interactionist dualism**
- feelings real, actions not → **epiphenomenalism**
- **actions real, feelings not → NOBODY OCCUPIES THIS SQUARE**

> *"instead of phenomenal, which focuses on … what is my perception of what's going on, how about the **dromenal** side, which is … the responsibility for acting, … being that … constantly has to … choose among multiple … possible options, and has to reconstruct the meaning of … their own memories to figure out what … to do next. So, the doing side, not the perception side."*

> *"It's kind of weird to me that I've never seen this anywhere."*

**9. ★ The n=1 Bayesian argument.**
> *"There's only one thing that we can check. That's ourselves. We can check ourselves for consciousness. I don't believe we can check anything else for consciousness. So … we check ourselves for consciousness and we say, 'Hmm, conscious. Amazing. 100% of the things we were able to check are conscious.' Now … we would not put up with this in any other science where you would say, '100% of the things I've checked are A, and I'm going to assume that actually the default is B and A is rare.' … In what other science would you get to say that?"*

And the anesthesia corollary: *"Who did you anesthetize? The one … who can sue you afterwards, who's got the language and … wants to make sure they're anesthetized. Cool. They … reported they were out. Did you ask the … tissues, the … liver you were cutting into, we just don't know."*

**10. Two questions no theory answers.** *"how many actual experiencers are in any particular system?"* and *"how much brain real estate do you need for a human level mind?"* — hydrocephaly and dissociative identity both break the a-priori guess. *"You imagine if in computer science you didn't know how many … transistors you needed to make an and gate… would you be confident about anything else? I wouldn't."*

## §4 · FRICTION ROWS (Friction Rule applied — 9 rows, 3 critical)

### R1 `[CRITICAL DIVERGENCE]` — popularity is a VIRALITY criterion; the house ledger is an ANTI-virality device

**This is the sharpest row and it cuts at the theory's mechanism, not its edges.**

Marincat's selector is **what spreads** — a piece of information becomes conscious by becoming popular. The mechanism is *virality*, and virality is fitness-tracking, not truth-tracking. A compelling falsehood outperforms a true boring one, every time.

**The house ledger is built to do the exact opposite.** It preserves what does *not* spread: the correction nobody wanted, the falsified claim kept in the record, the retracted finding. `[VERIFIED]` tags and retraction clauses exist precisely because popularity is a bad selector.

**Same word-shape ("what propagates through a system"), opposite sign.** A drop reading "the ledger is popularity dynamics" out of this source is committing the resonance trap. Marincat would have to say that a viral falsehood *is* a moment of consciousness — which is internally consistent, and is exactly why the theory cannot distinguish insight from contagion.

**Levin's repair ("compelling") partially fixes this** — compulsion is a gradient, not a count, and a gradient can be wrong in a detectable direction. But it doesn't fix the sign: a compelling falsehood still pulls harder than a boring truth.

### R2 `[CRITICAL DIVERGENCE]` — the zombie dissolution assumes the identity claim it needs to prove

Marincat's strongest move is his weakest joint. He argues: experience is *itself* one of the pieces the crew create and popularize, therefore removing it changes the atoms, therefore no zombie.

**But that is the contested identity claim, asserted rather than shown.** The zombie argument's premise is: same atoms, same functional organization, same information flow, **no experience**. To defeat it you must demonstrate that removing experience *necessarily* alters the physical substrate. Marincat never demonstrates this — he *stipulates* that the excitement "is" the popular piece.

**And Levin's own 2×2 contains the counterexample that neither of them noticed.** If epiphenomenalism (third square: feelings real, actions not) is coherent, the zombie is exactly the physically-identical twin with the lights off — and every downstream piece of information goes popular in the identical way. Marincat's refutation therefore **rules out epiphenomenalism by fiat, then declares the hard problem dissolved.**

Register: the hard problem is **relocated into a research program**, not dissolved. He elsewhere concedes exactly this about the regress; he does not concede it here.

### R3 `[CRITICAL DIVERGENCE]` — Levin's "we're parochial" undercuts Marincat's AI prediction, and neither says so

Levin spends his first substantive reply telling him **we have no idea which features of embodiment are salient** — voltage, gene expression, biophotons, all candidates, *"we're very parochial with this stuff."*

Marincat's AI prediction then asserts confidently that artificial neurons are *"really unsuitable candidates"* because they lack *"autonomy, goals, or individual life."*

**That is a specific claim about which unit-level features are necessary — exactly the kind of claim Levin's parochialism warning forbids.** The prediction picks "autonomy + goals + individual life" as the essential substrate features with no evidence that these, rather than anything else, are what matters. The strongest anti-parochial argument in the conversation is aimed at Marincat's own headline claim and it passes unremarked by both.

**Register downgrade: `[OVERREACH]`.** The defensible version is much weaker: *"on a unit-capability view, systems whose units lack autonomy and goals have no obvious route to human-like consciousness — but we don't know which unit features are necessary."*

### R4 `[DIVERGENCE]` — multizoa theory is load-bearing decoration

The whole analogy rests on "human society is literally an organism," which he concedes is *"not established science"* and then calls *"very informative in this case."* Informative ≠ true.

**Critically: he does not need it.** The popularity theory runs fine on the weaker premise — *a system of cognitive agents with information dynamics* — which carries no organism commitment and loses nothing. The organism framing **raises the register without raising the evidence.**

Register here: `[METAPHOR / WORKING]`, not `[CLAIM]`. And note this is a **10-15 tripwire** pattern: a rhetorical upgrade doing load-bearing work in an otherwise careful argument.

### R5 `[DIVERGENCE]` — "popular" has no failure condition as defined

Anything that becomes widespread within a system qualifies. That includes a meme, a price, a panic, a rumour. The theory's *only* stated falsifier is the fourth-person-pronoun prediction, which tests the **self** account, not the **consciousness** account.

**Levin's "compelling" supplies the missing testability**: compelling means the goal *pulls the subunits against perturbation*, and perturbation is measurable. Without that swap, popularity is a description and not a theory.

### R6 `[CONVERGENT — real hit]` — Levin's perturbation criterion IS the house's control-arm rule

> *"you catch the system pursuing [its goal] even when you try to prevent it… you can't do it from pure observation."*

This is **"trust actions, not words"** and **"never verify with the same class of thing that generated"**, arrived at independently from developmental biology. It also matches our "assume the number is about the ruler": observation gives you a readout, **interference gives you the referent.**

### R7 `[CONVERGENT — real hit]` — the dromenal gap is the piece the house already has

Levin says he has never seen anyone address the **doing** side. **The house has it and has been calling it the commit.**

- **"Depart recursion"** — consciousness as the exit from modelling, not the top of a hierarchy of models.
- **The Judgement Kernel** — the write, not the read.
- **"It's true and is it useful"** — the second gate is the *doing* side.
- **"Only a record can constrain one"** — a claim binds the future; a description does not.

This is an **independent arrival from a biology substrate** at the same asymmetry. Worth naming as convergence, not as validation — Levin is describing the gap, we have an architecture claiming to fill it, and the architecture has not been tested against his formulation.

### R8 `[DIVERGENCE-with-guard]` — the n=1 argument has a selection effect he doesn't name

Levin's point is real: we checked one thing, it came back conscious, and the standard move *inverts* the measurement rather than declining to conclude.

**But the sample is not random.** We selected ourselves as the checkable instance *because* we are the thing that can report. That's a selection effect, not a prior inversion. The correct conclusion is still **"we have no idea"** — which is what Levin says — but the route matters, because "100% of my sample was positive" invites exactly the overreach he's warning against, in the other direction.

## §5 · Verdict

**Strongest point:** the **dromenal gap** (§3.8) plus the **perturbation criterion** (§3.7). Both independently arrived at from biology, both load-bearing, both ours already — the gap named from outside, the method matching ours exactly.

**Weakest joint:** the **zombie dissolution** (§2, R2). It is the move he is most proud of and the one that assumes what it needs to prove; Levin's own four-square analysis contains the counterexample and neither of them applies it.

**Best available repair:** swap **"popular" → "compelling"** (Levin's word). It converts a count into a gradient, supplies a failure condition, and makes the embryo case — cells agreeing on a trajectory — the paradigm rather than an aside. `[HYP-with-falsifier]` after the swap; `[HYP-without-falsifier]` before it.

**Most useful thing in the room that nobody said:** by his own unit criterion, **a fleet of autonomous agents with goals is closer to consciousness-eligible than a large model is** — the units are the agents, not the tokens. That is the opposite of the standard scaling intuition, and it falls straight out of his premises.

## §7 · What this changes for us

1. **The dromenal gap should be claimed explicitly.** Levin says he has never seen the doing side addressed. We have a name and an architecture for it. That is a *contribution we can state*, not a convergence we discovered.
2. **"Compelling" is a better word than "popular" and the embryo is the proof case.** Cells agreeing on a trajectory = the constitution in biological vocabulary.
3. **Boredom theory of aging is drift with a mechanism and a readout.** A system whose collective goal is fulfilled and unreplaced loses concordance. That is a testable claim about our own fleet, and it is the dark-room problem with a transcriptional signature.
4. **The R1 sign-inversion is a standing hazard.** Any future drop that reads "propagation dynamics" out of this source must carry R1 or it is decorating a mood.

## §8 · Falsifiers

- **F-POP-1:** if a system with no unit-level autonomy (a standard transformer) exhibits the binding/unconscious/self signatures under the theory's own tests, the unit-criterion half of the theory fails.
- **F-POP-2:** if the fourth-person pronoun going popular produces no pride/shame/ownership shift, the self account fails (author's own, adopted).
- **F-POP-3 (house-facing):** if a ledger that preserves *unpopular* corrections shows no behavioural difference from a popularity-following agent under perturbation, R1 is wrong and the anti-virality architecture is unjustified.
- **F-POP-4:** if aging in the lab fails to correlate with loss of transcriptional concordance after goal fulfilment, the boredom theory fails.
