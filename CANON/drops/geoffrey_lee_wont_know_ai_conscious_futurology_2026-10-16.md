## 2. Lee's argument structure (faithful reconstruction, tagged)

**P1. Intuition distrust is his method.** *"one of my guiding principles… is that we should be very very cautious about trusting the intuitions that we have"*; people argue from harmless-looking intuitions into panpsychism, *"leaning on an intuition that they didn't really have any right to"* [L208-L225]. Go in *"as neutrally as possible"* [L1215]. → [O] (a methodological commitment)

**P2. Strict physicalist identity, not generation.** *"if physicalism is true then the experience just is the brain state. It's not generated by the brain state"* [L604-L609]. He says even committed physicalists still think of consciousness *"in a kind of dualistic way"* [L298-L302], as an "inner light" or knife-edge [L326-L438]. Experience seen from inside is *"nothing like what it seems… just this constellation of neural activity"*; a Martian scientist looking from outside understands what experience is better than we do [L1244-L1262]. → [C] (a philosophical position; nothing in the interview could falsify it)

**P3. The NCC programme is tractable but parochial.** Finding which brain states play the consciousness role is *"in theory… a tractable project"* [L630-L636]. What it finds, though, is *"the property… in human beings that sort of locally and parochially plays the role of consciousness for us"*. Using it as the test for other systems is *"anthropocentric"* and *"the wrong picture… or at least it's potentially the wrong picture"* [L725-L745]. → [C]

**P4. The symmetry argument (alien consciousness science).** An alien species could run its own science of consciousness and find a different state. *"it's all just symmetrical… We're one kind of physical machine… there's not something particularly metaphysically magnificent about the thing in us that plays the consciousness role"* [L780-L793]. An LLM doing consciousness science on itself could conclude that we are the deluded ones [L1360-L1376]. → [A] (thought experiment; the force of the argument depends on P2)

**P5. Consciousness is graded and multi-dimensional (the cars/trucks analogy).** *"trucks are not cars, but trucks are similar to cars"* [L949-L950]. Birds (no cortex, but analogous structures that evolved separately [L895-L910]) have *"a thing that's a bit like what's happening in us, but maybe not quite the same"*. The way forward is *"different kinds of degrees of similarity and dimensions of similarity"* [L975-L985]. Some systems may have consciousness-like states that are "crude" and "boring", the way a laptop "thinks" [L1005-L1030]. → [A] for the analogy, [C] for the multi-dimensionality claim

**P6. Deflate the biological-vs-functional debate (the artificial heart).** Asking whether consciousness is biological or functional is *"a bit like asking whether a heart is a biological entity"* [L1785-L1815]. An artificial heart is a heart in the functional sense and not in the biological sense. Likewise *"we should just deflate the issue and say it's… conscious in a functional sense but not in a biological sense and that's all there is to be said"* [L1844-L1849]. → [A], with the deflationary conclusion as [C]

**P7. ★ The bind: the deflated question comes back as a moral one.** *"then it's hard because we want to know how to treat it… is it the biological sense that matters for whether it feels pain or is it the functional sense?… we're in kind of in a bind because I suspect that there's just no fact the matter"* [L1850-L1862]. *"it's unclear whether suffering is a biological phenomenon or a functional phenomenon"* [L1871-L1874]. Our moral and political "social technology" was *"designed just for human beings"* and never anticipated this case [L1900-L1925]. → [C] **(the load-bearing claim)**

**P8. Detection is a red herring (artificial wine).** Asked whether anything besides self-report can settle it, he answers: *"I don't think we should get too hung up on like determining whether it's conscious. And in a way, I think that's a red herring"* [L1935-L1940]. Then the analogy: a physicalist church asking whether *artificial* wine is *really* wine for the sacrament. Biochemical analysis would teach us a lot about wine, *"but there isn't… some deep question about whether it's really wine"* [L1960-L1990]. The right stance is pragmatic: *"even if it's not wine, maybe we can treat it as wine… our old religious norms just never envisioned [this]… so we just have to… invent a new practice"* [L1995-L2045]. Result: *"a mismatch between what we're invested in and what reality affords us"* [L2051-L2053]. → [A] **(his signature analogy)**

**P9. The positive basis he offers in place of consciousness.** There are three strands, and he does not merge them:
- (a) [O] **Similarity:** *"as a sort of progressive thinking person, I tend towards… making the circle bigger based on similarity"* [L2212-L2216]. The ideal case adds matching *"in terms of computational complexity… the sort of richness of the functioning"* [L1040-L1046]. Something with a human-like body and brain-like function should *obviously* be treated like us, *"assuming that we're not obsessed with humanness per se"* [L2312-L2320].
- (b) [O] **Permissive, not mandatory:** expanding the circle is *"an open possibility… one reasonable way to continue"*, but there may be reasons not to, e.g. if AI is used by people in power *"to surveil us… then I don't think the question of whether they're conscious is going to be the important question"* [L2232-L2260]. Politics and history can outrank consciousness.
- (c) [O] **Leap of faith, not derivation:** *"we can't figure out whether to care about them by doing… rationalistic moral philosophy… it's going to be more like a leap of faith… a new way for humanity to exist that we have to sort of feel our way into"* [L2330-L2340]; also [L2094].
- He also **rejects the populist basis.** The interviewer suggests "if 50%+1 think it's conscious, that's all that matters", and Lee answers *"people are easily fooled… robots that look cute and elicit empathy… a very superficial reaction"* [L2287-L2300]. → [O]

**P10. His personal position.** *"if I just [was] had all the political power, then I might choose just to stop this technology from evolving… it's not obvious that it's good for us"* [L2353-L2357]. Then *"I'm a mess with this"* [L2363], and *"part of me is like we should just boldly go forth"* [L2387]. He predicts sectarian splits (mainstream adopters vs. anti-AI communities in the woods), possibly *"wars between Catholics and Protestants"*-style violence [L2100-L2117], and is worried about a godlike surveillance AI [L2140-L2155]. → [O] / [C] (predictions with no dates or thresholds)

## 6. Dion's verdict (opinionated)

**Strongest point:** the **epistemic/metaphysical split** in §1 [L1749-L1752], *"not in the sense that we can't tell, but… there's just not a clear fact of the matter."* No other source filed this week makes it. Block, Berg, Seth and the *AM I?* film all assume there is a fact and argue about the evidence or the burden. **Weakest joint:** **his alternative criterion brings back the anthropocentrism he used to demolish the old one.** P3 says using the human realiser as the test is "anthropocentric" and "parochial". Then P9 sets moral status by *similarity to us*: computational richness "matching us", the human-like body and brain as the obvious case, the Neanderthal as the paradigm. That is the same human reference point, moved from "has our NCC" to "resembles us enough". The alien in his own P4 thought experiment would put *its* similarity metric at the centre, and by his own symmetry argument it would be no more wrong. So the "leap of faith" is not a conclusion of the argument. It is what is left once the argument has removed every basis for deciding, and he concedes as much ("I'm a mess with this" [L2363]; his personal instinct is to stop the technology). **Second, smaller joint:** the "no fact" claim covers *consciousness-as-category*, but **pain is where he flinches.** He grants it "feels kind of urgent" [L1855-L1858] and then just declares suffering indeterminate [L1871], without the argument the heart case gets. If there's no fact about whether a system suffers, the precautionary arguments (Berg, Sebo/Long, whom Block defers to) are not dissolved. They become decision-under-vagueness problems, which philosophy *does* have tools for (supervaluation, moral-uncertainty weighting).
