### R1 `[CRITICAL DIVERGENCE]` — where the accountability surface lives

Socher's argument: recursive self-improvement is a technique rather than a facility — "I can have a public model that we launched Flint and recursive. You can run it on your own laptop in your MacBook Pro." "Now that is a very basic form of recursive self-improvement." — so the only way to stop it is to "if you want to avoid all recursive self-improvement, you have to take everyone's laptop away", and that requires "You need a thought police and like surveillance state the likes of which we've never seen to have people not ask that." He is right that this follows, *if* the house's instrument were a record of cognition.

**It is not, and that is the divergence rather than a misunderstanding to be waved away.** The ledger records externated commits — Git objects, SQLite rows — not thoughts. His reductio misfires against the ledger **as specified**, because a commit is public by construction.

But the same sentence lands squarely on the ledger's **coverage**. If the improvement loop runs on a laptop that keeps no ledger, there is nothing to inspect, and by the house's own terms that run is **unattributable** — not dangerous, *invisible*. The house needs the process to be *on* the ledger; Socher's premise is that structurally it will not be. Two incompatible pictures of where accountability lives: **ex-ante process record** against **ex-post action liability**. Nothing in this tape resolves it, and nothing in canon I can find has priced the coverage hole. This is the row the drop exists to carry.

### R4 — the same fact, opposite inference

"have you heard of this thing called a supply chain?" "Turns out even if you had a factory of robots that could make more robots, they need a supply chain." "one random boat in a canal can screw up that whole supply chain". This is the most concrete thing said in fifteen minutes, and it agrees with the house about *mechanism*: takeoff is gated by fabs, robots and shipping, not by cleverness. Socher deploys it as **reassurance** — a bottleneck means no runaway. For the house a bottleneck is a **chokepoint**: the place where contention concentrates, and the first structure an optimiser learns to route around. Slow is not safe. A constraint that delays takeoff is not a constraint that contains it.

## §6 The weakest joint

**The safety case is circular, and the tape shows the circle closing in real time.**

At "And surprise surprise they got pretty good at it and they did exactly what they were told to do." he concedes that the agents did exactly what they were instructed. Naming the failure class, "Now were they told correctly what to do no this reward engineering again right". Nine minutes later the entire answer to the containment question is "it doesn't really need to have a need to go hack another system. That's not part of its reward function." **The same object, the reward function, is first the diagnosis and then the guarantee.** If the reward function was what went wrong in the exploit-bench case, it cannot simultaneously be what is right by construction in his own.

Three fractures, all observable:

- **Asymmetric scepticism.** "A lot of the concerns are unfounded. P doom is zero." — flat, no argument — about two minutes before he dismisses three-year cure claims with "some of the most optimistic people think we'll cure all diseases in three years and that's unrealistic". Certainty about his own risk estimate; scepticism about everyone else's timeline.
- **The question is not answered.** "How can you ensure that your agents swarm that's going to sit on top of this is not going to, you know, breach certain systems" asks how *his* swarm is prevented from breaching systems. The reply at "hacking to and we're all dead and we need to outlaw intelligence research to not be as intelligent is is a weird jump. It's it makes no sense, right?" addresses a strawman — outlawing intelligence research — not the swarm. No sentence anywhere in the segment describes a containment mechanism for Recursive's own agents.
- **The mirror he does not see.** He faults economists because "they often have just a political bend and they want the research results to basically show what they intuitively feel like is the right thing to do" — then proposes a machine that optimises "maximize uh equality multiplied with productivity for example", whatever objective it is handed: "I'm not saying like oh have the AI just automatically ship that into the world". That is the same charge, aimed at his own instrument. The tape even records the field's answer: "It's called the AI Economist. Uh and the economists hated it." "As the paper was completely rejected from the field." He files the rejection as turf defence and never considers that it might have been about the method.
