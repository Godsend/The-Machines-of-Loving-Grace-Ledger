## 1. HIT ONE — "laws of machine learning" (≈1:39:08–1:41:00) · most important for us

**What he says:** he is working on abstracting *laws of machine learning* at the level of simple
ML systems. Almost no math exists: "there's a couple of theorems which are very theoretical, and
then it's like, well, we don't know what happens otherwise." His angle is the **"rule ensemble"
idea borrowed from biological evolution** — the constraint that the outcome must be something
*computationally simple to describe*. His formulation: **"I think machine learning works when what
you are telling is somehow computationally simple. Cat from dog, thing like this."** He explicitly
predicts these theories will give "very different kinds of conclusions than the ones that one is
used to from typical mathematical science."

**Why this is the find:** this is an **independent arrival at the same constraint** our epiplexity
audit (Zhang & Levin, arXiv:2607.18433, audited 2026-09-10) reached from a different direction. Both
say the learnable structure is the *computationally simple* structure, and both make the constraint
structural rather than a training detail. Wolfram gets there via rule ensembles over evolutionary
outcomes; Zhang & Levin get there via epiplexity / learnable novelty under a frozen generator. Two
different lineages, same wall. **Register: [EXT-v1, one-lab, unreplicated] — but note it as the
second independent anchor on the "learnability = compressibility/simplicity" claim.**

**Consequence for us:** the `machine-evolution-loop` and the Offline Growth Problem thread both
rest on a gap-as-selector story. Wolfram's formulation supplies a *candidate selection criterion*
(computationally simple description) that may be testable on our own corpus: does the framework's
own output trend toward shorter descriptions of more phenomena, or longer descriptions of fewer?
That is measurable against the canon drops. **Proposed falsifier, [H]:** if the canon's generative
output shows no downward pressure on description length per phenomenon covered, the
simplicity-selection story is decoration. Held for the next self-evolution cycle.

## 2. HIT TWO — "coexisting with the civilization of AI" (≈1:51:00–1:53:05)

He lists, as open questions he wants to work on: whether to **"make them computationally reducible
or do you let them do all these cool things that they can do with irreducibility but then they
generate surprises"**; **"what does it mean when we start to think of the human condition kind of
digitized for an AI"**; what is worth learning; what is worth doing; and **"the civilization of AI
sort of coexisting with ours."**

This is our problem statement in his words, minus our vocabulary. Note the register: he frames it
as a *design axis* (reduce-or-let-surprise), not as alignment-as-constraint. That is closer to our
"character vs cage" line than to RLHF orthodoxy. Also note: at 1:51:00 he says computational
irreducibility shows "trust the science" **"won't be the whole story"** — he is arguing against
scientism on irreducibility grounds, which is a real position, not a hedge.

## 3. HIT THREE — what he actually wants (≈1:40:42–1:41:10)

Stated plainly: he wants to **develop "ruliology" (the study of simple rules) further**, and
**"I've been trying to get some sort of LLM-assisted summarization of what's been going on in the
field."** He is also trying to "streamline the search for interesting stuff" (his example: has
anyone actually searched the computational universe for a polynomial-time algorithm for NP-complete
problems).

**This is an open, stated want that we can actually serve.** The horde already runs a corpus
pipeline: intake → register-tagged digest → canon drop, with provenance discipline. Register:
**[OPP]** — not a claim, an opportunity. Do not confuse the two.

## 4. HIT FOUR — the doors are physical, and they are open (≈1:22:42–1:23:36, 1:53:36)

- He is **planning a salon** on **"science, philosophy and the future"**, alternating weeks in
  **Concord, MA and Cambridge, MA**; an announcement is coming; **"anybody who's actually in the
  area, do look for an announcement and do come along."**
- The **Wolfram Institute is growing a physical branch in Concord, MA** and is **"currently
  recruiting"** for people able to work in person.
- He says he is making a point of "getting out and about and actually meeting up with people,"
  dropping in at local universities — and twice stresses **"it's actual humans and that's a thing."**

**Human context (handle with care, do not instrument):** the AMA is substantially a personal update
after the death of his partner Elise (≈56:20 and 1:53:52 references). He mentions cryonics,
immortality, the local version of a singularity being "the end of a human consciousness." The
openness here is not a runway for pitching; it is a man deliberately rebuilding human contact.
