## §2 Spekkens's actual core claims, as stated in this video

**2.1 The Leibnizian methodological principle = "ontological identity of empirical indiscernibles" [HYP — his stated programme; his own term].** *"I like to say it's the ontological identity of empirical indiscernibles"* `[QUOTE, L106]` (0:12:47). Content: if two scenarios a theory calls ontologically distinct make **identical** empirical predictions, **reject the theory**. His textbook case, in his words: Einstein eliminating the ether (*"the current's exactly the same in the two situations"*, L126) and the equivalence principle (*"any experiment you do looks exactly the same"*, L136). He is explicit that it is **not testable**: *"it['s] not a principle that you can go and test. It['s] rather a principle that constrains your kind of realist theories"* `[QUOTE, L156]` (0:19:03).

**2.2 Two constraints, and they are in tension [HYP — his framing].** (i) Leibniz; (ii) *"correlations should have causal explanations"* `[QUOTE, L196]` (0:23:53) — being told two variables are correlated but not *why* is, to him, *"a kind of anti-[explanation]"*. And: *"to understand what's really going on, we need a causal account"* `[QUOTE, L208]` (0:25:38). **[EST]** He flags the tension himself: Leibniz ⟹ local causality ⟹ contradiction with the quantum predictions.

**2.3 Superluminal influence is a Leibniz violation [HYP — his argument].** Given no-signalling, a "superluminal influence" is an ontological difference with **zero** empirical signature: *"at the empirical observed phenomena scale, there's no evidence of any influence … But to imagine that there's nonetheless an influence, well, that's violating Leibniz"* `[QUOTE, L389–390]` (0:46:5x). **[EST]** This is the exact form of his local-causality claim and the core of his case.

**2.4 Influence ≠ signalling [EST — his cleanest formal point].** Via the Vernam cipher / one-time pad: the plaintext **causally influences** the ciphertext (else decryption would be impossible) while **no** variation of the plaintext changes the ciphertext's distribution. *"there's no signal from the plain text to the ciphertext … But of course there['s] a causal influence … So signaling is stronger than influence"* `[QUOTE, L472–476]` (0:58:18–0:58:45). He then says hidden-variable models that reproduce Bell violations with superluminal influence use **exactly this trick** — *"they sort of wash out"* the influence with local noise `[QUOTE, L479–481]` — and that this is precisely why they are non-Leibnizian. **He reuses the structure as a weapon, not as a licence.**

**2.5 Causal influence is asymmetric; inference is symmetric [EST — his own claim].** *"influence, causal influence, is asymmetric"* `[QUOTE, L449]` (0:55:31). Cannonball / rooster examples. He refuses the interventionist definition: *"I don't subscribe to the view that we need agents and the notion of intervention to define causation … interventions are a great way of getting a hold of what['s] going on causally but I don't think it['s] part of the definition of causation"* `[QUOTE, L496–497]` (1:01–1:03). **Causation is a primitive of physical description** `[QUOTE, L518]` (1:03:59), and he rejects the "no causation in physics" line (`[QUOTE, L519–525]`) that Curt attributes to **Emily Adlam**.

**2.6 The arrow of time is in causation, not in the kinematics [HYP — his claim].** Time-symmetry arguments are *"really being arguments for time symmetry of inference"*: *"the theory of inference doesn't care whether you're making inferences from the past to the future or the future of the past. but I think causation does care and because I think it's a primitive notion I think there is an arrow of time in our physical theories"* `[QUOTE, L604–607]` (1:14:09–1:14:32). Explicitly forward: *"it makes sense to imagine causes always proceeding from past to future not in the other direction So I don't see any reason to depart from that"* `[QUOTE, L516–517]` (1:03:46).

**2.7 The anti-mystification move, and it targets retrocausation by name [EST].** *"I don't see any reason why we should go in for backward-in-time influences or any kind of exotic notions of causation"* `[QUOTE, L526–527]` (1:04:55). And the *reason* the exotic options arise: *"when you try to understand the quantum state as being something real, you often find that you have to go in for some idea like, oh, consciousness collapses the wave function and learning about something late influences the past. You know, like in a Wheeler's Delayed Choice Experiment … And it's only when you properly formalize what it would be for a quantum state to represent mere ignorance that these things become very conventional. If I learn something in the future, I might update my knowledge about the past. That's not exotic or new-agey in any way. It doesn't involve consciousness."* `[QUOTE, L579–583]` (1:11:18–1:11:48). His own illustration: *"I find some fossils in the ground and I infer something about dinosaurs in the past. I haven't caused dinosaurs to exist. I've updated my information about dinosaurs."* `[QUOTE, L584]`.

**2.8 Thermodynamics is his precedent for doing physics with agents [HYP — his argument].** The second law came from **pragmatic** questions: *"what are the fundamental limits on what I can achieve"* (L563, 1:09:20); *"limitations on how much work I can extract from some heat path[s] tells me something about the laws of physics … even though it['s] about these sort of parochial concerns … it can still tell me something really important about the laws of physics"* `[QUOTE, L566–569]` (1:09:39 ff.). Then Boltzmann: *"we don't have knowledge of the microscopic configuration … we're going to imagine a probability distribution … representing our ignorance"* `[QUOTE, L570–572]` (1:10:07). He is explicit that this is **precedent, not novelty**: *"it's not as if … talking about quantum states as states of knowledge is something highly innovative. It's been there in stat-mech forever."* `[QUOTE, L574–576]`.

**2.9 What ψ is epistemic *about* is unanswered, and he says so [EST — his own admission].** *"that's a good question. That's the right question to be asking. And unfortunately, the situation is that this is a research program, and I would say we don't have a good answer to that question."* `[QUOTE, L1113]` (2:17:51). And the closing image: *"we're not there yet, so we need to find our Coptic."* `[QUOTE, L1190]` (2:27:22).

**2.10 He is NOT a proponent of ψ-epistemic ontological models, and he rejects the frame they live in [EST — decisive, and it is the point most likely to be misread].** *"the first thing I say in response to this is I kind of don't care because I am not a proponent of psi epistemic ontological models. I want to get rid of the ontological models framework."* `[QUOTE, L1011–1012]` (2:05:01). And on the toy theory: *"I['m] not proposing this as the correct interpretation of quantum mechanics … the correct interpretation of quantum theory is not going to be this sort of thing. In fact, I think that we have to abandon this framework"* `[QUOTE, L928–930]` (1:54:10 ff.); *"I want to reject the framework of ontological models altogether"* `[QUOTE, L939]` (1:55:51). **He studies the toy theory as a foil (his term: *"the methodology of foil theory"*, L312).** He names the misreading as his own top frustration (*"people think I just endorse this framework … and that's not what I want"*, L938–939).

**2.11 The PBR rebuttal, in three moves [EST — his claims].** (a) *"a theorem that says you can't have that. I already knew that. Bell's theorem already tells me I can't have my Leibnizian principle even if I take psi to be epistemic"* `[QUOTE, L1012–1013]`. (b) The popular assumption is **ontic separability** (composite ontic space = Cartesian product of component ontic spaces ⟹ *"a commitment to there being no holistic properties"*, L1025) plus **independence preservation** (product preparations ⟹ factorizing distributions, L1027–1030); he notes independence preservation *"actually follows from Leibnizian principles"* `[QUOTE, L1032–1033]`. But with those assumptions the theorem **kills ψ-ontic too**: *"it also rules out psiontic models … ontic separability is the assumption, then here's the easy no-go theorem. You can't have a sci-ontic ontological model. That's trivial. It just falls [out] from the existence of entangled state."* `[QUOTE, L1041, L1047–1048]` (2:08:49 ff.). Hence: *"the natural assumptions, if you make them, rules out both psi-epistemic and psionic models"* `[QUOTE, L1077]` (2:13:26) — his **"rehabilitated PBR theorem"** `[QUOTE, L1050]` (2:10:00), i.e. PBR as another Bell/Kochen–Specker rather than as an argument for ψ-ontology. (c) What PBR actually needs is the narrower **"entanglement-holism link"** `[QUOTE, L1059]` (2:11:02) — holistic properties allowed in the *support of entangled* representations but disallowed for *product* states — which he argues is *"only plausible if you already believe that the quantum state is a state of reality"*, making the argument *"kind of circular"* `[QUOTE, L1070–1075]`, and he issues the challenge: *"please provide a motivation for this entanglement-holism link that doesn't just rely on your sci-ontic intuitions"* `[QUOTE, L1071–1073]` (2:12:34).

**2.12 His answer to Leifer's 2014 exponential result is a *motivation* challenge, not a rebuttal [HYP — his move; see §5 of the Friction table].** Curt raises it: *"in 2014 or so, Leifer … these psi-epistemic models are exponentially not good at explaining distinguishability"* `[QUOTE, L1079–1080]` (2:13:42). Spekkens's reply: again an assumption — **maximal ψ** (*"when two distributions are indistinguishable it should be entirely explained by the magnitude of their overlap"*, `[QUOTE, L1086]`, 2:14:29) — which he says *"you can derive … from the Leibnizian principle. It's an instance of non-contextuality"*; therefore *"the game becomes, can you give me any reason for believing this assumption about how to discriminate states that isn't just an appeal to Leibniz?"* `[QUOTE, L1088–1091]`. **Note for the ledger: he does not contest the exponential lemma; he contests whether the premise is an *assumption* at all.** Marked `[HYP]` and `[ATTACK SURFACE]` below.

**2.13 Foil-theory methodology, and his explicit standard for "no classical explanation" [EST — his claim, and a usable house standard].** *"the problem with what Feynman said was that he didn't prove a no-go theorem. He didn't say this is what I mean by a classical explanation … Here are the predictions of quantum theory. Here['s] the contradiction."* `[QUOTE, L401–403]` (0:49:24 ff.). *"I can have a classical model, reproduces the phenomenology"* `[QUOTE, L401]`. He also rejects logical-weakness-as-plausibility: *"suppose assumption X is, you know, Newton's laws hold. And assumption Y is Newton's laws hold on Tuesdays … that's logically weaker … It's not physically more plausible"* `[QUOTE, L1098–1100]`.

**2.14 The hieroglyph analogy [A — his own analogy].** Prevailing error = glyphs are **ideograms** (concept directly); the truth = **phonetic**, and the key was Champollion's **Coptic**. Mapping onto QM: cartouches (pharaohs' names read phonetically while everything else was read ideographically) ↔ the **Clifford sub-theories** that *do* admit local, non-contextual (i.e. Leibnizian) models (L962–L967); *"the phonetic versus ideographic is kind of like the psi-epistemic versus psi-ontic"* `[QUOTE, L1178]`. **Coptic = the missing formalism for causation/inference.**
