## 5. THE GÖDEL CHECK

**The standard that applies [VERIFIED — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems", rev. Oct 2025]:** the theorems concern *derivability in a particular formal system*. The SEP entry states it directly: *"A common misunderstanding is to interpret Gödel’s first theorem as showing that there are truths that cannot be proved."* — *"the incompleteness theorem does not deal with provability in any absolute sense, but only concerns derivability in some particular formal system or another."* It also states *"the incompleteness theorems do not essentially presuppose classical logic, but also apply to systems with, e.g., intuitionistic logic"*, and on the mind argument: *"These Gödelian anti-mechanist arguments are, however, problematic, and there is wide consensus that they fail."* — because *"the alleged truth of the Gödel sentence of a system depends on the assumption of the consistency of the system"*. On Gödel himself: *"he did not maintain that the incompleteness theorems alone establish Platonism"*.

**Verdict: YES — both speakers use incompleteness to argue FOR substantive conclusions, in opposite directions. Each also has a moment of using it correctly as a boundary.** [as of 2022-02]

## 6. THE PREDICTED FLASHPOINT — verdict: **NOT CONFIRMED as predicted; a displaced version holds**

**Prediction:** both agree a system's self-model is *necessarily* incomplete, then split (Hoffman → non-spatial reality behind the interface; Bach → ordinary self-reference in a computational system).
