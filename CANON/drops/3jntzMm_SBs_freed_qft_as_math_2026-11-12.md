## §2 — The actual argument, in the order it is made

1. **Research is confusion-management, and that is trainable.** *"If you're not confused, you're not working."* `[00:00:00]`; the pedagogical consequence is giving *"more problems than I know they can solve"* `[00:02:49]` and open-ended problems, because *"it's a psychological training as much as a mathematical training"* `[00:02:21]`. `[A]`
2. **The three-word problem.** The host recalls Freed's exchange with **Nati Seiberg**: a real number is a *"complete ordered field"* `[00:22:00]` — a hard-won three-word characterisation that does not reflect how we *use* real numbers. Freed's point: *"For a quantum field theory, there's not a unique quantum field theory, but we want a definition or an axiom system that captures the essence of it, but doesn't tell you how to construct a particular one"* `[00:23:42]`. **There is no QFT analogue of "complete ordered field", and its absence is not a gap in effort — it is a gap in kind.** `[A]`
3. **Why QFT resisted axiomatisation when QM did not.** QM and its mathematics (von Neumann; Hilbert spaces, operators, operator algebras) *"fed off of each other"* `[00:24:21]`. QFT was *"much more mysterious from the beginning"* `[00:24:32]`. The 1950s–60s produced two axiom systems for **relativistic QFT on Minkowski space** — **Wightman** and **Haag–Kastler** — which are *"very powerful and still today are very, very useful"* but *"don't capture all aspects of quantum field theory, and they don't capture all quantum field theories"* `[00:25:19]`. `[EST]`
4. **The elephant.** The 1980s brought geometric examples (partly from string theory) and QFT studied *in other dimensions*, so that *"quantum field theory appears like the proverbial elephant, where you see parts of it. You see the trunk, you see the tail, you see different parts of it, but you never see the whole elephant… Different people see different parts of it. And so we lack that overall view"* `[00:26:18]–[00:26:28]`. `[POETIC]` — **an image, not an argument**: he never treats "the elephant" as a technical object, and nothing in the episode depends on it. Tagged `[POETIC]` deliberately, to keep the house from later citing it as a structural claim.
5. **Segal's axioms as the partial repair, and what they bought.** **Graeme Segal**'s late-1980s axiom system for **Wick-rotated** theory (time → imaginary time), introduced *"in the context of two-dimensional conformal field theories"* `[00:27:03]` and then recognised as more general. Its payoff is stated concretely: resonances in **low-dimensional topology**, **symplectic geometry**, parts of **algebraic geometry**, various **algebras**, and more recently **number theory** `[00:27:57]–[00:28:20]`. `[EST]` for the mathematics; `[A]` for the assessment of payoff.
6. **Projectivity — the load-bearing claim.** Classical state space is a curved manifold; quantum mechanics replaces it with something linear *because you have the superposition principle*, but the **pure states are not vectors**: *"they're actually collections of vectors that are related by a scalar multiple"* `[00:31:47]`. Hence *"the states in a quantum system form a projective space"* `[00:32:23]`, and — the sentence the drop exists for — *"there's a really well-defined sense in which quantum mechanics is projective geometry, not linear geometry"* `[00:32:44]`. Linearity is needed only when you **quantize** (to sum in a path integral), so *"we have to take the projective system and linearize it"* `[00:33:31]`. `[EST]` (standard mathematics, stated accurately)
7. **An anomaly is the obstruction to that linearisation — i.e. the thing that *is* the physics.** *"the kind of cohomology, if you like, cocycle that measures this obstruction to linearizing… is what's called an invertible field theory… And that's what's called an anomaly in physics. That's the thing you have to trivialize in order to linearize this projective system"* `[00:34:01]–[00:34:27]`. Historically, *"in the '80s, an anomaly was thought to be just something you compute, a kind of feature or really a bug of a system"* `[00:34:41]` — and the later understanding is that **the anomaly itself is a field theory**, with QFT's locality properties. `[A]` for the historical claim; `[EST]` for the mathematics of invertible field theories.
8. **The slogan, and its scope condition.** *"quantum theory is projective, quantization is linear"* `[00:49:37]`. Then the distinction that keeps the slogan from being a platitude: once the theory is **finished**, *"that's where the anomaly is a feature"*; but *"if we're in the middle of constructing the theory, then the anomaly is a bug"* — and that is where you cancel it (Green–Schwarz) `[00:49:54]–[00:50:44]`. **So "feature not bug" is not a global slogan; it is indexed to whether you are done constructing.** `[A]`
9. **Anomaly matching as a computational tool.** 't Hooft's move, in Freed's words: a projective system at short distance and a projective system at long distance, *"but the anomaly or the projectivity has to match"* `[00:47:47]`; embedding in more parameters makes the invariant richer and the matching more constrained — *"that's one example of how that's really used as a feature in studying a physical system and not as a bug"* `[00:48:31]`. `[A]`
10. **Locality and unitarity are the two bedrock principles, and they are not symmetric.** *"locality is built into the domain, this bordism category. But unitarity is something you would ask of a representation"* `[00:56:53]` — i.e. optional in the formalism. Extended locality (cutting below codimension one) is where unitarity becomes hard; with **Mike Hopkins**, the pair needed it to fix a classification of invertible phases: *"we got wrong answers before we incorporated unitarity"* `[01:00:29]`. `[A]`/`[EST]`
11. **The Standard Model does not satisfy the Segal axioms, and nobody can currently make it.** Even a free field theory is *"there surely it's possible, and surely someone could do it"* but *"I don't think anyone's ever done that"* `[01:06:01]`; for interacting theories, being able to formulate pure Yang–Mills on all Riemannian manifolds *"means you would have constructed it, and you would be able to calculate whether there's a mass gap"* `[01:06:55]`. **So the Yang–Mills mass gap is entangled with the axiomatisation problem, not separate from it.** `[A]`
12. **Quantization is not a functor, and the failure is informative.** Planck's constant becomes a parameter, and *"that's again an oxymoron… because it's a constant"* `[01:29:39]`; in Chern–Simons it is roughly the reciprocal of an integer, *"you can't take a continuous limit"* `[01:30:17]`; in abelian Chern–Simons there is a good classical→quantum map but *"it's infinite to one"* `[01:31:44]`. Conclusion: *"these quantum systems probably should be studied as quantum systems, and then specializations become these classical systems"* `[01:32:10]`. `[A]`
13. **Dimension four is a genuine singularity in the classification of smooth structures, and Freed explicitly refuses to give it physical significance.** In dimensions 1–3 a topological manifold has *"essentially a unique smooth structure"*; in ≥5 compact manifolds have finitely many; **in dimension four a compact manifold can have countably infinitely many, and only there** `[01:19:53]–[01:20:34]`. On ℝ⁴: *"in every dimension except for four, there's a unique structure"* `[01:20:54]`. Asked whether this bears on 3+1 spacetime: *"I don't know of any particular cogent argument about why those should say something in physics"* `[01:22:28]`; he offers only the conditional *"you can imagine in quantum gravity theories that you should sum over smooth structures"* `[01:22:37]`. `[EST]` for the topology; `[C]`-grade refusal for the physics.
14. **The Swansea story, told as second-hand.** See §7 — this is the drop's most quotable anecdote and its **provability is the weakest**, because Freed says on tape that he was not there.
15. **The AI-mathematics shock, in his own ordering.** Unit-distance/Erdős in **May**; the **Jacobian conjecture** disproved; **complex structure on S⁶**; **10 problems at the beginning of August**; **Navier–Stokes at the beginning of September**; then *"last week… about 475 problems or 475 papers, all the papers written by AI"* `[00:11:36]–[00:12:47]`. The framing he insists on is not the count but the **verification debt**: *"it's going to take a long time for the community to go through to verify… these proofs and, more importantly, to see what new understandings and what further developments can come out of them"* `[00:13:13]`. `[C]` for the enumeration, `[A]` for the debt.
16. **Two mechanisms of AI mathematical success** — the cleanest analytic content in the episode. (i) *"it constructs examples and counterexamples by pushing an idea that probably was in the literature… but people didn't pursue it to the extent that an AI can do it"* `[00:09:12]`; (ii) *"it can tie together mathematics in some area A and some area B where there aren't experts that know both in detail, but the AI of course does, and it can solve a problem in area A by… leveraging techniques in area B"* `[00:09:45]`. And the deflation he adds himself: *"tells you all the human edifice on which these AIs are building to do these mathematics problems"* `[01:18:58]`. `[A]`
17. **Mourning, precisely located.** Not "math is over": *"a lot of my working identity is tied to sort of having year-long projects, decade-long projects sometimes… I think that's gonna go away. So there's something about that that I think I'm definitely grieving"* `[01:17:16]–[01:17:31]`. And the asymmetry: *"if you're a young person… what do you have to mourn?"* `[00:18:30]`. `[A]`
18. **Singer, and the freedom to err.** Freed's advisor **Isadore Singer** *"was very prescient"* `[00:37:40]` about mathematicians engaging QFT directly; the working relationship was *"mostly on the side of tennis courts"* `[01:46:24]`; and *"if you don't have the freedom to err, then you can't make progress"* `[01:48:42]`. The illustrative anecdote is Atiyah's *"Why is the A-hat genus of a spin manifold an integer?"* at Oxford, ~1962 — the question everyone could answer and nobody had asked *why* — which Freed presents as the germ of the Dirac-operator route into the index theorem `[01:50:08]–[01:51:38]`. `[A]`; the anecdote is **Singer's story, retold by Freed** (double relay — §7).

### ★★ R1 — [CRITICAL DIVERGENCE] — the ledger is a linearisation, and the source says the linearisation is a choice with an obstruction

This is the row the drop exists to carry, and the divergence is **structural, not verbal**.

The house's central artifact is **linear and additive**: commits are appended, the state is a growing sequence, and the invariant the ledger protects (non-rollback) is a property of that sequence. The whole architecture presumes that **the thing being recorded is a point in a linear space** and that accumulation is well-defined — you add a record, the total changes, and nothing is lost so long as you never delete.

Freed's claim is that this is **false at the level where it matters**. Pure quantum states are not vectors; *"they're actually collections of vectors that are related by a scalar multiple"* `[00:31:47]`, so the state space is **projective**, and *"quantum mechanics is projective geometry, not linear geometry"* `[00:32:44]`. Linearity is then a **tool used in construction** (you need sums to write a path integral), which is why *"we have to take the projective system and linearize it"* `[00:33:31]` — and the linearisation **does not always exist**. When it fails, the failure is measured by a cocycle, and *"that's what's called an anomaly in physics. That's the thing you have to trivialize in order to linearize this projective system"* `[00:34:27]`.

**The friction, stated plainly:** the ledger's fidelity claim is contingent on a **linearisation that Freed says is non-canonical**. Two different trivialisations of the same projective system give two different linear records of the *same* state, and nothing in the record marks which trivialisation was used. So "the ledger records what happened" is true only up to a choice the ledger cannot express — and the anomaly, the term that carries the difference, is precisely what a linear commit log has no slot for, because it is an **obstruction to having a well-defined state at all** rather than a fact about one. A house reader who takes projectivity as *support* for the ledger has inverted it: Freed's result is the strongest available argument that a linear append-only record **cannot be a complete representation** of a projective process, and that the missing information is not "unrecorded" but **unrepresentable in that form**.

**Why this matters for the house specifically.** It is the same shape as the house's own F22 correction (§18.8): the ledger certifies origin, never validity; it supplies an **unmodified** target, and unmodified ≠ correct. R1 sharpens that into a *representational* limit rather than an *evaluative* one: even a perfect, never-rolled-back record of every step is a record **in the wrong category** for a state that lives in projective space. **The honest house response is not to deny the mathematics but to say what the ledger is for** — a record of *commits made by an agent*, not a reconstruction of *the physical state* — and this drop does not close that gap. **The tape does not map here; it runs against the ledger's implicit carrier.**

### R3 — [DIVERGENCE] — an anomaly is a symmetry that *fails to survive*; the ledger's invariance is a record that *cannot be rewritten*

The house's invariance claim is **non-rollback**: what was committed stays committed, and the protected quantity is the record's integrity across time.
