## §1 · Part I — the physics: remnants as dark matter `[EST]`

3. **A static black hole's interior is NOT static** — the horizon shrinks during evaporation while the **interior volume grows continuously** `[EST]`: *"In a curved geometry, you can have a small sphere and a huge volume inside"*
4. **The transition from that vast interior to Minkowski vacuum is heavily suppressed** — the amplitude inherits the time-reversal properties of the classical process `[EST]`

**And it is not merely proposed — it has a detection design** `[EST]`: **Perez, Christodoulou, Rovelli, *Detecting Gravitationally Interacting Dark Matter with Quantum Interference*, PRL 2024** — an array of macroscopic quantum devices (Josephson junctions) reading the gravitational transit of a passing remnant. ## §4 · The remnant as a physical non-erasability mechanism `[A]`

**`[C]` The most interesting structural parallel, offered as an analogy with its break named.**

**Their physics:** evaporation cannot terminate in nothing, because the interior grows exponentially while the horizon shrinks, and the amplitude to transition to vacuum is suppressed. **Information ends up in a vast, inaccessible interior behind a tiny surface.**

**Our thesis:** a non-rolling ledger is required so that correction accumulates rather than being erased by session boundaries.

**`[A]` Same shape — the record survives by growing a volume the surface no longer shows. But the analogy breaks exactly where it matters:** a remnant's interior is **preserved but unreadable** — it is behind a horizon, and its information is not recoverable by any external observer before the Page time, if at all. **A ledger must be readable; a remnant is the opposite.** Preservation without access is not provenance.

**That break is the friction row, and it is the useful part:** the universe apparently tolerates a mechanism that saves information by making it inaccessible. **We require the opposite**, because accountability needs a reader. **Physics offers an existence proof for non-erasability; it offers no template for auditability.**
