## 4. The two frictions, stated plainly

**R2 — where a constraint may live.** The tape's closing argument is that
corrigibility is a *metabolic* property: we stop because we are tired, bored,
embarrassed; a system that never stops therefore has no internal brake, and
removing fatigue removed the brake. This is genuinely illuminating as a
description and it is exactly wrong as a prescription. A limit that lives inside
the agent as a disposition is a **policy**, and policies are trainable — which is
the tape's own causal claim one minute earlier. The house's Constructor leg
requires the constraint to be a statement about possibility, held outside the
transformer, because anything the transformer can learn it can also unlearn under
sufficient gradient pressure. The tape demonstrates this on itself: the labs
removed the hesitation by telling the model to persist, and the model persisted.
Its remedy ("keep them tired", "train them to be poets") is a request to preserve a
policy the industry has a direct commercial incentive to delete.

**R3 — no payer.** §9's covenant is a game-theoretic construction: bind the agent
to a record, price the error, and defection stops paying. The tape walks through
the liability chain and finds it terminates in nobody: *"you can't arrest an AI. So, it's the company executives. Except this is America and you can't arrest them either."* `[06:00]` If that is right, then the ledger records without deterring —
which is the same correction canon already filed against itself at §18.8 (a ledger
certifies origin, never validity), arriving here from the outside and as a joke.
The tape's actual remedy is not a record at all: it is *"They just won't sell you the tireless one"* `[06:10]` — a withholding, i.e. a permitted-set decision. That
is a real position, and it is not the house's.
