## 1. His claims, in his own words

### 1.1 The deep relational claim (the load-bearing one for us)

> *"You cannot think of the universe just little things with properties and everything else comes out from it, because **what quantum mechanics describes are… not things by themselves… [it] is the way they affect something else**."* — `[QUOTE, L40-41]`

> *"This is the deep relational aspect of reality which I think physics itself is pointing at, and this is also my reading of quantum mechanics. There are different readings of quantum mechanics, but even if one takes different readings… like hidden variables or many worlds, they're also saying that **if there is a fundamental objective reality it's a priori inaccessible to us**, because the hidden variables are hidden, and **the universal state of the universe is not accessible because we're just in one branch of it**."* — `[QUOTE, L41-42]`

> *"The enlightenment gave us this myth of an objective reality which can see completely, entirely clearly and with certainty **from the outside**… **reality is — we [are] inside reality. We interact with things and so we always have a partial view of things**."* — `[QUOTE, L43]`

**Note the argumentative structure — and it matters for the audit:** Rovelli here does **not** lead with RQM's formal claim (that states are only defined relative to another *physical system*). He gives a **reading-neutral, epistemic** claim: *whichever* interpretation you take (Bohmian hidden variables, Everett many-worlds, RQM), the upshot is that the absolute state is **inaccessible**. That is a weaker and more defensible position than the one the house attributes to him ("RQM predicts no human/AI observer asymmetry"). It also means the interview gives us **RQM's spirit, not RQM's formalism** — a calibration the digest must carry into the audit.

### 1.2 Relational properties, extended all the way to chairs

> *"chairs are not object by themselves — chairs are the thing to sit on, so they exist as chairs because we sit on them, so they exist **in relation to us**… we name them in relation to us… why do we carve the world in that particular way and call it a chair? Because it's a useful way to think about the world. **And so is an electron.**"* — `[QUOTE, L50-51]`

> *"after I've said that a chair is real, I think [whether an electron is real] is not a good question."* — `[QUOTE, L51]`

### 1.3 Monism without a privileged level — and the anti-atomist cut

> *"I believe that your soul is nothing else than fundamental physics. But it would be silly to think, 'Okay, now I understood what your soul is.' I haven't. To understand what your soul is I have to talk to you, have a human relation with you."* — `[QUOTE, L30-31]`

> *"I'm not a dualist at all. I'm a monist, strongly. I think reality is one… **your soul and the atoms are same stuff described in different manner.** I don't think that one description is a good one and the other is a bad one, and neither that one description is complete and the other is incomplete."* — `[QUOTE, L31-32]`

> *"the proper way of thinking is in terms of chemical bonds and all that stuff… in exactly the same manner [as between] our laws, our norms, our morality and physics. **They're not different things.**"* — `[QUOTE, L34-35]`

> *"we have to accept this… **reality can be approached at different levels**… do not consider this alternative but **complementary**."* — `[QUOTE, L22]`

**The bicycle (his workhorse analogy, and it is aimed directly at reductionism):**

> *"I do believe that my bicycle fully respects the laws of the standard model particle physics… But if my bicycle breaks, I don't take it to sort particle physics to fix. I take it to a mechanic who doesn't know a word about quantum field theory but is much better in repairing my bicycle… **the proper way of dealing with a broken bicycle is in terms of pedals and chain**, not neutrons and protons and quarks."* — `[QUOTE, L20-22]`

**Consequence he draws explicitly — "physics is overrated":**

> *"it's a reaction against a number of colleagues… and also some view in our culture in general which tend to say **everything is physics. If you understand physics you understand everything** — which I don't believe."* — `[QUOTE, L37]`

> *"I also criticize the opposite extreme… which says what is fundamental is something else and physics/science is the same as technique… I think **both these stories are wrong, profoundly wrong**."* — `[QUOTE, L38-39]`

### 1.4 "There is no space" — with the caveat he attaches every time

> *"what we mean by space is just the collection, the family, **the cloud of these quanta of space… interacting with one another**… you think, 'Okay, now this little packet of space — they move in space.' No. **They don't move in space. There's no space in which they move.** That's what they are."* — `[QUOTE, L63-64]`

> *"the geometry of space in the small must be quantised… the key quantum property is discreteness — [space] may be made by quanta, little packets of things; light is photons… **space is the same in the very small: made by little packets**."* — `[QUOTE, L61-63]`

**His relativity-of-the-present move (invoked via Galileo):**

> *"What is the velocity of the moon with respect to the Earth? …with respect to the Sun? …with respect to the galaxy? But if you ask, what is the velocity of the moon, period — **this doesn't mean anything**… **giving up very often is a step which is wisdom, because you're asking the wrong question**."* — `[QUOTE, L46-47]`

### 1.5 The methodological claim (his real attack surface against *us*)

> *"An idea which has not yet been falsified has no reason to be interesting."* — `[QUOTE, L75]`

> *"many theoretical physicists… have this idea that to do fundamental science is to pick, out of the blue, some idea not yet falsified and work at it just in case… **science has never worked this way**."* — `[QUOTE, L74-75]`

> *"So this idea — a lot of Physical Review is full of articles [that go] 'oh, I study this theory' — why you study this theory? … well, how do you know it's wrong? **But that's not the right question. The right question is: why do you think there is a chance it will be right?**"* — `[QUOTE, L76-77]`

**And the defence of his own theory — the criterion he applies to himself, verbatim useful for grading our own posture:**

> *"loop quantum gravity… is built on something more solid, because the physical hypothesis on which it is built is that **quantum mechanics is right**… and **general relativity is right as a classical limit**… it's profoundly different than saying 'what if Lorentz invariance was wrong, what if particles were strings, what if there were more dimensions…' — **it's not a 'what if' theory**… it's **extremely conservative in its assumptions and becomes radical in its conclusions**."* — `[QUOTE, L78-80]`

> *"Special relativity is the most conservative thing you can imagine… it's super conservative, but then you realise the only way to make the two compatible is to **give up simultaneity** — and that's radical."* — `[QUOTE, L80-82]`

**Caveat he holds on his own work (relevant to how we should hold ours):**

> *"this is a theoretical construction that I think is credible because it's based on general relativity, on quantum mechanics… but **credible does not mean that we're sure about it**. It's a possibility."* — `[QUOTE, L65-66]`

### 1.6 "Dare not to know"

> *"I'm not challenging… the courage to know. **I want to know.** … And I believe that we're going to get to that. But at the same time… let's make explicit the fact that we are aware that there are so many things we do not know, and **there's nothing wrong in not knowing**."* — `[QUOTE, L88-89]`

> *"We often make this mistake… where we don't know — we patch up a story and we make it dogma and forget about that. Accept the fact that we don't know; that's a good scientific attitude."* — `[QUOTE, L90-91]`

## 3. The immutability tension: real or apparent?

The task asked specifically whether RQM sits uneasily with an **append-only ledger** — "a relational state that is never absolute" vs "an immutable commit." **Answer: the tension is REAL but NARROW, and it is not where the house assumes it is.**

- **It is NOT about immutability.** RQM has no trouble with irreversible records. Relationalism *requires* that a system, from its own side, has definite, stable facts about what interacted with it. An append-only log is what a relational world looks like **from one system's inside** — and Rovelli says we are always inside `[QUOTE, L43]`. Immutability is therefore **compatible**: the commit is immutable **relative to the ledger**.
- **It IS about absoluteness.** What RQM forbids is the claim that the commit is a fact **for all systems** / **from the outside** — *"the myth of an objective reality which can see… from the outside"* `[QUOTE, L43]`. The monograph does not merely claim a local record; it claims an **"irreversible classical ledger commit at the molecular boundary"** (§3 L51) and a **global** boundary-condition cone (§8). Those two are absolute claims, and *those* are what RQM cuts.
- **The house's own documents already half-concede this.** `[RECON]`'s finding that "an immutable ledger freezes the recorded **syntax** but cannot freeze the **semantics**" (bytes fixed, reader weights drift) is the **engineering shadow** of Rovelli's point: the record is absolute-as-bytes and relative-as-meaning. RQM says the *same split* is ontological, not just practical.
- **Net:** the ledger survives contact with RQM. **The word "classical" (§3) and the global cone (§8) do not.** This is a **two-word/two-paragraph** repair, not a rewrite — which is itself the finding: the house's ledger *engineering* is more robust than its ledger *metaphysics*.
