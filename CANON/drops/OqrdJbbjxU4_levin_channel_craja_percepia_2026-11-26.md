## 1. Summary

Patricia (maths/statistics background, data-science freelancer) opens with a personal motive (her mother's cancer, years of medically unexplained symptoms that resolved after meditation) and her route into active inference via Andy Clark. The talk proper:

1. **Thesis.** Perception is the inferential process by which a mind builds and updates its world model; per active inference, "precision parameters um are these primary control axes or variables of perceptual variation" (sensory, prior, policy/preference, meta-precision). What matters is their relative weighting, a *perceptual phenotype*. Her research question: "can we recover the latent precision parameters of a mind's generative model from natural conversation through reverse inference from observable observable variation in personality and belief dynamics" `[VERIFIED]`.
2. **Instrument.** Percepia, an LLM-intermediary layer; "it captures longitudinal trajectories um not snapshots and distributions not point estimates" `[VERIFIED]`.
3. **Experiment 1.** "we prompted 125 Claude based LLM personas to approximate low medium and high sensory prior and policy precision", each replicated five times, "which gave us 625 personas"; conversation-inferred Big Five "converged with the self-reports of the personas at a high correlation of 0.86 to 0.95 per trait"; "policy precision generally was the most strongly recovered"; against "over 400,000 human big five self-reports", "around 10 percent of these personas fell outside of like human big five space", mostly low-sensory/low-prior configurations `[VERIFIED]`.
4. **Experiment 2.** "we prompted 25 personas to vary and described meta precision across five levels", read out on Kegan stages; meta-precision correlated negatively with the lower stages and positively with the higher, and "the science flipped exactly at the socialize to self-authoring boundary"; Greek archetypes partly tracked it, "the Buddha families were not that easy to to get out" `[VERIFIED]`. She hedges: "of course this does not say LLMs are conscious".
5. **Applications.** A personalization layer that "has as a training signal not engagement like today's LLM but that has like an expansion of of meta precision of of epistemic death" (i.e. depth); mechanism-based psychiatry; long-term, charting minds and asking for cancer "can we see that in in in a belief space before we see that in biology" `[VERIFIED]`.
6. **Q&A.** The interlocutor's core objection: with a language interface, the problem is "how much of what you see is coming from the from the language layer as opposed to from the system itself"; "You're basically making a composite intelligence then."; "the one thing the language encodes is a bunch of human cognitive architecture" `[VERIFIED]`. He suggests language-free measures (mutual information, transfer entropy), offers xenobot calcium-signalling video, describes non-regenerating mammalian limbs as cells "having a prior that they can't", and sets out a thoughts-and-thinkers spectrum and the view that we are "patchworks". Patricia concedes "At the moment I need I need speech data, like language data." and says she will think about non-language data. On how she finds directions she cites bodily tingling and dreams ("I just listen to my bodily tingling").

## 2. Strongest point

**Ground truth by construction.** Precision parameters are latent in humans, so she moved to a setup where they are set: "we needed a setup where we know them by construction". Without known parameters the inverse problem cannot be scored at all, so this is the right first move, and she calls it "a bit flawed" herself rather than overselling it `[VERIFIED]`. The secondary result has an honest edge too: about 10 % of prompted personas fall *outside* the human Big Five band, which is a falsifiable claim about where some parameter settings sit `[VERIFIED]`, and it hints that the space under study is wider than human personality data `[HYP]`.

## 3. Weakest joint

**The recovery loop is closed through language at both ends, so it validates the prompt, not the parameter.** The personas were told to "behaviorally approximate" precision levels *through language*; an LLM pipeline then reads those levels back *from language*; the Big Five check compares two outputs of the same prompted persona (conversation vs. self-report). A correlation of 0.86–0.95 therefore shows that a prompted LLM stays consistent with itself and that a second LLM can decode the style it was told to adopt `[HYP]`. It does not show that a generative precision parameter exists behind the text, which is the claim the clinical and cancer applications need. The interlocutor names the same joint from the other side (the language layer brings its own human cognitive architecture, and adding it makes a composite intelligence) `[VERIFIED]`, and her answer on tape is that she needs language data for now. Runner-up: the jump from persona experiments to cancer appearing first "in a belief space before we see that in biology" has no data on tape at all `[VERIFIED that none is shown]`.
