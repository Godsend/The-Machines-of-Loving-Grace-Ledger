## §1 · What Garton Ash actually argues (the source's own words)

**1.1 — How liberalism went wrong: hubris, then external shocks.** The diagnosis opens with *"in the early 2000s we fell prey to hubris"* — *"we really believed it was mission accomplished"* — followed by shocks arriving **from outside**: *"the empire started striking back"* (Putin), the global financial crisis → eurozone crisis, *"neoliberalism, extreme economic inequality"*, and *"the quite spectacular rise of China as a competitor superpower."* `[EST]` (his stated account).

**1.2 — China is the true ideological alternative: "developmental authoritarianism."** *"If you wanted to describe it very kindly, you would say developmental authoritarianism."* His sharpest structural claim: seen from the Global South, *"China looks in many ways more attractive"* because *"Western democratic capitalism is just failing to deliver. And in many respects, they're not wrong."* `[EST]` as his thesis; the empirical payload (South American / African perceptions) is `[HYP]`.

**1.3 — He declines the "we're back in the 1930s" analogy.** In the 1930s *"people thought communism was the future… And then there was another big group of people who thought fascism was the future"*; today *"I don't think many people… think Putin's Russia is the future. Maybe not so much even Xi Jinping's China."* What they believe instead is that the West is failing. `[EST]` (his own qualification).

**1.4 — The US midterms: he refuses European over-reading.** *"He's going to be the largest and angriest lame duck in the history of the world"* — still *"in charge of foreign policy"*, still *"intent on trying to impose a capitulation piece on Ukraine in his farcical attempt to win the Nobel Peace Prize"*, proposing *"a trilateral summit with Putin and Xi Jinping in Shenzhen, China, which is a sort of parody of the February 1945 Yalta meeting."* `[HYP]` (all of these are near-term forecasts and characterisations, not outcomes). The lesson he draws: *"keep doing the same only more so"* — build European resilience and independence.

**1.5 — Ukraine: "a just peace" is not available; a partial outcome can still be a Ukrainian victory.** *"People talk smoothly of a just peace. I'll tell you what's not possible. That's a just peace… a just piece would be Ukraine regaining all its sovereign territory and Vladimir Putin being on trial in the Hague."* What is *"possible"*: Ukraine accepts *"the de facto loss of about one-fifth of its territory"* (Crimea, eastern Ukraine) and becomes *"a strong sovereign independent state on its way into the European Union"* — *"a victory for Ukraine"* and *"a huge defeat for Putin."* `[HYP]` (his judgement of the achievable set).

**1.6 — Escalating Russian hybrid war; the "paper tiger" demonstration.** *"There is constantly escalating Russian hybrid attacks on us"* — *"explosive-laden drones at Leipzig Airport is a spectacular example"* — and the fear that Putin may *"having a go at some corner of European NATO territory"* (he names *"Nava"* `[ASR: Narva]`, Estonia's majority-ethnic-Russian city) *"to demonstrate that with Trump in the White House, NATO is a paper tiger."* `[HYP]`.

**1.7 — The settlement-condition claim (relayed third-hand).** Asked what would move Putin to negotiate, he relays: *"When his generals tell him he's losing, then he will negotiate seriously. He does not yet believe that he is losing."* `[HYP]` — a **relayed** anecdote (a friend's conversation with "a Central Asian leader"), not his own intelligence.

**1.8 — The European political balance is "finely balanced" but tilted.** Over the 2025–2027 election cycle — Spain, *"two very worrying big wins for the alternative for Germany"*, a French presidential election *"which Marine Le Pen at the moment looks likely to win"*, Italy, Poland — *"by the end of 2027 we may be getting a very different and anti-liberal nationalist populist Europe."* His analytical call: *"analytically I think the nationalists are more likely to prevail"* — *"but it is by no means inevitable."* `[HYP]` throughout.

**1.9 — Social democracy's structural problem, stated bluntly.** In *"Donald Trump's definition of communism, we are enjoying all the delights of communism in Europe"*; the real bind: *"we have aging populations, very large welfare states, high levels of national debt and populations that are getting increasingly allergic to the immigration we need to pay our pensions"* plus *"anti-liberal nationalist populist parties that are exploiting those fears."* `[EST]` (his framing of the constraint).

**1.10 — Antisemitism: two distinct currents, kept apart.** He shares Simon Schama's anxiety but splits it: *"old-fashioned European antisemitism, particularly on the right"* versus left-wing *"fury about Gaza"*; *"I think that, you know, old-fashioned European nationalism and xenophobia, and indeed anti-Semitism is very much extremely worrying in Poland, but there is also a part of the story for whom the person most responsible is probably Benjamin Netanyahu."* His test: if Israel-Palestine changed, *"a good deal of the anger… would fade away"* and *"you would see what is actually a kind of hardcore, very worrying… actual antisemitism and xenophobia."* `[EST]` as his position; the counterfactual is `[HYP]`.

**1.11 — The missing liberal idea: heroes and ideas.** He invokes Brecht's *Galileo* (`[ASR: "Battle of Brechtin"]`): *"unhappy of a[the] land that has no heroes"* / *"unhappy of the land that has need of heroes."* He looks for a Havel but *"don't know that I see that anymore, anywhere."* He nonetheless names figures he rates: *"someone like Kaya Kallas from Estonia is not half bad"* `[ASR: Kaya → Kaja Kallas]`; *"Netta Fredrickson, the Danish prime minister"* `[ASR: Mette Frederiksen]`; Peter Magyar's Hungarian election win; a *"leaderless student movement toppling a strong man in Serbia"*; and *"Andy Burnham, our new prime minister, is not half bad"* `[EST]` as his assessments. His own guard: *"I think there's a real danger of putting it all on the leaders."*

**1.12 — Brexit and the "stupidity" thesis.** *"Stupidity is a much underrated factor in history. And the most dangerous form of stupidity is the stupidity of extremely well-educated and intelligent people like Boris Johnson and Michael Gove who gave us Brexit."* On public regret: *"All the opinion polls tell you very clearly"* — if Britain could *"say abracadabra… Britain is back as a full member state of the EU… Most people would be very happy."* Rejoin would be *"an absolutely bloody process"* and yield *"a much worse deal than we had before"*, hence *"a matter of five to ten years."* `[HYP]` (the timeline and the counterfactual).

**1.13 — Immigration and integration (the book's core).** *"Our societies have changed out of recognition in the last 60 years"* — from *"one in 20 people with a migration background"* in 1970s Western Europe to *"one in five or one in four, one in three in a younger generation and one in two in big cities."* The prescription: the *"precise opposite"* of the last 30 years — *"Take back control… It was a brilliant slogan because that's what people all over Europe feel."* He rates **Britain** as unexpectedly good at integration because *"Britishness is already such a baggy identity."* `[EST]` as his argument; the demographic figures are **his, relayed, not independently verified** `[HYP]`.

**1.14 — NATO trust is broken.** *"I don't think the trust in the NATO Article 5 guarantee… the US actually would come to our aid. I think that's gone."* Europe's best era was *"being built under this American security umbrella, all the way from 1945… to 2025. But I think that trust is blown."* He concedes Trump *"was a little bit right in terms of the percentage of GDP being spent in Europe on defense"* — but notes *"every other American president… since George W. Bush had been saying that, no one had paid any attention."* On the "West": *"we used to talk about the West… It existed as a geopolitical actor. And I think what's happened in the United States between 2016 and now has destroyed that."* `[EST]` as his assessment.

**1.15 — Taiwan: squeeze, not invasion.** *"I think if I'm Xi Jinping, I look at Ukraine and I think, God, I'm not so sure if I want to risk it as a military invasion. But I also think I can go on just gently squeeze… by economic and political means."* `[HYP]`.

**1.16 — The "five Melonis" argument.** On reassuring ourselves that Le Pen "won't be so bad": *"George and Maloney is correctly described as a post-post-fascist"* `[ASR: Giorgia Meloni]` — *"Five Malones is very different from one Maloney. **1.17 — The "triple challenge" and "a scramble for Europe."** *"We are under attack militarily from Russia, economically from China, and politically from the United States. Unprecedented triple challenge, right?"* — and, echoing 19th-c. colonialism, *"what we're now beginning to see is a scramble for Europe… buying up bits of Europe, the Chinese, the Russians, Turkey and others"*, so that *"the roles are reversed and suddenly we become the object of the ambitions of non-Western… great powers."* `[EST]` as his framing; `[POETIC]` on the "scramble" figure.

**1.18 — The threat of "Trumpism without Trump," oligarchy, and the reservoir of anger.** *"The more I think about Donald Trump, the more I worry that we're still vulnerable to Trumpism… the reservoirs, the aquifers of anger are still overflowing"*; *"the United States is quite far down the road to oligarchy"*; and *"Elon Musk's what was it 500 million dollars didn't swing at this time. **1.19 — The post-Western world (his strongest structural claim).** Putin's **2008 Munich** speech (`[EST]`, historically accurate) *"used hypocrisy as the lever against us… And in some measure, he was not wrong."* Then: *"what I think is historically new about this moment is that we're already in a post-Western world.
