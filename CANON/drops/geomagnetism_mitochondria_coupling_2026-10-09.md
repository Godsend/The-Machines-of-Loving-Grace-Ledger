# Core Claims & Audit

- **[VERIFIED-STRUCTURAL] Mito-polariton (preprint):** anomalous **71.0 THz** mode appears only in *living/intact* cells, mouse tissues, and isolated mitochondria; vanishes on drying/grinding. Model: light couples to lipid **CH₂** bonds in cristae, splitting the intrinsic **87 THz** vibration into **71 THz + 103 THz**. Mitochondrion length (~2.2 µm) ≈ 87 THz wavelength in the membrane → cristae act as a microcavity.
- **[VERIFIED-NUMERIC, WEAK-N] ATP effect:** 10 min weak MIR → **ATP +10.3% @ 71 THz**, **+10.1% @ 87 THz**, **no significant change @ 53.7 THz control**. **n = 8 per group, single cell line (HEK-293T), cultured cells only.** Frequency dependence argues *against* pure heating, but the mechanism is not established.
- **[VERIFIED] Geomagnetic shielding (fly study):** MuMetal GA4 shield, internal field **5 nT (0.0056 µT)** vs ambient **25–60 µT**. Male *Drosophila*, WT (w¹¹¹⁸) vs *Pink1⁻* (Pink1^B9/Y), entry at day 10 or 20, survival to day 70.
- **[VERIFIED] Differential / genotype-specific response:** *Pink1⁻* under HMF → **~+20% lifespan, hazard ratio 0.50** at the day-20 entry, but **climbing ability declined**. WT under HMF → **decreased lifespan, improved locomotion**. Authors' own framing: *not uniformly good or bad; depends on mitochondrial/genetic state.* This genotype-dependence is the paper's real finding and the video underweights it.
- **[VERIFIED] Mechanism trace:** NV-centre diamond quantum sensors → elevated **superoxide**; high-resolution respirometry → **Complex II**-supported respiration increased (statistically solid in WT; weaker/partial in *Pink1⁻*). Authors propose Complex II upregulation partly compensates Complex I loss in the mutant.
- **[VERIFIED] NUMTs:** mitochondrial DNA segments inserting into nuclear DNA; prevalent in **human brain (prefrontal cortex)**; more NUMTs → **earlier death** in >1,000 ROSMAP adults; stress accelerates insertion 4–5×.
- **[VERIFIED] Mitokines:** neuron mitochondrial stress → Wnt/EGL-20 + serotonin → peripheral UPR^mt → increased healthspan/lifespan; the **germline** is a required coordinator (Cell 2024), not just a bystander.

# Friction Row (limits of coupling — rebuilt)
1. **The video's title overclaims.** Neither paper shows "direct interaction" between the geomagnetic field and mitochondria. The fly study shows *removal* effects; the mechanism (radical-pair? magnetite? something else) is **not identified**. The paper's own word is "modulate."
2. **Drosophila magnetoreception already failed a large replication.** Bassetto et al., *Nature* 620:595 (2023), tested **~110,000 flies** and found **no magnetic-field effects on behaviour** — and Kyriacou (*Nature* 629:E3, 2024) rebutted. The Nottingham paper **cites both** (refs 47–48), so the authors know the field is contested. Their study is about metabolism/lifespan, not compass behaviour — but it lands in a literature with a live replication problem.
3. **Weak-field physics has a conservative ceiling.** Hore's group derived a **<10 ppm** upper bound on radical-pair yield change from a 1 µT ELF field in a 25–65 µT geomagnetic background (*eLife* 2019). BUT: the hypomagnetic (near-zero) regime is the documented exception — theory predicts HMF effects can be comparable to or *larger* than applied-field effects (arXiv 2208.10465). So a large HMF effect is not automatically disqualifying; it is, however, exactly the regime where the fly result lives and where replication is thin.
4. **The polariton is inferred, not observed.** The 103 THz branch sits inside the water/protein absorption band → **the model's upper polariton branch is experimentally inaccessible by their own method**, and the authors state their main limitation is the absence of 87 THz energy-transfer dynamics. One branch observed + one branch unobservable + a fitted model ≠ demonstrated strong coupling. ScienceAlert and The Brighter Side both flag this; the video says it too, then moves on.
5. **Paternal mtDNA "17 people" is likely an artifact.** Wei et al., *Nat Commun* 11:1770 (2020), analysed **11,035 trios / 33,105 genomes** and concluded the "biparental" signatures were **mega-NUMTs** (nuclear-embedded mitochondrial segments) — *"no evidence of paternal transmission of mtDNA in humans."* The video presents a contested/possibly-retracted reading as settled.
6. **Title/scale mismatch on the fly result.** A 20% lifespan change in a *Pink1* mutant under 5 nT is a big effect from an extremely small perturbation. Either (a) the hypomagnetic regime is genuinely a special large-effect window, or (b) there is a confound. The paper controls light/noise/temperature and uses pilot-agreed phenotypes, but n is modest (40–60 flies/treatment, 20/vial) and there is **no independent replication**.
7. **What would settle it:** (i) independent replication of the 71 THz mode with different instruments/labs; (ii) direct measurement of polariton level splitting + coherence lifetime; (iii) fly HMF replication in a second lab with the same shield spec; (iv) a *Pink1* rescue experiment showing Complex II is causal, not compensatory decoration.
