**Why R4 cuts at the house — stated plainly.** Bender's whole thesis is that attributing goals/desires/planning to the software is *the* trick of the AI con, and that the systems are, in her words, a *"designed, built artifact that somebody designed and built."* EILT's machinery is built on the opposite commitment: it is a theory of **bounded agency** (Markov blanket), of **active inference** (systems that minimise prediction error), and of agents that carry ledgers. The house cannot claim both Bender's critique and its own agency vocabulary without paying for the distinction. Either (a) EILT's "bounded agency" is a *falsifiable modelling commitment* — a Markov blanket is a statement about statistical conditional independence, not about desires, and the house must say so in those terms — or (b) "agent/agency" is doing exactly the anthropomorphizing work Bender names. **The house claim is tagged DOWN here:** we may not assert "these systems are agents with goals" as established; at most "agent" is a modelling label on a bounded, non-desiring system, and the burden is on us to operationalise the difference.

**Why R5 cuts at the house — stated plainly.** EILT's central good is **provenance** — a non-rolling, append-only ledger that records origin. Bender's criterion is strictly stronger: provenance is meaningful only when there is a **socially situated speaker with a reputation** who can be held to account (*"who we think is actually publicly committed to having said those words"*). A record of *"which bits of words go where"* [19:36–19:38] is **not an accountable author.** So provenance-without-truth is, on Bender's account, **necessary but inert**: it does not restore accountability by itself. This is a genuine sharpening *against* the house's framing — the ledger documents; it does not, and cannot on its own, locate a responsible agent. **Tagged down:** witness/provenance claims must not be inflated to "accountability."
