# Koray Kavukcuoglu on the path to AGI

**Speaker:** Koray Kavukcuoglu, CTO, Google DeepMind. **Interviewer:** Hannah Fry.

## §1 · The load-bearing claim — AGI has no test, and never will

> **"The strongest version of the argument is we don't have a test for AGI. We'll never have a test for AGI."** `[04:52]` `[EST]` as a statement of position; `[HYP]` as a claim about the future.

> **"AGI is something that if people find it useful, if you trust it, if you can work with it, the more you trust it to make decisions for you…"** `[05:05]` `[EST]`

> **"Trying to build AGI without products in the loop, I don't think it's going to happen."** `[06:24]` `[EST]`

**The structure of the argument, reconstructed:** there is no static objective test for AGI `[EST]`; therefore the only available evaluation is *use* — usefulness and accrued trust `[EST]`; therefore consumer products are not a distraction from AGI but a **constitutive part of the path to it** `[EST]`.

**This is a coherent position, and it is not stupidity.** It converts an unfalsifiable target into a measurable proxy (adoption, retention, trust) at the cost of measuring something adjacent to the thing itself.

## §3 · The clean opposition

**Both positions start from the same premise and split entirely on what follows.**

| | Premise | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| **Kavukcuoglu** `[EST]` | No static test for AGI exists | Ship it; usage and trust are the evaluation |
| **This house** `[HYP]` | No static test for AGI exists | Build the record; provenance and adversarial verification are what remain |

**The discriminating question — and it is the one his own wording invites:** *usage generates familiarity, not trustworthiness.* "The more you trust it" describes an accretion curve. A system can become trusted through exposure while remaining wrong, and exposure cannot detect that, because exposure has no adversarial arm.

**A ledger does not solve this either** `[C]`. It produces the evidence on which trust could be *justified*; it does not produce trust. The difference is that justification is auditable after the fact and familiarity is not.

## §4 · Friction rows (where our own framing is weaker than his)

Required by the standing rule — a mapping with no divergence row is decoration.

1. **His proxy has a real advantage we lack: it is actually measured.** Adoption and retention are numbers. "Provenance and adversarial verification" is a program, not yet a metric. `[C]` He can show a curve; we cannot yet.
2. **Products expose systems to real users, which is genuine adversarial contact.** Our verification is self-administered. His is distributed and involuntary. `[C]`
3. **"Trust" and "verified" are not the same, but neither is "verified" and "safe."** A perfectly audited system can be harmful in ways the audit does not model. `[C]`
4. **His position is more falsifiable than ours at the level of prediction.** He says architectures change within five years and names recursive self-improvement as a *current* priority `[EST]`; that is a dated, checkable claim. Our equivalent claims have been less specific. `[C]`
