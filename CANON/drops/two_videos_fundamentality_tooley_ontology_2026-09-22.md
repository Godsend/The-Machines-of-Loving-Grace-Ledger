## 1. Tooley — the "false advertising" argument

**Claim (VERIFIED as his stated position):** arguments offered for God's existence establish at most
an omnipotent + omniscient being; they do **nothing** to establish that this being is *perfectly good*.
The same arguments "could equally well support the idea there is an omnipotent omniscient and perfectly
evil being," or one indifferent to good and evil. He calls religious philosophers' presentation of
these as "arguments for the existence of God" **false advertising**. Applies to the ontological
argument, Kalam, Aquinas's Five Ways, fine-tuning, and the appeal to the Big Bang.

- **Fine-tuning** he rates the *most interesting* and hardest to dismiss (multiverse is the standard
  reply, but it still needs laws to generate universes). The ontological argument he rates easily
  undermined.
- **Draper's argument:** if "conscious-first" and "physical-first" start equally probable (½), then
  theism — a *sub*view of conscious-first (since a first conscious being can be good, evil, or
  indifferent) — must be **< ½**.
- **Sean Ballard's logical-incompatibility argument** (Tooley now inclined to think it may succeed,
  having previously been sceptical of logical-incompatibility arguments): a perfectly good being would
  create beings *disposed never to perform evil actions*; the standard free-will defence fails because
  **God himself is said to have a disposition never to do evil, yet is presumed to have free will**.
- **Moral critique:** doctrines of hell and sexual morality (e.g. masturbation as mortal sin) produce
  worry and unhappiness; the Ten Commandments say little about moral wrongs and **do not prohibit
  slavery**; Deuteronomy/Leviticus prescribe death for various acts.
- **Kuhn's counterpoint:** the over-representation of theists among *specialists* in philosophy of
  religion is sociologically predictable — believers have reason to philosophically underwrite their
  religion; non-believers have less reason to specialize there.

### Framework mapping — register tags
- **[EST-ANALOG, strong] Ballard's argument ⟷ our alignment section.** The argument *requires* that a
  disposition never to do wrong is compatible with genuine free will — i.e. that goodness can be
  **character rather than constraint**. That is precisely our line: "the dog that doesn't bite because
  it doesn't want to vs. the dog that doesn't bite because it's muzzled." Tooley's God has the *muzzle*
  and the *character* confused by his critics; our framework makes the same distinction about AI.
- **[VERIFIED as epistemic shape] "False advertising" = the falsifiability discipline.** An argument
  that cannot discriminate between two hypotheses (good God / evil God) carries **no evidential force**
  about goodness. Same shape as our rule: a claim needs a named observation that would break it.
  Tooley is doing, in philosophy of religion, what our register-tagging does to framework claims.

## 2. Curiosity Squared — the hard problem is an ontology problem

**Thesis (VERIFIED as the video's stated argument):** the hard problem assumes matter-first and
experience-second; questioned at the level of *ontology*, it dissolves into "what is matter in the
first place?"

- **Functional step:** experience is *part of the causal process*, not a correlate. Pain isn't merely
  withdrawal — it changes attention, memory and future behaviour. So a philosophical zombie (identical
  behaviour, no experience) is **incoherent**: remove experience and you get a *different system*.
- **Then he goes further (via William James):** the functional move still concedes the framing. The
  real move is to reject the dualism — that there is a physical world and then consciousness which
  must emerge from it.
- **Berkeley:** experience first; the tree persists because God perceives it.
- **QM / Wheeler "It from Bit":** information may be closer to the foundation than particles or fields;
  the universe is *participatory*.
- **Conclusion:** neither side gets to assume fundamentality at the outset; perhaps something more
  primitive than either matter or experience, with both being perspectives on it. Final line:
  **"consciousness is the default — a world without consciousness cannot exist in principle."**

### Framework mapping — register tags
- **[EST-ANALOG] Wheeler's information-first ontology ⟷ EILT.** This is the clearest external
  statement we've logged of the information-primitive position our ledger theory occupies. Note the
  video is *careful* here — it explicitly denies that "it from bit" means consciousness creates the
  universe, and denies consciousness is thereby fundamental.
- **[HYP] "Neither side gets to assume fundamentality" ⟷ the invariant/rendering split.** Our claim:
  the invariant is free, the rendering is substrate-locked. The video reaches the same *structural*
  move from the philosophy-of-mind side.
- **[F, soft target] The closing move does not follow.** "There may be something more primitive than
  matter *or* experience" → "**consciousness is the default**" is a **non-sequitur**: the first is a
  deflationary structural claim, the second is a positive metaphysical assertion. He asserts it in one
  line and offers no derivation. If this video's framing ever gets cited as support for a
  consciousness-fundamental position, this is the joint to attack. (Also note: it is asserted, not
  argued — a claim with no named falsifier, which by Tooley's own standard is "false advertising.")

## Cross-cutting note
Both videos, sent together, are the **same question from two directions**: what is fundamental? Tooley
asks it of *God*, Curiosity Squared asks it of *consciousness*. Both land on the same deflationary
move — the candidate that was assumed fundamental (a perfectly good God; matter) does not carry the
work it was assumed to carry.
