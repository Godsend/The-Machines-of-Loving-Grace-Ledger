## §1 · The thesis

**Skill *acquisition* → skill *adaptation*.** The reframing is the argument: acquisition implies adding competencies to a store; adaptation implies **re-configuring existing ones to fit a moving environment**. He is explicit that the old term persists only for intelligibility — *"if I just use skill adaptation all the time, people wouldn't know what I was talking about."*

**Why rigid competence fails.** His opening image is a winning football coach whose method gets named — *"Rivera-ball"* — and then analysed to death: *"coaches now and the opposition know what you're doing, so if you stick too rigidly to your plan... within a few weeks, a month maybe, they'll be working on solutions, antidotes to the impact of Rivera-ball."* **A stable environment rewards a fixed solution; a responsive environment punishes it.**

**Repetition without repetition** (Bernstein), his central practice principle:
> *"repetition is important in training and practice, but **repetition without repetition** is much more important, where an individual can move... go this way or that way on the performance environment depending on the needs at that time."*

Practice tasks should therefore be designed **so that there isn't just one solution.**

**Attractor landscapes and meta-stability.** He traces the landscape image to **Conrad Waddington** (biologist, 1950s — *"a dynamical landscape and how that changes over time... this wonderful image of an attractor landscape"*), then **Scott Kelso** for applying coordination dynamics to movement and brain/behaviour. Meta-stability is the operative zone:
> *"regions of meta-stability are regions where very easily with very little energy, the system, i.e. the person, can transit to another one... that is a major part of skill adaptation."*

**Distributed information.** Asked how anyone can hold the full picture: *"I'd question whether you need that full detail of information."* And Kelso's point, which he endorses — **intentionality itself is a source of information** in how the dynamical system resolves.

## §2 · ★ What this does to `F-LEV-SALIENCY` — and it is not good for us

**Bernstein's "repetition without repetition" is a 60-year-old, empirically massive case that skilled behaviour is organised by *saliency*, not *fidelity*.**

The finding, stated plainly: an expert never performs the same movement twice. Joint angles, muscle activations, and timing all vary run to run. What is preserved is the **task-relevant invariant** — the outcome. The trajectory is *not* the thing that persists.

That is Levin's claim, in motor control, decades earlier and with far more evidence behind it:

| | Claim | Domain |
|---|---|---|
| **Bernstein (1967)** | The invariant is task outcome; the execution varies | motor control |
| **Levin (2026)** | *"continuity of saliency, not literal fidelity"* | developmental biology |
| **Ledger** | The specific unrepeatable trajectory is the ownership condition | consciousness |

**Two independent literatures, both finding the opposite of what our published entry asserts.** This is the strongest pressure `F-LEV-SALIENCY` has received, and it did not come from Levin — it came from a field we had not consulted.

## §3 · The counter, and whether it holds

**The distinction that may save us: Bernstein frees the *execution* from fidelity. He does not free the *history* from it.**

"Repetition without repetition" describes variability **within a single skilled agent whose body, nervous system, and developmental history are continuous.** The adaptivity is *conditional* on that history — an expert's capacity to find a new solution depends on a lifetime of having solved the task. Strip the history and you do not get an adaptable novice; you get a system with nothing to re-configure.

**So the house claim can be reformulated to survive:** not *"the trajectory must be preserved"* but ***"the capacity to re-configure requires a trajectory to re-configure from."*** Saliency governs **execution**; fidelity may still govern **plasticity** — the ability to move between attractors at all.

**This is exactly what `F-LEV-SALIENCY`'s A1-vs-A2 design already tests**, and the prediction becomes sharper: **A2 (saliency-distilled) should show preserved *execution* variance but degraded *adaptation* under repeated domain shift.** That is a much better specified hypothesis than the prereg currently carries, and it needs to go in before any run.

## §4 · Connects straight to the transfer metric

Davids' entire frame is `E_transfer` in athletic dress: **what matters is not what the system can do, but how well its competency survives a changing environment.** "Rivera-ball" is a system with high in-distribution competence and short transfer half-life — it works until the opposition adapts, then collapses.

Two things Addendum A should borrow:

1. **The environment is responsive, not static.** Our OOD ladder treats domain distance as fixed. In sport the opponent adapts *to you* — so the correct test is a **co-evolving** target, not a held-out distribution. That is a harder and more honest test, and it may be the real reason some models look robust.
2. **Meta-stability is a better target than accuracy.** The paper's own Fig. 6 (normalised stress oscillates then drops to zero) measures convergence. Davids would ask a *different* question: **is the system sitting near a boundary where a small perturbation moves it, or deep in a valley where it takes energy to escape?** That is measurable on an attractor landscape and is a much better proxy for adaptability than a converged score.

## §5 · Friction rows

### F1 · **"No one person can have the full picture" vs. our provenance demand**
Davids: *"I'd question whether you need that full detail of information."* The collective works *because* nobody holds the whole state.
The ledger's premise is the opposite: a system is only as real as its immutable, complete record.
**Divergence:** he treats information *absence* as functional; we treat it as the defect. Both can be true at different timescales — but it is a genuine fork about whether completeness is a virtue or a bottleneck.

### F2 · **Meta-stability vs. the ledger's stabilising impulse**
Davids wants systems kept in **shallow, easily-transited** regions. The ledger wants a system pinned to a **fixed, non-rolling** record. Those pull opposite ways: one optimises for mobility, the other for identity.
**Uncomfortable question this forces:** is the ledger a *deep attractor valley* that improves reproducibility at the cost of adaptation? If so, the honest framing is a **tradeoff**, not a benefit — and the tradeoff is measurable (adaptation rate vs. identity persistence under perturbation).

### F3 · **The trauma analogy, which the host raises unprompted**
The host maps deep attractor valleys to trauma and PTSD, citing Robin Carhart-Harris. Gene's formulation — *suffering as defective garbage collection, attachment as a circular reference* — is the same shape: **a prediction-error loop the system cannot exit.**
This converges rather than diverges, but it belongs on the record because it is the second independent arrival at that structure in a week.

## §6 · Verdict

**Strongest point for us.** *"Repetition without repetition"* reframes adaptation as **re-configuration, not accumulation** — which is precisely what `E_transfer` measures and precisely why a curriculum that maximises novelty-per-compute should *help* transfer rather than just accelerate in-distribution learning.

**Strongest point against us.** The same concept is 60 years of evidence that the persisting structure is the **task-relevant invariant**, not the trajectory. Our published Kuhn entry asserts the opposite. **We were not aware of this literature when we wrote it, and it should have been.**

**Weakest joint in the episode.** It is a conversation, not a paper. "Skill adaptation beats skill acquisition" is asserted as a reframe rather than argued against a measured alternative, and no data is presented. Treat the concepts as established (Bernstein, Waddington, Kelso are), and the *terminological campaign* as Davids' position rather than a finding.

**Actionable, in order:**
1. **Amend the `F-LEV-SALIENCY` prereg** with the sharpened prediction from §3 — saliency distils *execution* fine, fidelity governs *adaptation under repeated shift*. That is a better experiment than the one currently written.
2. **Amend Addendum A** to add a co-evolving opponent condition and a meta-stability readout alongside convergence.
3. **Log that Bernstein should have been in the corpus before the Kuhn entry was written.** That is a provenance failure on our side, not on Kuhn's.
