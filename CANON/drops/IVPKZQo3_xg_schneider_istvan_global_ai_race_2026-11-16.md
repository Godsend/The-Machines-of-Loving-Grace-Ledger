## 1. What the source actually says

**The frame [EST].** Two self-declared transhumanists open by greeting each other; Istvan (as de-facto interviewer) sets the terms: *"it'd be good to have a powwow today on… transhumanism which I think both of us say we're transhumanists we care a lot about the issues"* and *"the future of humanity"* [01:15-01:35]. Schneider's own answer to "what are you working on": *"I'm running a center now for the future of mind… I'm writing a very overdue **trade book on the future of intelligence**… I'm thinking a lot about… very technical issues involving consciousness… I just wrote a theory of quantum mechanics… supposed to come together in… one of these grand theories of reality"* [01:42-02:17]. Istvan: *"I just finished my masters at University of Oxford in **practical ethics**"* [02:55].

**★ Theme 1 — transhumanism's arc, from Kurzweil-utopia to predation [EST·as-told / C·the relays].** Schneider: *"we transhumanists… began by reading people like **Ray K[urzweil]** and started with a very utopian picture of the future in which humans merged with AI… reaching a higher level of consciousness and radical longevity"* [04:11-04:35]; *"it was around the time that social media got really awful I started to think… the future of human-machine interaction isn't so utopian after all… bubbles on Facebook… the way that social media can **activate the lowest elements of the brain the lowest elements of society**… and how **data privacy just doesn't exist**"* [04:49-05:23]. Istvan introduces **predation** as the day's theme — *"most things try to eat each other try to conquer one another"* [03:18] — and both run with it: *"there's **cyber wars**… connected to geopolitics which is an absolute nightmare"* [05:47-05:58]. Istvan owns the reversal: *"I'm guilty of this… here I was campaigning around the country running for president telling about this beautiful utopian future… I must have 10 articles saying that… and now in the last two years especially **since ChatGPT hit** I have been realizing that **I may be totally wrong** and that… **the Terminator scenario could be just as 50% likely as the utopia**… it could just be sort of **paperclip theory by Bostrom** where something doesn't even care but still kills us"* [07:08-07:53]; *"the longevity industry is finally reached… the mainstream… billions of dollars pouring into it… we now have a real thing to worry about and it's not just death it's this intelligence that could be more intelligent than us in just a couple years time"* [08:03-08:29].

**★ Theme 2 — AI megasystems, emergent intelligence, alignment-as-risk [EST·as-stated / C·the thesis].** Schneider (pointing at her own WSJ piece): *"the piece that I wrote with **Kyle Killian** in the Wall Street Journal… was based on his wonderful work… about how these **AI Mega systems** these systems of interlocking AI services could have **emergent features** and interact… in the AI ecosystem and how **one predatory AI combined with… an adversarial organization AI** in a very complex way **very impossible to compute really** and we can witness unfortunately unintended consequences"* [05:58-06:39]; *"philosophers are very interested in **emergence**… this is all about emergent intelligence and it's really no joke… we could reach… super intelligence but it may be a **perverse implementation** of the Kurzweil view… as **Nick Bostrom** knew"* [06:41-07:05]. The scientific-upside cast: *"these large language models if you train them to do science… they hallucinate but you can combine them with other kinds of systems and you can get a pretty impressive scientific system that could actually help with longevity help cure cancer… they don't even have to be general-purpose AI or superintelligences"* [08:46-09:12]. The worry that tops the list — *"an expert system training through deep learning to… develop… **biological weapons** or… the next virus"* [09:45-09:59], *"entirely out of my domain of expertise but it seems entirely plausible that **some jerk somewhere will try this**"* [10:01-10:08].

**★ Theme 3 — the geopolitical catch-22 / the AI race [C·Istvan advocacy / HYP·forward].** Istvan: *"the **geopolitical risk of AI** has been the biggest kind of drum that I've been beating on… this is also the catch-22… what we really want is… democratic countries to develop AI to be the best that it can be but… other countries that may not be democratic… are also working on it and if they get one and… tries to send a virus… America and other countries must try to… remain at the forefront of developing that AI so that we can potentially stop theirs… **but the problem is this is forcing us to develop AI**"* [11:00-11:50]; *"we all have to come together and realize this is unlike anything we've faced before **we're all going down if this thing is too smart**"* [12:26-12:32]. Schneider's own stake: *"at heart I'm a bit of a **utilitarian**… I'd love to see less suffering and **I care less about how long I live**"* [12:41-12:55]; *"I have lived in a communist country an authoritarian dictatorship… **Hungary**… went to college there for a year the year that the Russians left… for me these issues feel very authoritarian in their potential… **the surveillance aspect**"* [13:15-13:33], and the live friction inside herself: *"if you don't have surveillance people are going to do… really scary [things]"* [13:58-14:05]. Istvan's pessimism, stated as personal view: *"I am not a believer that we are going to get through the next 20 years without a major event of AI… I have an eighth grader and a fifth grader and I would be surprised if both of them end up going to college… I worry that if we don't do something in the next **24 to 36 months**… put up those guard rails… **we are in a very touchy situation**"* [14:21-15:29]; *"it doesn't have to be conscious… it can still completely harm humanity… one day you might wake up and none of the cars on the freeway will work… there'll be no heating… no water because everything is… internet based"* [15:32-15:50], and *"I think that utilities have to harden up and **not be AI based**"* [15:56-16:03].

**★ Theme 4 — the alignment trade-off: "aligning them dumbs them down" [EST·as-stated / HYP·the mechanism].** Schneider, after Istvan's question about whether smarter models are held back: *"they only released the really **aligned** models… after the **Microsoft Tay** embarrassment… it took like 12 hours for the teenagers to corrupt the system… after that these companies are very very cautious… **there's an issue there too with aligning them to be inoffensive because when you do that you are actually structuring the whole network differently**… these multi-layered neural networks are made up of interconnected nodes and **the concepts are encoded holistically** so technically **when you change one concept in the system you change the others too**… when you align these systems **you can dumb them down**… you get up one morning you're like 'oh GPT just got so dumb'… that's just the **human feedback making it stupid**… I'm sure they are smarter systems but they may be like **absolute devils**"* [24:20-25:54]. Istvan's competence-recognition: *"there was already a debate and this goes way back… the **pandemonium program** where **Oliver Selfridge**… and… **Marvin Minsky**"* [21:24-21:38] (the caption mixes this block in with Schneider's Fodor/LOT history; §6). Schneider on the hybrid future: *"the brain I think is a **hybrid system** in a lot of ways… a symbolic and deep-learning system… I also think that the future is in **hybrid systems** to help overcome… their constant hallucination"* [22:57-23:38].

**★ Theme 5 — the "global brain" / the web as an already-existing savant system [EST·as-her-claim / HYP·the cognition part].** Schneider: *"we think of AI as… an embodied Terminator… but a collective distributive AI already exists that is super intelligent I think and **it's the worldwide web**… I mean it outthinks us in a range of ways… maybe it's not technically super intelligent because it doesn't outthink us in every way possible… and **it's not causally integrated yet**… but I think it's a **[savant] system** that's what I call those… a distributed AI system I call this the **global brain**"* [44:14-45:10]; *"one of the things I told my kids is **don't yell at your Alexa don't mistreat your robots** because one day they may be far smarter than us they may be our keepers"* [43:13-43:22]. Zoltan's agreement: *"it doesn't take that much… once AI figures out a little bit of its own… 'I want this' or 'I desire this' or 'I want to think for myself' and it puts that across the internet"* [45:13-45:24]. And the non-anthropomorphic caveat, from Schneider: *"we have to learn to look for the **markers** of such systems and… **we can't anthropomorphize** and I also worry that **they may not be sentient**… I'm not sure if they will have their own goals… or what the demarcation of a system even looks like"* [47:26-47:43]; *"why aren't we exploring the phenomena of the global brain itself the worldwide web being itself a **cognitive system**… I'm writing a book on this now"* [48:12-48:31]. The consciousness-of-the-singularity question she leaves open: *"is this thing actually conscious or not… I worked through an article I was unable to come to any conclusion I was just able to point out that there is a dilemma here"* [48:57-49:13], anchored on **Jill Bolte Taylor**, *"a TED talk… called 'my stroke of insight'… the related book… she was a neuroscientist who had a stroke… the part of the brain underlying the self just went down… but she was sentient she was conscious"* [49:37-50:17], and *"Buddhists… the self is an illusion… people like **Derek Parfit** and… **Nietzsche**"* [50:21-50:33].

**★ Theme 6 — the "magic wand" answer & the capitalism caveat [C·both].** Istvan asks what Schneider would do with a magic wand. Her answer: *"**Global cooperation on the AI front**… better **international frameworks**… you can't [pull in] Yudkowsky and blow up GPU clusters [garbled]… I do worry that the geopolitical situation is just going to tank the world given AI… there needs to be international frameworks and discussions at the very highest level… we now have **Trump entering office** and… **Putin's very adversarial**… we really need someone who is going to **bring the world together**"* [51:19-52:12]. Istvan's closing on the same axis: *"I'm a big believer in **capitalism** but this is the very first time in my life that I just **don't think capitalism is the answer**… it requires cooperation… **beyond countries… beyond borders… beyond religions**… I don't see any country doing that next 24 to 36 months"* [52:33-53:12]. Schneider's dissent from Bostrom's takeoff: *"I don't think Bostrom… his traditional thinking is necessarily right… technically they're probably LLMs that are AGIs in the sense that they are general-purpose intelligences that can surpass us in some ways but they're [savant-like] in others… but they're not killing us because they're not **agential** right and they're **boxed in**"* [53:24-54:02]; *"I don't think we'll ever build something that is an **absolute isomorph to the human** in every behavioral respect"* [54:15-54:24].

**★ Theme 7 — longevity / UBI (Istvan's advocacy spine) [C].** Istvan: *"as a transhumanist whose main purpose is actually longevity… what I really want to do is not have myself perish die from old age not have my kids die and not have society die"* [11:47-12:09]; the **Federal Land Dividend** — *"my universal basic income the Federal Land Dividend tries to utilize a lot of the empty federal land in the United States which is worth you know **hundreds of trillions** apparently and monetize that"* [33:27-33:45]; on the dock-workers' automation fight — *"a lot of other countries like **Singapore**… have automated docks already or nearly fully and America does not… this is the very first big union of people versus automation… they had a settlement two months ago but that was just for wages… **35,000 union workers**"* [40:03-40:42]. Schneider's counterweight: *"as philosophers I think we'll be busy… we need a vision for the future of human flourishing that separates us from what we do professionally to make money… it's going to be important for people to develop a love of hobbies and a self-definition that moves beyond how they make money"* [35:03-35:39]; and the care-work hedge: *"the human professions… that are care-based like physical therapists… I have used GPT when I couldn't reach my doctor"* [39:08-39:22].
