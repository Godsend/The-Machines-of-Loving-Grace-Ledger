## §1 · The thesis, in the source's own words

**The book, and its stated purpose** — Kuhn quotes her own first sentence:
> *"The purpose of this book is to **rehabilitate the idea of the soul** from its state of condemnation in recent philosophy."*
> — **Victoria Trumbull**, *On the Memory of the Soul*

**The genealogy she reconstructs** (an "alternative philosophic genealogy"):
> *"It begins with Plato, as so many things often do in philosophy. But it moves through **Plotinus, who then inspires Augustine**, who then inspires many French thinkers in the medieval and early modern period, which then comes to produce … someone like a **Bergson**."*

**The core claim — that the storage model of memory is an imported computer metaphor:**
> *"We're accustomed to thinking in this way because of the **computer model of mind**, and various metaphors which help us to think of ourselves as sort of ingesting all of this information, storing and rearranging this information, and then representing it to ourselves again. And so, we think of memory as a process that is essentially like some kind of **storage drive, or a phonographic record, or a suitcase**."*

**The non-sequitur argument** — the sharpest move in the interview:
> *"When we see something like brain damage where a great part of, you know, the hippocampus [or] medial temporal lobe … is removed, and … someone's memory is impaired, well, then we must conclude that the memories are somehow stored in those physical places, and the fact of the matter is that **that is not the necessary conclusion**. … you can say that the brain and these specific brain regions are **essential to supporting the dynamics of remembrance**, but **it doesn't follow that specific memories are contained within those specific regions**."*

**And the question that follows from it** — three candidates she refuses to collapse:
> *"What is being lost there? **Is it the memories themselves, or the power of memory itself being destroyed, or is it just the explicit ability to recall**, the actual ability to remember, which is somehow being interrupted?"*

**Bergson's inversion, which she adopts** — the image is the *output*, not the store:
> *"The image is not the memory itself. The image is actually something like the **product or result of remembering**. And this process of remembering is something like a **movement from unconsciousness to consciousness**, and memory kind of has to be sought in this pastness, which is not visibly represented to the mind."*
> Supporting distinction: **habit vs. memory-imagery**; the memory-image is *"a dynamic qualitative sketch of a past event"* that carries an intrinsic **pastness** — *"when we remember, we know that we're thinking about a memory rather than a present perception or an entirely fictive event."*

**The warning she issues against exactly our kind of move** (quoted by Kuhn from her text, then unpacked by her):
> *"There is a risk, however, in elevating these memory-images as beings in their own right, that these images will **breed misguided desire to seek out their physical or psychical location**."*
> Her alternative: *"their presence points to an **anterior movement of the mind which gives birth to them**, and to a pure memory … which is, in its sense, **imageless**."*

**On the soul's definition** — vital activity, not a fixed entity:
> The soul is treated as *"vital activity rather than a fixed definition"* (her section heading on the tape), and as *"the vital activity and essence that's characteristic of the human person."*

## §3 · Verdict

**Strongest point.** **R2** is the cleanest statement of the substrate-supports-but-does-not-contains argument in the corpus, delivered as a *logical* point (*"that is not the necessary conclusion"*) rather than as a metaphysical one. It gives us an independent, non-architectural derivation of F-T1 — and it is delivered on the same channel, to the same host, whose taxonomy this house has already submitted to.

**Weakest joint.** The **"imageless pure memory"** is posited to escape the location objection but is given **no criterion** — it cannot be distinguished from an indefinitely deferred *explanans* by any test she states. Runner-up: the genealogy is presented as a recovered tradition (*Plato → Plotinus → Augustine → Bergson*) with the connecting threads asserted rather than argued.

**Why this belongs in canon.** It is the first source ingested that **names and rejects the house's own central move** — a physical ledger as the seat of memory — from a position of scholarship rather than dismissal. That makes it more valuable than a dozen confirming sources, and it must be carried as a *live constraint on our language*, not as a convergence.

**Falsifiers opened.**
- **F-TRU-1:** if the ledger is ever described as *containing* or *storing* experience, meaning, or memory-content (rather than accountability for commits), this source's R4 objection is confirmed against us by construction.
- **F-TRU-2 (R5):** name one ledger-structural property that produces a *sense of pastness* distinguishable from a reconstructed record. Absent it, the house has no account of the mark she says is essential, and should say so.
- **F-TRU-3:** her non-sequitur argument (R2) is only as strong as the alternative it refuses to specify. If the "power of memory" and the "ability to recall" are not independently characterisable, R2 reduces to a promissory note — and F-T1's independent support from her weakens with it.
