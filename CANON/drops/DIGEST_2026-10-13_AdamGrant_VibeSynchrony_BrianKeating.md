## 0. One-paragraph read (not a recap)

The synchrony material is a **40-second opener**, not the substance of the episode. Grant makes one physiological claim, at L68-81 (repeated as the cold open, L1-3): "when you and I are vibing, our brain waves actually start to synchronize and so do our heartbeats… This is real. It's observable." **He cites no study for it on air.** Throughout the rest of the conversation he names his sources: Bendersky, Woolley, Keltner/ten Brinke, the Eastwick lab, and his own fundraising experiment. The synchrony line is the one empirical claim he leaves unsourced, and it also carries the episode's packaging.

The underlying fields are real and mainstream: EEG hyperscanning, and interpersonal autonomic (HR) synchrony. The best-known findings in them support the house's phase-locking account **more directly than they support "vibe" as a construct**:
- Synchrony is reliably produced by shared sensory context and proximity (He et al. 2026; Burgess 2013).
- It is mediated through ordinary channels: gaze, positive affect, watching the other person (Kinreich 2017; Konvalinka 2011).
- It has been causally driven from outside with phase-locked brain stimulation (Novembre 2017).

So Grant's sentence is directionally defensible as a *marker* claim: rapport → more synchrony. Calling it *proof*, or a lever ("make it happen on purpose"), inflates it. Most of that inflation sits in the channel copy, but Grant adds one inflation of his own: "We are literally vibrating on the same energy frequency" (L74-76). That is a metaphor stated as if it were literal, the opposite of what the description claims.

**Net verdict:** this is an EST-tier anchor for the house account, but cite the **primary literature, not Grant**. This episode is a pointer to the literature, not evidence in itself.

## 1. Claim inventory (Grant's own claims, in order of empirical specificity)

### G1. Rapport is accompanied by interbrain and cardiac synchrony [VERIFIED] [QUOTE, L64-81] [ATTACK SURFACE]
> "So when I think about a vibe, I think about a highquality connection between people. And I think about it happening in ways that neuroscientists can actually measure in the body and the brain. When you and I are vibing, our brain waves actually start to synchronize and so do our heartbeats. And so there is a physiological trace. And I don't think we should find that surprising, right? We are literally vibrating on the same energy frequency. But I think when people hear vibe, they start to think of something that's a little bit hippie or woo woo. and they don't realize, no, no, no. This is this is real. It's observable."

- **Citation status on air: NONE.** No author, lab, modality, effect size, or frequency band is given. [VERIFIED by a full-transcript grep for synchron|heart|brain|EEG|hyperscan|study: there is no synchrony citation anywhere in the 1,140 lines.]
- I could not open the book's endnotes this session. The adamgrant.net and Globe & Mail pages returned no synchrony citation text. Which papers *Vibe* actually cites is an open item, not a finding. [HYP] The likely candidates are the canonical ones checked below (Kinreich 2017, Dikker 2017).
- **What the literature actually supports [EST]:**
  - **Kinreich, Djalovski, Kraus, Louzoun & Feldman 2017**, *Sci Rep* 7:17060 (PMID 29213107).
    - Design: EEG hyperscanning, n = 104 adults, naturalistic conversation.
    - "Neural synchrony was found for **couples, but not for strangers**," in temporal-parietal **gamma**, and was absent during rest.
    - It was "**anchored in moments of social gaze and positive affect**" and was "**unrelated to episodes of speech/no-speech or general content of conversation**."
    - → Real, but tied to existing attachment and to a visible nonverbal channel. Two strangers "vibing" on first meeting, the book's whole use case, is the condition where the main finding was *null* for synchrony at the group level. Within strangers, it scaled with gaze and affect duration.
  - **Dikker et al. 2017**, *Curr Biol* (doi 10.1016/j.cub.2017.04.002), and the follow-up **Bevilacqua, Davidesco, … Poeppel, Dikker 2019**, *J Cogn Neurosci* 31(3):401-411 (PMID 29708820).
    - Students' synchrony tracked engagement. Student-teacher synchrony tracked reported closeness, **but only in lectures**.
    - Critically: "students' retention of the class content **correlated with student-teacher closeness, but not with brain-to-brain synchrony**."
  - **Heartbeat side: He, Christensen, Sørensen & Konvalinka 2026**, *PNAS Nexus* 5(6):pgag181 (PMID 42345043).
    - n = 72, HR, GPS and acoustic recording across multiday NYC trips.
    - "HRs reliably synchronized when participants were in close physical proximity, indicating that **shared environmental context was sufficient to elicit synchrony**." Synchrony was higher among familiar peers and during joint attention, and was reduced by noise.
  - **Konvalinka et al. 2011**, *PNAS* 108(20):8514-9 (PMID 21536887).
    - Fire-walking ritual, n = 38. HR synchrony between walkers and *related* spectators, not unrelated ones.
    - "the mediating mechanism may be **informational**, because participants and related observers had very different bodily behavior." The informational channel here is ordinary: watching a loved one walk on coals.
  - **Palumbo et al. 2017**, *Pers Soc Psychol Rev* 21(2):99-141 (PMID 26921410).
    - Systematic review: physiological synchrony "correlates with a number of psychosocial constructs," but "this scientific literature is **fragmented**, making it difficult to evaluate consistency across reports."
- **Verdict on G1:** the existence claim (physiological traces co-occur with connection) is **[EST]**. The conditional "when you are vibing → they synchronize", stated as near-universal, is **overstated**:
  - it fails for strangers in Kinreich;
  - it occurs *without* vibing under mere shared context (He 2026; Burgess 2013);
  - the "heartbeats" side is HR co-variation over windows, not beats aligning.

### G2. "We are literally vibrating on the same energy frequency" [VERIFIED] [QUOTE, L74-76] [ATTACK SURFACE]
- Interbrain synchrony is phase or amplitude *coupling* within a band (e.g., gamma in Kinreich). It is not two people sharing "an energy frequency". Cardiac synchrony is lagged covariance of heart-rate series. Neither is a shared vibration of anything.
- This is the episode's one genuine woo-adjacent move, and it is **Grant's**, not the host's. It sits two sentences before "no, no, no. This is this is real."
- [RIFF] This is the same rhetorical shape the Radin drop flagged: a physics word ("frequency", "entanglement") is borrowed to make a correlational social finding sound like mechanism. Grant's version is milder because nothing in his argument depends on it. Remove the sentence and G1 is unchanged. Radin's argument depends on the entanglement word.

### G3. Fundraising callers: a five-minute beneficiary contact "more than doubled" phone time and "nearly tripled" revenue [VERIFIED] [QUOTE, L933-953]
> "I randomly assigned some of them to have a five-minute interaction that a month later the average caller had more than doubled in weekly phone time and nearly tripled in weekly revenue."

- **[EST]** Grant, Campbell, Chen, Cottone, Lapedis & Lee 2007, *OBHDP* 103:53-67 (doi 10.1016/j.obhdp.2006.05.004). Grant & Shandell's 2022 *Annual Review* summary gives **142% more minutes and 171% more revenue**, i.e., ×2.42 and ×2.71. On-air "more than doubled / nearly tripled" is a **faithful** paraphrase.
- Minor drift: he says "five-minute" on air, while the Annual Review summary says "just 10 minutes". This is not material.
- **This is his single most specific, falsifiable claim of the episode, and it is correctly cited, but it is not a synchrony claim.** He also volunteers that it was not preregistered ("At that time pre-registration didn't exist", L954-955) and describes how he would preregister it now (L962-976). That counts **for** his credibility.

### G4. Labelling disagreement as "debate" fosters healthy task conflict [VERIFIED] [QUOTE, L204-211]
- **[EST]** Josephs, Bendersky et al., *Organization Science* (doi 10.1287/orsc.2015.1025): "Expressing Task Conflicts as Debates vs. Disagreements Increases **Perceived Receptivity** to Dissenting Opinions in Groups."
- The on-air paraphrase ("even just saying can we debate this is enough to foster healthy task conflict") is slightly broader than a perceived-receptivity outcome, but it is fair.

### G5. Productive remote teams communicate in bursts (Woolley) [VERIFIED] [QUOTE, L337-347]
- **[EST]** This matches the Riedl & Woolley burstiness line of work (HBR/ *Academy of Management Discoveries* era). The citation was not re-pulled this session. Brainwriting > group brainstorming (production blocking, evaluation apprehension) is long-established group-creativity literature.

### G6. Zoom fatigue is "boreout, not burnout": understimulation, not overstimulation [VERIFIED] [QUOTE, L733-737] [ATTACK SURFACE]
- **[CONTRA]** The most-cited theoretical account, Bailenson 2021, "Nonverbal overload: A theoretical argument for the causes of Zoom fatigue" (*Technology, Mind, and Behavior*, doi 10.1037/tmb0000030), argues the **opposite** mechanism: *overload* from close-up gaze, self-view, and reduced mobility.
- Grant states his position flatly ("Zoom fatigue is not over stimulation. It turns out to be under stimulation"), with no citation and no mention of the competing account. It is a contested claim delivered as settled.
- [RIFF] There is irony relevant to the house account: if channel bandwidth matters for synchrony (He 2026: joint attention and proximity raise HR synchrony), then a degraded video channel *reduces coupling*. "Understimulation" then has a phase-locking reading: low-bandwidth channel → weak entrainment → flat interaction. That would be a coherent bridge Grant didn't draw.

### G7. Zoom vs in-person first dates are comparable in attraction [VERIFIED] [QUOTE, L826-843]
- **[EST]** Moreno, Finkel, Ammerman & Eastwick, "In-person and virtual dates are comparable, but people don't know it," *PSPB* (in press per pauleastwick.com). That matches the on-air "Eastwick lab".
- [CONTRA] **for the house as well as for G1.** If heartbeat and brain synchrony depend on proximity and joint attention (He 2026, Kinreich gaze-anchoring), and attraction is still comparable over video, then **rapport survives a channel that should reduce physiological coupling**. Either synchrony is not doing the work, or video carries enough coupling. Grant does not notice the tension with his own opener.

### G8. Epistemic stance: "don't believe anything that can't be verified… but also don't disbelieve anything that can't be falsified" [VERIFIED] [QUOTE, L1011-1027]
- Paired with "we should all be good Bayesians" and "talk in conditional probabilities". This is the right stance.
- [ATTACK SURFACE] The second clause is the exact license Radin-style claims trade on, and it is the house's main friction point with this episode's epistemics. "Don't disbelieve the unfalsifiable" is fine as agnosticism. It is not a reason to grant **any positive credence**, and it gives no evidential weight to an unfalsifiable claim. The Radin drop's withheld-prophecy rule ("unfalsifiable by construction scores zero evidential weight") is the stricter and correct formulation.

### G9. Desirability bias worse than confirmation bias [VERIFIED] [QUOTE, L621-628]
- **[EST]** Tappin, van der Leer & McKay 2017 (*JEP: General*) separated desirability from confirmation bias in belief updating. Citation was not re-pulled this session, so treat it as [HYP] pending a pull.

## 2. Does it fit the house phase-locking account? (Gene: "more synchronized → more synchronicities")

**Short answer: yes. The underlying literature, not Grant's sentence, is the strongest mainstream anchor the house has for that account.** It is much stronger than anything in the Radin audit, because every positive interpersonal-synchrony finding checked here has an identified **local, physical** channel:

| Finding | Channel that carries the coupling | Non-local channel needed? |
|---|---|---|
| Kinreich 2017: couples show gamma synchrony, anchored in gaze + positive affect | Visual (gaze), affective display | No |
| He et al. 2026: HR syncs with proximity; "shared environmental context was sufficient"; noise reduces it | Shared acoustic and physical environment, joint attention | No |
| Konvalinka 2011: related spectators' HR tracks fire-walkers' HR | Visual observation + prior attachment | No ("informational", via sight) |
| Burgess 2013: pseudo-pairs recorded *separately* under the same conditions show "spurious hyper-connections" that "reflected real similarities between the EEGs" | Shared stimulus/task structure alone, no interaction at all | No. This is the purest case: correlation with zero coupling between the two people |
| Novembre et al. 2017: in-phase 20 Hz dual-brain tACS increased movement synchrony vs anti-phase/sham | Externally imposed phase-locking (electrodes) | No. The causal arrow was demonstrated with a physical driver |
| Huygens 1665 (Keating's framing, L142-148) | Mechanical coupling through the shared beam | No. Textbook coupled-oscillator phase-locking |

[RIFF] **Burgess 2013 is the single most useful citation for the Radin friction table.** It shows that two people who never interacted produce measurably correlated EEG simply because they were put through the same conditions. In the abstract's words, the correlations are "not Type-1 errors that could be overcome by some appropriate statistical control." That is the house mechanism in mainstream form: correlated states from shared drivers, which *feel* like connection if you only observe the correlation. It also states the burden on any psi hyperscanning claim (e.g., the Wackermann et al. 2003 *Neurosci Lett* "spatially separated subjects" EEG-correlation line, which drew a published comment, Thaheld 2004, PMID 15082162). Shared-condition rhythmicity must be excluded *by design*, because statistics cannot remove it. [HYP] for the Wackermann application; we did not re-audit that paper.

## 4. Calibrated verdict

- **Credibility of the source:** high. He cites by name everywhere except the synchrony opener, paraphrases his own 2007 effect sizes faithfully, volunteers that it wasn't preregistered, and says out loud that a cosmologist's critique of his op-ed is spawning follow-up research (L1073-1079). His conduct scores well.
- **The synchrony claim specifically:** it is the least-sourced, most-inflated material in the episode, and also the part the channel chose as its headline. It follows the familiar book-promotion pattern: an EST-tier finding (interpersonal physiological synchrony exists and correlates with closeness and engagement) is promoted to a near-universal signature and implied lever. The literature only supports it as a context-dependent, non-specific co-traveller. The inflation is **moderate**, not Radin-class. Nothing in Grant's argument requires new physics, and remove the "energy frequency" line and the claim is just slightly overgeneralized.
- **For the house's phase-locking account:** **real strengthening, but cite the primary papers, not the podcast.** Every positive synchrony finding checked here runs through a local physical channel, and Burgess 2013 shows correlated EEG from shared conditions with no interaction at all. This is the mainstream, replicated form of "synchronized systems look connected". Recommended anchors for the Radin friction table:
  1. Burgess 2013, the shared-driver correlation that statistics cannot remove;
  2. He et al. 2026, environment-sufficient HR synchrony;
  3. Kinreich 2017, gaze- and affect-anchored synchrony;
  4. Novembre 2017, the causal arrow via a physical phase-locking driver.
- **The limit (row 7):** this anchors interpersonal *state* correlation only. It does not by itself explain mind-event "synchronicities", and the closeness-not-synchrony result (Bevilacqua 2019) suggests the felt connection may run on shared priors more than live entrainment. That makes the house account *more* mundane, which is fine, but it means "phase-locking" should be stated as "shared drivers + shared priors", not live entrainment alone.

## 5. Falsifiers

- **F-G1 (for Grant's marker claim).** In a preregistered stranger-dyad hyperscanning study with pseudo-pair and shared-stimulus controls (Burgess design), real-pair minus pseudo-pair synchrony should predict post-interaction rapport ratings at r ≥ 0.2, with n giving 80% power. A null on the *interaction-specific* component falsifies "vibing ↔ synchrony" for first meetings, the book's core use case.
- **F-G2 (causal arrow).** Phase-locked dual-brain tACS (Novembre protocol) vs anti-phase vs sham, with the outcome being **liking/closeness**, not tapping. If in-phase stimulation does not raise rapport above sham (MDE d = 0.3), "synchrony causes connection" is falsified and synchrony is a marker only.
- **F-G3 (umbrella).** In the same dyads, measure EEG coupling, HR cross-recurrence, and behavioral mimicry simultaneously. If the three correlate at r < 0.2 across dyads, "vibe" is not one physiological construct.
- **F-H1 (cuts at the house).** In a fully isolated two-person EEG design (no shared stimulus timing, separately randomized stimuli, Burgess-corrected metrics), the house predicts interbrain correlation at the pseudo-pair baseline. A robust excess, independently replicated, would falsify the "shared drivers only" account, the same fork as the Radin drop's F-R1.
