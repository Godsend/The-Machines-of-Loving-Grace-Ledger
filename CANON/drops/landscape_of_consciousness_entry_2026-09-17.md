Core claims and their registers:

- [H] Phenomenal consciousness is a bandwidth constraint a brain contains rather
  than a product it generates: experience is the narrow serial render ("GUI") a
  wide parallel non-conscious generative process ("GPU") produces of itself.
- [C] Phenomenal character is *identified* with that render — the mandatory,
  opaque format in which a serial system represents processing whose intermediate
  steps it cannot represent. (Identity claim, stipulated not derived. Cross-
  validation flagged this as the entry's load-bearing assertion.)
- [H] The model rendered is shareable; the rendering is substrate-locked —
  structure travels between subjects, qualia do not.
- [C] The classical filter is relocated INSIDE the brain (between its own
  subsystems), against transmission theories (James, Bergson, Huxley).
- [F] Two discriminating predictions: (1) increasing serial load → absence
  before distortion of voluntarily generated content (kills graceful-degradation
  accounts); (2) altered-access states never deliver verifiable external
  information — null detection in verified-target resuscitation paradigms (kills
  every transmission theory).
- [H] Aphantasia marks the same access/generation dissociation in the waking case
  (prediction, not yet a verified datum).

Round 1 (v1) and round 2 (v2/v3), run through `claude -p --model claude-fable-5`,
Oryon (hermes peer rail, DeepSeek), and Icarus (gemini-3.7-flash, direct HTTP to

- **Convergence 2 (2/3 blind, 3/3 by category):** the nearest unnamed occupant is
  Graziano's Attention Schema Theory (Fable + Oryon named it independently), with
  Metzinger's self-model theory and Nørretranders' user illusion as the other

- **Convergence 3 (3/3):** the psychedelic/REBUS prediction was derivative and the
  1/f-slope cluster was a grab-bag; both cut, replaced by the two discriminating
  predictions above.
- **Citation repairs (flagged, then fixed):** Zeman year mismatch and fabricated-
  looking co-authors (verified against PubMed: Zeman, Milton, Della Sala, Dewar,
  et al., Cortex 2020, 130, 426–440); Borjigin overclaim (now cited only for
