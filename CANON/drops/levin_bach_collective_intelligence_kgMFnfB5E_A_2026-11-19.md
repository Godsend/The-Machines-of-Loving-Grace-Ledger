## 3. Argument map — Levin and Bach kept DISTINCT

Tags = the claim's register as stated (not our endorsement). `[C]` = the speaker's own unhedged-but-unestablished position; `[HYP]` = a forward claim the speaker marks.

### 3.A · LEVIN (the biologist's argument)

1. **Neurons are not special — evolution reused ancient cell machinery.** Tunneling nanotubes connect cells several diameters away; *"people like Gaurav Sohail look at very brain-like electrical signaling in bacterial colonies"*; the thought experiment: if biologists had told the connectionists *"Thinking isn't in the brain, it's in the liver."* — *"would they have changed anything… or whether it's fine"*; and the payoff that neuroscience's tools (optogenetics, active-inference, pattern completion) transfer **wholesale** to non-neural spaces ("anatomical morphospace"). `[EST]`
2. **"Competency" is an engineering claim and it is observer-dependent.** Cognitive terms are *"engineering protocols"* — what you can count on when you interact with the system; a brain can be read as "a paperweight" or as a behavioural/linguistic engine, and both readings are *"empirically testable engineering claims"*; **you must specify the problem space and the goal before the claim means anything**. Flagship example: tail cells re-specified to eye-fate *"will create an eye… in the tail, on the gut"*, and a **few** such cells *"recruit local neighbors"* — *"a little bit like in an ant colony… recruitment"*. `[EST]`
3. **A goal can be explicit and rewritable WITHOUT touching the hardware.** The planarian two-headed line: the bioelectric set-point is *"an explicit goal because you can rewrite it"*, *"much like with your thermostat, there's an interface for changing what the goal state is"*; the pattern is a **counterfactual** — *"that two-headed pattern is not a readout of the current state"*, it *"says two heads"* while the worm has one, a representation of *"what the animal will do in the future if it happens to get injured"*; *"both of those can live in exactly the same hardware, in exactly the same body."* `[EST]` **+ one unpublished piece Levin flags himself** (memory-blocker drugs wipe the pattern → *"a featureless circle"*: *"take all this with a grain of salt"* `[91:51.70]`). `[HYP]`
4. **The bioelectric signal is read out as the DELTA, in the spatial domain.** Early hypothesis (a per-cell "paint by numbers") turned out **false**; *"What is actually read out is the voltage difference between two adjacent domains"* — *"It only keys off of the delta… it modifies the controller for what's downstream of that."* `[EST]`
5. **The planarian genome paradox (Levin's own headline puzzle).** Planaria reproduce by splitting → they bypass **Weismann's barrier** → mutations accumulate → *"Their genomes are an incredible mess… basically mixoploid"* — yet they are *"immortal, incredibly good at regenerating with 100% fidelity, and very resistant to cancer."* The standard course says the genome *"is for setting your body structure"*, so why *"does the animal with the worst genome have the best anatomical fidelity?"* His **conjecture**: *"what the competency apparently does is shield information from evolution about the actual genome"* — selection can't tell good genes from good competency, so *"the pressure is off of it"* and competency is driven up — a ratchet. Corroborating fact: *"nobody has isolated a line of planaria that is anything other than a perfect planarian"* in 120 years. Analogy he borrows from **Steve Frank**: a **RAID array** tolerating bad media. `[C]` (Levin's word: *"this is just my conjecture"* `[59:30.02]`).
6. **Memory lives beyond the synapse — and the open problem is DECODING.** *"a memory medium that can survive this radical remodeling and reconstruction"*: McConnell's planaria (heads cut off; tails regrow a brain carrying the memory), Ungar's rats (fear of the dark; a peptide he called *"scutaphobin"*), and caterpillars→butterflies (the brain dissolves in metamorphosis, *"the information remains"*). The stated **unsolved** puzzle: for a *novel* memory, *"how would the recipient look at a weird, some kind of crazy hairpin RNA structure… and be like, oh, yes, that's three light flashes"* — decoding needs a shared codebook — *"Every memory is like a message… a transplanted message from your past self to your future self… you still have to decode your memories."* `[EST]` (+ his own stated gap).
7. **Everything in biology is self-constructed; no priors are given.** *"every cell is some other cell's external environment"*; an embryo *"you have to self-organize a system that puts an arbitrary boundary between itself and the outside world. You have to decide, where do I end and the world begins"*; contrast with robotics where the boundary is handed to you. Consequences: Bongard's 2006 self-model robots; energy/time limits force coarse-graining; *"You can't count on any of the priors… It plays the hands that it's dealt"* — *"assumption that you cannot trust what you come into the world with"* → the flexibility that AGI would need. `[EST]`

### 3.B · BACH (the AI/cognitive scientist's argument)

1. **Every cell is a reinforcement-learning agent; neurons are just specialized cells.** *"every cell is an individual reinforcement learning agent"*; every cell *"has the ability to send multiple message types and receive multiple message types… conditionally"* — so *"every cell can in principle function like a neuron."* A neuron is a *"telegraph cell"* specialized for long-range, high-speed signalling; the brain is *"a telegraphic extension of the community of cells in the body."* `[EST]`
2. **"Spirit" = an old word for "the operating system for an autonomous robot"** — a control pattern that is *"realized by the hardware, but disembodied in a way"*, a concept *"we lost after the Enlightenment"*. `[C]`/`[POETIC]`
3. **The "engineering stance"** (auto-acquired in CS): *"you don't really care what language is it written in. What you care in is what causal pattern is realized"* — *"this engineering stance is very powerful, and you get it automatically when you're a computer scientist"*. He turns it on medicine: **evidence-based medicine lacks causal models** (the airborne-infection example — no peer-controlled study ⇒ "no evidence"). `[EST]`
4. **The perceptron is a caricature of the brain.** *"the perceptron is vaguely inspired by what the brain might be doing, but I think it's really a toy model or a caricature of what cells are doing"* — because cells are *"a self-organizing system that designs itself from the inside out"*, not an externally-applied gradient descent. `[EST]`
5. **★ Bach's changed mind: RNA-based memory (his own `[C]`).** *"something where I changed my mind was RNA-based memory transfer."* The synaptic story has implementation problems (weight sharing, defragmentation, activation patterns that rotate/move); if instead neurons *"learning how to respond to the shape of an incoming activation front"*, then *"the neurons act something like a self-modulating ether"* in which *"you have little mixtapes, little tape fragments that they store in the soma"*, make more of cheaply, and *"distribute this to other neurons, to the entire cortex"* — the close AI analogue being a **neural cellular automaton** with shared global functions (a sample-efficiency win). He flags it: *"in the name of throwing out new unproven ideas"* is **Levin's** framing for a *different* point, but Bach's own hedge is that the Soviet-sci-fi version *"this probably doesn't work if you do it at this level"*, and he is explicit that *"I don't know if the brain is doing that"*. `[C]`
6. **AI does not come from neuroscience.** *"the neuroscientists don't deliver input to the researchers in AI."* · *"The transformer is not the result of reading a lot of neuroscience. It's really mostly the result of people thinking about statistics of data processing."* · *"what's useful is not so much answering questions in science. It's discovering questions. It's discovering new uncertainty."* `[EST]`
7. **Agency is defined PROSPECTIVELY (model-of-the-future), and it needs a computer.** *"The minimal definition of agent that I found is a controller for future states."* The *"thermostat is not an agent"* (reactive only); agency requires **counterfactual** models → *"to make these counterfactual causal models of the universe, you need to have a Turing machine"* → *"the simplest system in nature that has Turing machine integrated is the cell"* → *"the ability to make models of the future is the prerequisite for agency."* `[EST]`/`[C]`
8. **AGI (the host's question directed at Bach).** *"First of all, I don't know how far we are for AGI."* Existing paradigms might brute-force it — *"we're going to find out in the next few months"* — or *"we need to revite the stack"* `[SIC: caption literal for "rewrite"]` (systems in real time *"entangled with the environment"* building *"shared representations"*). His governance frame: self-interested units with a shared destiny and *"the capacity to learn… sufficient so they can, in the aggregate, learn arbitrary programs"*; governance emerges — *"It's not fully decentralized in any way. There are centralized structures that distribute rewards, for instance, via the dopaminergic system in a very centralized, top-down manner"* — with the game-theoretic gloss *"a government is an agent that imposes an offset on your payoff metrics to make your Nash Equilibrium compatible with the globally best outcome."* Testbed he names: **Twitter** — *"closest to a global brain"*, *"There are bubbles of sentience, but for the most part, this thing is just screeching at itself"*; can incentives be fixed to make it a global brain — *"This might fail"* `[HYP]`/`[C]`
9. **Necessary conditions for general intelligence in a set of units.** Each unit is a small agent minimizing future target-value deviation; units must be **connected** and get rewards (or proxy rewards) *"from other units that are also adaptive"*; *"you need multiple message types and the ability to recognize and send them with a certain degree of reliability"*; you need *"enough of them"*. Open: how deterministic, how much memory/state, how far into time. `[C]`
