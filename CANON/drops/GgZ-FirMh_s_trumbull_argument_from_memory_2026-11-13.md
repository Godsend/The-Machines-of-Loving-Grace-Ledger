**The argument from memory, stated formally by the guest** *(the episode's title atom, and the thing the predecessor drop did not have in explicit form)* — [EST] as her stated position:
> *"The argument from memory is that when we look at our ability to remember, we find certain features of the human mind which make it um impossible to reduce this mind to matter. **What "soul" is doing in that sentence** — [EST]:
> *"which is called, at least by Augustine and Bergson, the two thinkers that I study, which is the soul. Or the spiritual force or activity behind the mind of man."*

**Its argumentative shape (her own hedge against a premise-chain reading)** — [HYP]:
> *"it's not an argument in the sense of, you know, we have premise one, premise two, premise three, therefore, you know, the human person has a non-physical element. Um but it's a different kind of argument. It's an argument which is sort of following different lines of facts or putting different pieces together."*

**Habit and image are distinct *in kind*** — the structural spine of her chapter 1 — [HYP]:
> *"we make this distinction between habit and image, which is Bergson's famous distinction which actually predates any notion in um can that we had notions that we have today in neurophysiology, for example, in psychology as the distinction between episodic and procedural or habitual memory."*
> *"habit and image are distinct in kind. And that when we're going to talk about recollection proper, we're not talking about habits or motor patterns. We're talking about this ability to remember a specific episode of your past, a specific moment which bears a date and which is unable to occur again."*
> *"we can take as many habits as we want, but we're not going to be able to build up something like an image from our childhood."*

**The "magical moment" — her sharpest single move, and NEW to the corpus** — [HYP]:
> *"How do you have a dynamic image be first stored in that in that method in the brain where biophysical quantities themselves are decaying rather rapidly? And then how do you then retrieve that experience and represent it before the mind? … they still have a kind of **magical moment** where aha, all of a sudden the image becomes a code or becomes physical and then aha, the code is capable of becoming an image again."*

**The second problem — continuity of experience** — [HYP]:
> *"if the brain is constantly breaking down perceptual scenes and then storing them for later retrieval, well how do you have the original continuity of the experience to begin with?"*

**The conclusion she draws from both** — [HYP], and the load-bearing negative claim:
> *"maybe it's it's it would be more helpful to stop this really simple tendency that we have to try to make the memory into a thing capable of being stored somewhere. And maybe we have to force ourselves to think that perhaps **memory is not something that's physically contained in space at all**. … instead it's more like an activity, a movement, an unfolding, a power, a tendency, which forces us to then think in terms of time."*

**The Kandel exchange — where she draws the experiment/philosophy line** — the cleanest epistemics in the episode. Kuhn supplies the premise; she grants it and relocates the burden — [EST] for Kuhn's relay, [HYP] for her response:

> Trumbull: *"No, it doesn't it doesn't depend on on on on those on those experiments being wrong and the things that we've discovered to be essential to the dynamics of memory supported by the brain. It doesn't depend on that being wrong. What it does depend on is the **metaphysical hypothesis** that therefore we've proven that memories are stored in the brain. … What it does depend on is us drawing a line between what's actually demonstrated in experimental cases and the philosophical conclusions or sometimes presuppositions that are brought to bear upon those cases."*
> *"We need to be able to say, 'This is where the experiment ends, and this is where the philosophizing begins.'"*

**Her concession to the substrate (important, and it tightens the predecessor's R2)** — [HYP]:

**Time, duration, and consciousness as temporal contraction** — [HYP]:
> *"consciousness in any meaningful sense of the word is never momentary. It's always sort of **stretched between these poles of the past and the present**. … we are not enchained in in the present."*

**Augustine, and the Manichaean-to-Neoplatonic turn** (biographical; her own framing) — [HYP]:
> *"Augustine's sort of first conversion uh before before his conversion to the Christian uh Christian faith was this conversion to philosophy, and this conversion to from Manichaeism, which was a very materialistic philosophy to a kind of Neoplatonic philosophy where all of a sudden, he's recognizing that there is this possibility of there being **dynamic, invisible realities, which are which aren't material**. > *"where I do think philosophy can lead is in something like the **probability of survival for a time** although the nature the meaning of that survival is necessarily going to remain out of its purview."*
> *"not argue for the survival of the soul as some a priori feature. We first define the soul as indestructible incorporeal **immutable** and all these other things like Plato does and then therefore the the soul never dies. … if you do something that looks more like based off lines of facts lines of evidence um then at least you can kind of shift the proof, the burden of proof, **shift the burden of proof** to those who deny the possibility of survival."*
