## 1. Bottom line

- [EST] **Paper 1's claim:** cognition in both natural and synthetic systems is characterized by **two equally important substrate-independent invariants**: (i) **remapping** of embedding spaces (creating/updating the internal latent representation — world-models), and (ii) **navigation** within those spaces via distributed error correction. Scale-free: cells, organisms, swarms, transformers, diffusion models, NCAs.
- [EST] **Paper 2's claim:** internal boundaries with Markov-blanket structure function as **classical information channels between components**; imaginative experience is generated on these inner screens, can be **internally generated but still surprising**, and varies across large state/trait variability in humans.
- [A] Together these give the house's two most load-bearing claims **published, peer-reviewed anchors**: (1) ledger-vs-inference (remap = write the latent/commit history; navigate = run inference in it); (2) GPU/GUI internal filter (inner screens = the render surface; aphantasia sits in the trait variability they name).

## 3. Verdict

- [A] **Strongest value:** Paper 1 gives a published, peer-reviewed, substrate-independent statement of the **remap vs navigate** distinction — the cleanest external phrasing of "ledger is built, inference runs." Paper 2 gives the internal-filter claim an explicit FEP treatment with a named variability axis (imagination trait variance) that the aphantasia lane can cite directly.
- [A] **Weakest joint / do not over-cite:** neither paper touches irreversibility, thermodynamic cost, or an append-only commitment semantics — the part of EILT that is *ours*. Borrow the invariants; keep the friction open (row 7).
- [A] **Outreach use:** cite Paper 1 (and possibly Paper 2) as [EST] primary support for substrate-independent cognition and inner-screen/attention models — with the row-7 caveat if the claim is about ledger semantics. The 2026-10-25 Fields talk drop is the companion piece (same lane; his constraint-first/no-go posture and Hoffman-Fence split).
