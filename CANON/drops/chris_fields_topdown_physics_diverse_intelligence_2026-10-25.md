## 2. Faithful summary, register-tagged

### 2a. The paper and the tower of spaces

- [EST] The talk is a re-contextualisation of a paper **"Embodied cognition, the free energy principle and the unity of science,"** stated as *"coming out hopefully by the end of this year in Mind and Matter"* (L2–8). No co-authors are named in the transcript. [EST] He says the paper *"sort of attacks the notion of physicalism"* (L8) and later that it is *"in many ways an argument for the 'who cares' answer to physicalism"* (L559–563).
- [HYP] **Platonic space vs math space** (L51–164): the math space requires well-defined, self-consistent, **finitely specifiable** contents; following Gödel (*"girdle"* ASR, L85–90) math is **only a piece** of the Platonic space. Current math is described by **category theory** (objects, morphisms, identity, associative composition) and functors (L99–131). He cites an old paper by **Goguen** arguing category theory is the natural language of CS (programs vs execution traces/semantics) (L144–158) [name corrected, [A]].
- [HYP] **Ingression is defined operationally, not mystically** (L184–195): the question is *"what processes from the math space do we expect to see implemented or ingressed into our observable physical space."* "Ingression" = the relation an abstract form bears to its physical realisation ("what do we expect to see satisfying this ingression relationship"). [A] This is **not** a claim that forms come from a mind-independent Platonic realm into matter — it is framed as a modelling question, but it does presuppose a **Platonic-realist** inventory of forms (see §5 friction).

### 2b. Top-down physics as a constraint structure

- [HYP] **Physics can be built top-down from four quantum-theory axioms** (L305–335): an isolated **universe**; degrees of freedom whose states *"encode information"* (he flags the scare quotes — *"basically undefined terms"*); an abstract universal-time parameter (*"not measurable by anybody"*); **unitary evolution** (*"conserves information"*), a conserved energy, and finite action (E·t).
- [HYP] **The "physical pattern space"** = patterns that are *information-conserving, energy-conserving and finitely acting*; because action is finite, every such pattern is *"fundamentally discrete"* (L340–356). [EST] He states explicitly it is a **strict inclusion** in the math space (continuous functions, non-conserving processes are excluded) (L347–359).
- [HYP] **Persistence = functional boundary = conditional independence** (L367–384): identity is modelled by *"separability… functional persistence of the systems boundary,"* which maintains **conditional independence** of system and environment. [EST] **The words "Markov blanket" are never used** — this is Fields' own separability/QRF framing, close in shape to a blanket but stated in quantum-reference-frame terms.
- [HYP] **A system cannot measure its own conditional independence** (L410–422): a no-go theorem *"no system can determine by measurement that it's conditionally independent from its environment."* He lists this among physics' **no-go theorems** as the tools that define the experiential (umwelt) space of any system.
- [HYP] **The umwelt is receiver-relative and resource-bounded** (L423–465): a system sees only what it can detect / compute / act on; temporal resolution is set by its clock and memory, which he ties explicitly to **"Mike's notion of a cognitive light cone"** (L437–445). [A] "Mike" = Michael Levin; this is the one direct cross-link Fields draws to Levin's programme.
- [HYP] **Intersubjective agreement is possible but conditional** (L495–517): it requires two agents to *"jointly manipulate their shared world and communicate in language"* — a **LOCC** protocol (*"local operation and classical communication"*, L510–512).

### 2c. Constraints, no-go theorems, and the Gell-Mann principle

- [HYP] **The mid-1960s turn** (L837–881): science's inherited questions *what exists* and *how does it work* gave way to a **negative-constraint** regime. He attributes the line **"anything not explicitly forbidden is mandatory"** to **Murray Gell-Mann** [name corrected, [A]] (L853–856), and notes he *"was wrong about that in some previous talk"* re: T. H. White's prior phrasing (L857–861) [self-correction by Fields, [EST] that he said it].
- [HYP] **Consequence:** *"if it's not impossible then you're going to see it happen somewhere for some amount of time"* (L868–870); the job is to prove **what isn't possible** (L870–873), which is why physics and quantum information theory are *"driven by no-go theorems"* (L874–881).
- [HYP] **The game is hard because language is slippery** (L882–941): Einstein's *spatial separation ⇒ separability* was falsified by Bell (L897–898) and the **Aspect experiment** (*"early 80s… first… rigorous test"*, L900–903) plus decades of refinements showing entanglement (*"across microscopic distances"* L904–909). [EST] He then says you can save Einstein by reinterpreting space's **topology**, identifying **entanglement with topological connectivity** — the idea he attributes to **Susskind and Maldacena, "over 10 years ago now"** (L911–937) [names corrected, [A]]. [A] This is the ER=EPR idea; the transcript does not use the term.
- [HYP] **The two readings are empirically indistinguishable** (L938–941): *"these two are not distinguishable empirically… one can prove that by any doable experiment."* [A] Consequence he draws: proving impossibility is hard partly because *"we're faced with this very loose language"* (L942–945).

### 2d. Diverse intelligence, the FEP, and LLMs

- [HYP] **Universality breaks the reductive hierarchy** (L777–831): *"agents all the way up as well as agents all the way down"*, and *"we're not used to thinking of ourselves as small components of very large cognitive agents, but we are in this picture"* (L826–831). [EST] He states the only way *"to get out from under being small components of large cognitive systems"* is to *"actually throw away our fundamental physics"* (L832–836).
- [HYP] **The FEP as a "universal psychology" / anthropic principle** (L660–703): invoking the **realist assumption** (observed patterns ↔ physical systems, L617–625) and then **Friston's free energy principle** (L669), he argues every system we can *see* is *"acting right now to preserve [its] boundary"* and is therefore an *"active inference agent"* (L680–683), a *"bayesian satisficer"* (L687–691). [EST] He labels this *"a universal psychology that applies to everything that we see… just in virtue of the fact that we see it"* — the **anthropic aspect** (L695–703).
- [EST] **E. coli as the test case** (L709–729): the *"diverse intelligence project is all about"* this; E. coli "likes sugar and doesn't like acid," resists foreign DNA but accepts swapped DNA — behaves *"the way we would expect an agent with a psychology to respond."*
- [HYP] **The LLM claim (his strongest move into current AI)** (L730–757): *"LLMs are very competent at things like manipulating human psychology… they clearly behave as psychological agents that have goals and understand a lot about us… they actually act like psychological agents that have theory of mind."* Conclusion: *"agentic behavior that's rooted in statistical analysis… being rooted in statistical analysis doesn't make it non-agential"* (L742–746) — *"and that's a good thing because our own uses of language are probably also rooted in statistical analysis"* (L746–751). [EST] He does note we don't *need* psychology language for *"thermodynamics exchange in rocks,"* but that *"doesn't mean… they're not active inference agents"* — just weak, slow predictors (L758–775).
- [HYP] **The reformalisation goal** (L1041–1096): develop a *"formal language… well-defined, self-consistent, finitely specifiable"* mapping Platonic/math morphisms to observable morphisms while respecting *"the capabilities of the observers"* (L1041–1052). [EST] He dismisses **John Lucas**-style "infinite capability of the human mind" arguments as *"completely ignoring resource constraints… off in fantasy land"* (L1053–1065). [EST] He proposes a **category-theoretic** ("commuting diagrams") route and says colleague **"Jim Gresbo"—Glazebrook [A]—** has *"been working on this question with me"*, going *"particularly to topos theory"* (L1072–1096).

## 4. The Hoffman check (Hoffman Fence)

[EST] **What the transcript literally says about Don Hoffman — three passages:**

1. **L471–473:** the umwelt / "experiential space" notion is *"shared by people as different as Carl Fristen and Don Hoffman."* → Fields places Hoffman and Friston **together on the interface/umwelt mechanism**.
2. **L531–544:** asks *"are experiential worlds physical?"* — *"you can argue no because the objects and processes… are all data structures and computations. And that's basically Don Hoffman's argument."* The "yes" answer is *"basically Carl Friston's argument."* Fields' own answer: *"an equally good answer is to say who cares"* (physicalism is poorly defined).
3. **L615–625:** *"making a realist assumption… that observed patterns correspond to physical systems. Now this is an assumption that people like Don Hoffman reject. But most of us make this and in fact it's hard to do science without assuming that things like detectors or voltmeters or computers [are] physical systems."*

[HYP] **Reading:** Fields **credits Hoffman's interface/umwelt mechanism** (passage 1 ≡ "take the rendering/interface seriously") but **explicitly rejects the conclusion Hoffman draws from it** (passages 2–3: he declines the "no, not physical" answer and defends the realist assumption as pragmatically necessary for science — and pragmatically sides nearer Friston's "yes" than Hoffman's "no"). He then refuses to fully endorse physicalism, landing instead at panpsychism (§2a, §1).

- [A] **This SUPPORTS the house's Hoffman Fence in both halves:** take the interface seriously, reject the realism-rejecting conclusion. Fields is an independent, on-record witness who splits exactly where the fence splits.
- [A] **…but it COMPLICATES it too:** Fields is *not* a house-style informational physicalist. He rejects physicalism as ill-defined and ends at **panpsychism**. So he corroborates the *fence line* without corroborating the house's *non-mystical informational footing*. [A] Cite him for "the fence's split is defensible," **not** for "an expert endorses our informational realism." Any use that blurs this is register-inflation.
