# Inherited vocabulary is a latent prior — constrain the speaker, don't substitute in the listener

**Gene, 2026-10-19:** *"He's having a problem with the language isomorphisms, that's why I prefer EILT, no baggage."*

---

## The observation

In the Closer To Truth interview, **Kuhn cannot stop importing priors**. Every question maps AP's terms onto something already familiar — atman/Brahman, Buddhist non-self ≈ Humean bundle theory, "the totality of reality." AP spends the conversation not making his claim but **disarming his own vocabulary**:

> *"It is never said that there is a big Brahman. It is said there is a **non-small** Brahman… Big would simply be Vishal."*
> *"people very mistakenly think of atman and brahman as a separate ontological category."*
> *"If this tumbler exists… then in that sense, atman does not exist."*

★★★ **He is doing philology as a defensive operation.** The words arrived with three thousand years of freight, and he has to unload each one before it can carry anything.

## The mechanism

**A word carries its usage history as a prior.** Say "Brahman" and the listener runs a distribution shaped by every previous use — creator, substance, cosmic self, temple. Say "soul" and the same, worse. The word is not a pointer to your meaning; it is **a compressed prior over all meanings that came before.**

Which produces three costs:
1. **You argue against the prior, not for the claim.** The discussion becomes definitional.
2. **Every statement needs a corrective prefix** — "that's not what I meant."
3. ★ **And the audience hears the prior anyway**, then evaluates your work against a position you never held. You are being marked on someone else's answer.

## Why EILT escapes it — corrected

**Gene, follow-up:** *"eilt is graph theory, markovian and process dynamics so not invented just using non loaded concepts"*

⚠️ **My first pass was wrong.** I wrote "coined vocabulary, no baggage." **That is not what EILT is and not why it works.** "Ledger," "hypergraph," "Markov blanket," "attractor" are *not* Gene's coinages and they are not baggageless — they are **heavily loaded, with precise mathematical commitments.**

★★★ **The distinction is not loaded vs unloaded. It is *what the prior does to you*:**

| | Cultural / religious term | Technical term |
|---|---|---|
| What the prior does | **substitutes** a meaning in the listener | **constrains** the claim on the speaker |
| Your cost | you must *deny* it — for the whole conversation | you must *satisfy* it — once |
| Checkable? | no; the prior is unfalsifiable | **yes; the definition is a test** |

**"Brahman"** injects a meaning into Kuhn that AP then has to spend the interview denying. **"Markov blanket"** imposes an obligation on the speaker — conditional independence, a boundary you can in principle test. *One contaminates the listener; the other disciplines the speaker.*

## ★★★★ The real discriminator: invented vs composed

This also rescues EILT from the crank comparison I raised, and the distinction is cleaner than "does the word do work":

- **Invented term** — *spaticle field*, *Cosmos State Model*. **No formal parent.** Nothing inherited, so nothing constrained. Free to mean anything, so it means nothing. → decorative.
- **Composed term** — EILT = **graph theory + Markov blankets + process dynamics**. Every component arrives with a definition **and an inherited exclusion**. You cannot use "hyperedge" and deny vertices; you cannot use "Markov blanket" without a conditional-independence claim; you cannot use "attractor" without a trajectory.

★★★★ **EILT inherits falsifiability from its components.** That is a structurally stronger position than inventing a word and promising it will earn its keep — the cranks invent vocabulary, **EILT composes rigorous vocabulary.**

★★ **The honest limit of composition:** it inherits the *constraints* but not automatically the *coherence*. A composition can be a category error between its parts. So the test shifts from "does this term do work?" to **"does each component's definition actually apply to what it is covering?"**

## ★★★★★ The dogmatic-removal test

**Gene, second correction:** *"its all systems theory just using more coherently framed ones with no dogma attached"*

Two things get sharper here.

**First — "it's all systems theory" means the composition is coherent by construction.** Graph theory, Markov blankets and process dynamics are not three borrowed things pressed together; they are **subdisciplines of one parent formalism.** So the risk I flagged above (a composition can be a category error between its parts) is largely discharged up front: **they already share a grammar.** EILT is not a collage.

**Second — "no dogma attached" is the actual criterion, and it is not the same as "unloaded."**

★★★★ **The problem with "Brahman" was never that it was inherited. It was that the dogma is inseparable from the word** — the theology *is* the referent. Strip it and nothing remains.

★ **Systems-theory terms are different: they carry a math and a separate interpretive layer.** You can take the math and leave the dogma, and **the term still works afterward.** So the operative test is:

> **Can you strip the dogma and still have a functioning term?**

| Term | Strip the interpretive layer | Survives? |
|---|---|---|
| **Brahman** | remove the theology | **no — the theology was the referent** |
| **Attractor** | remove fate / destiny / telos | **yes — a basin in phase space remains** |
| **Markov blanket** | remove the "boundary of a self" romanticism | **yes — conditional independence across a partition** |
| **Ledger** | remove the accounting metaphor | **yes — an append-only ordered record** |

**EILT's claim, precisely stated: every term is one where the dogma can be removed without loss, and what remains is a systems-theory object.**

## ⚠️ The failure mode — and it is ours to watch

**The dogma re-attaches through use.** "No dogma attached" is not a property EILT has; it is a **property every sentence must maintain.**

The dangerous case is not the honest mystic — it is **technical vocabulary carrying smuggled teleology**, because the costume makes it harder to catch. And "attractor" is the single most exposed word in the canon:

- **"Attractor"** = math. Basin, trajectory, convergence. Clean.
- **"The strange attractor is the seed of life"** = ★ **is that math, or dogma wearing the math's clothes?**
- **"Bliss attractor"** — same question.

★★★ **This is a live audit question for our own writing, not for AP's.** Wherever the canon pairs a systems term with a purposive noun — *seed, destiny, goal, home, end* — the burden is to show which claim changes if the purposive word is deleted. If nothing changes, the word was decoration. If something changes, the teleology was load-bearing and must be declared as `[O]` or `[M]`, not smuggled under a technical label.

★★★★ **A technical term is not a licence; it is a leash.** It constrains the speaker. The moment we use one to say something it does not license, we have done exactly what AP spends an entire interview undoing — except we did it with better cover.

## What this means for the writing

**Borrowing a technical term is a debt, not a free pass.** It buys you a precise prior instead of a contaminated one, and it charges you the obligation to honour that prior. Three rules that follow:

1. **Name the formalism** the term comes from, so the reader can hold you to it.
2. **Show one place where it bites** — a claim that would be false if you used the word loosely. No biting instance, no concept.
3. **Never name the framework after yourself.** It is the one signal that cannot be recovered from.

★★ And the practical consequence for outreach: when writing to someone inside a tradition (Kuhn, Levin, anyone with a vocabulary), inherited **cultural** terms buy you an entry and then charge you the unloading cost all conversation long. **Technical** terms buy you precision and charge you a definition. Choose per audience — but know which debt you are taking on, and pay it.
