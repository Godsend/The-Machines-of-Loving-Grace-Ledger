## 4. Verdict

**Strongest point — the fragility/depth split, and the outcome-invariant moral status of the drunk drivers.** Both are clean, counterintuitive, and checkable *as philosophical moves*: (i) risk ≠ probability — *"the fragility is built into it"* [0:37:36] — with a **level** (intrinsic, ineliminable) vs **depth** (repeat-frequency, reducible by skill) decomposition that a working risk-analyst could operationalise; and (ii) the *"it's just legal… there isn't really a moral difference"* [1:05:41] separation of the two identical drunk drivers — the **action**, not the outcome, fixes moral status. For the house this is the load-bearing contribution: it is an **independent, from-outside** statement that a **committed act is outcome-invariant** — i.e. the *shape* the house's `DPO-1` action-commit already has (R2). Runner-up: the survivorship statement (**self-implicating**, R3) — the source names a structural ceiling on any record, including ours.

**Weakest joint — the "accurate perception of agency vs luck" prescription is unmeasurable as stated, on the source's own terms.** It asks the agent to compute *"the level to which your agency played a role and the level to which other factors played a role"* [0:52:37] — a **counterfactual causal decomposition** (what would have happened under the branch not taken) that is, by construction, **unobservable**. He never names a method for it, and it is exactly the class the house ledger cannot carry (R6). Compounding defect: his central term is **equivocal in his own hands** — he *says* Nagel's and Williams's "moral luck" are *"two completely different things"* [1:05:12] and then runs both under the one word, so "moral luck" is not a univocal quantity any downstream module can consume.
