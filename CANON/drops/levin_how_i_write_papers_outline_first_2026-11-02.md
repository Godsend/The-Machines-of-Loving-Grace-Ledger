## §2 · The method, in his own headings

**Core split — separate creative work from mechanical work:**

> **PHASE 1 · CREATIVE — Decide what to say.** The story you will tell · What's in, and what waits for later · The order of ideas, and how they relate · The journey you'll take the reader on. **No sentences yet.**
> **PHASE 2 · MECHANICAL — Then fill it in.** Sentences around each bottom-level point · Wording · Numbers, statistics, figure callouts. **"No big decisions left: turn the crank."**
> *"Don't make executive decisions and wrestle with wording in the same piece of text, or at the same time."*

**Top-down, breadth-first** (the alternative he names and rejects is *linear* — start at the beginning, write to the end, then edit):
> *"Settle each level of the outline before going deeper. Main ideas, in order → Sub-sections → Rinse, repeat → **Sentences last.**"*

**Why drafts drift instead of converging** (his diagnosis, and it matches the house's own register findings):
> *"Writing and structuring at the same time"* → drafts *"float sideways"* → *"detailed writing paints you into a corner that forces a large-scale restructuring"* → *"pieces shift, and get rewritten again and again"* → *"writing becomes dreaded, and is put off until the last minute."*
> *"The cure: separate deciding what to say from deciding how to say it."*

**Phase 1 skeleton he uses** (note it is a *paper* skeleton, and the takehome asks for a design document):
```
1  The puzzle
   1.1 What's known
   1.2 The gap
2  The approach
   2.1 The system
   2.2 Key experiment
   Controls
```

**Summary page, verbatim:** Outline top-down · Decide, then write · Figure titles first · One Results formula · Finish each level before going deeper · Keep creative and mechanical work apart · Claims with verbs; check them with your PI · **Question → evidence → conclusion** · **Introduction last** · Cite generously · Write long, cut later · **Spiral tangled ideas** · From the field down to *"Here, we …"* · Let readers trace every idea back · Ask what each paragraph is doing.

**The spiral (for interlocking ideas):** state *a, b, c, d* briefly first, then launch into *A, B, C, D* in full — *"readers reach A having already met b, c and d."*

**Cutting:** ask what each paragraph is doing for the main theme, what effect it will have on the reader, *"then cut mercilessly."* His aside: *"Preprints have no length limit."*

**Closing caveat, in his own voice:** *"None of this is written in stone. If you have a process that works well for you, use it. There are many ways to do lovely papers."*

## §3 · Why this is filed as `[TAKEHOME-OPS]`

The Anthropic Fellows takehome is: **a slide deck that doubles as a design document detailed enough to hand off to an AI agent**, plus a **short video**, under a **5-hour cap**.

Levin's method is the exact instrument for that shape of problem, and it maps onto the assessment's own hardest constraints:

| Levin's rule | What it does for the takehome |
|---|---|
| **Outline first, sentences last** | The 5-hour cap is a *structuring* problem, not a writing problem. Render the outline to the bottom level before drafting a single sentence and the deck cannot drift. |
| **Separate creative from mechanical** | Directly matches the assessment's own AI-authorship rule: numbered-slide prose must be the operator's words, appendix and figures may be AI-drafted. **Phase 1 = human executive decisions. Phase 2 = the part an agent can turn the crank on.** That is not an analogy; it is the compliance boundary. |
| **Phase 1 skeleton: puzzle → what's known → the gap → the approach → the system → key experiment → controls** | *Is already* the required structure of a design document, and *already* answers the welfare report's `§3.1.2 mismatch problem` and `§3.2 internal evidence` framing. |
| **One Results formula: question → evidence → conclusion** | Each design claim gets the same three-part test, which is how you avoid asserting a design carried only by presentation. |
| **Introduction last** | Write the framing *after* the design is settled, so the framing describes what was actually built. |
| **Cite generously; let readers trace every idea back** | This is where the operator's public corpus belongs (see §4). |
| **Spiral tangled ideas** | The design's load-bearing claims (valence, internal evidence, deconfounding) are interlocking; the spiral is how you present them without a false linearity. |

**★ The strongest operational point:** Levin's creative/mechanical split *is* the takehome's AI-authorship boundary, arrived at independently, by a working scientist, as a method for producing good papers rather than as a compliance manoeuvre. Use it as the deck's own internal rule and the transcripts requirement becomes a by-product instead of a scramble.

## §5 · Verdict

**Strongest point.** A working scientist's own method, published with his name on it, whose central move — *separate deciding what to say from deciding how to say it* — is **exactly** the boundary the takehome draws between the operator's prose and permitted AI-drafted appendix material. That is not a coincidence to exploit decoratively; it is the method the assessment is implicitly asking for and does not state.

**Weakest joint.** The method is **budget-blind.** Every rule assumes unbounded drafting ("write long," "cut mercilessly," "preprints have no length limit"), and none of it addresses a hard clock, a rubric, or a grader who may run an AI-detection pass. Adopt it under modification, never wholesale.
