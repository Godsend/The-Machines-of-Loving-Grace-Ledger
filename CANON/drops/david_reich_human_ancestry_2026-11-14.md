## §4 — The mandatory friction row, expanded (R2, CRITICAL DIVERGENCE)

**The house's non-rolling ledger** (EILT §1/§6/§9, §18.8, §20) holds that a record, once written, is **not rewritable** and that this permanence is what makes the record *legible* and *checkable* — the ledger is *"the CHANNEL that makes a multi-system verifier possible,"* and its value is *"non-repudiation."*

**What the source shows** is a substrate that is **structurally append-only yet whose readable past is destroyed by ordinary events.** Mutations and admixture are permanent (`[EST]` mechanism); but Reich's central empirical claim is that the *trace* of a prior population in a place is reduced to ≤1% — *"homeopathic, diluted"* — and that continuity is *"interpolated"* and *"broken… by profound disruptions"* every few hundred to few thousand years. So a record can be **non-rollable and still illegible**: the record is not erased (so "non-repudiation" survives in the weak sense), but the *identity* it is supposed to preserve is displaced, not simply appended-to. *"It's really an untenable picture"* `[24:11]` — and Reich means the *purity* picture, but the same sentence is a warning about the *continuity* picture.

**Why it cuts at the house:** the house conflates three properties that the source separates — **(a) mechanism permanence**, **(b) semantic legibility**, **(c) identity continuity**. The source exhibits a real system with (a) firmly true, (b) statistically recoverable only against a model plus independent data (archaeology, linguistics), and (c) false. Any house claim that reads "the ledger is non-rolling" as entailing "what was there is still readable" inherits the gap. **This is the friction the Friction Rule demands: a mapping that breaks.**
