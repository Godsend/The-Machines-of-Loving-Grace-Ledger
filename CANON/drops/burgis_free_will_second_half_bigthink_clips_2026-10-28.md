## 2. Summary — what the clip (parent Chapter 2) actually argues

**Title audit: FAITHFUL.** *"Free will: the real casualty of the algorithm"* is the clip's own thesis, not marketing: Burgis takes Jack Dorsey's provocation and reframes the whole debate from **speech** to **will** — *"I think the the free speech debate is a complete distraction right now. I think the real debate should be about about free will."* (clip `2:14`) — and the clip then spends 30 minutes on **how the will is formed and thinned** (screens → thin rites of passage, curiositas, acedia; the crowd; the demonic). The opening teaser states it in one line: *"Social contagion is a phenomenon where we're caught up in emotions and ideas and even a sense of identity through a a process of contagion without actually understanding the response that we're giving to other people."* (clip `0:00`).

**The argument, in Burgis's order (clip times):**

1. **Free will, not free speech. [EST]** Dorsey's move, as Burgis reads it — *"this new power… to actually uh make a person's will um get lost into the power dynamics of the crowd"*; *"we are losing the ability to make our will our own. So, it's less free. You know, we are carrying out the desires of other people."* (`2:39`, `2:49`); and *"free speech is really just the outward expression of free will. We say what we will."* (`2:54`).
2. **Three thinkers describe one phenomenon. [EST, and note the names are the MANUAL track's, corrected]** Le Bon (*"This new power that Gustave Le Bon had recognized as important in the late 19th century, the power of the crowd… to actually uh make a person's will um get lost into the power dynamics of the crowd"*, `3:20`); Bowen (*"within a family the emotional system is so strong that we can get bound up with it to the point of losing a sense of self"*, `3:43`); Girard (*"mimesis, um this unconscious power of imitation, causes things to spread very quickly between people… in a knee-jerk way, not in an intentional way"*, `3:56`) → *"all of these things are really talking about the same phenomenon"*, social contagion (`4:0x`).
3. **Dunbar is the boundary condition. [EST]** *"Robin Dunbar is famous for Dunbar's number, which is 150"* — the **maximum** of real stable relationships, *not* an optimum (`5:09`); and the reframe — *"maybe we can have relationships with a thousand friends on the internet as a pseudo self"* — is the only way to exceed 150, and it is exhausting *"the kind of self that's constantly negotiating itself in real time"* (`5:41`). The password is in the small group: *"identify the seven or eight, and invest in those"* (`~7:2x`).
4. **The three temptations of the screen. [EST for the frame; the sources are patristic]** *"The three temptations are rites of passage that are thinner than they used to be"* (`8:28`) · *"The second is what Augustine of Hippo called curiositas"* (`8:37`) · *"the third temptation is accidie… a spiritual disease that was named by monks in the 4th and 5th century, which was an extreme form of restlessness of mind and soul"* (`8:53`, `9:00`).
5. **Thin rites of passage. [EST]** A rite of passage is *"the process through which a person uh does something that's usually hard and risky and they usually have to do it alone"*, *"it's the kind of thing that forms you know, character"*; it always has separation → liminal → reintegration; the substitutes have migrated online and *"don't necessarily go into the marrow or into the bone cuz they're They make everything a little bit easier. There's less friction. And a good rite of passage does have some friction."* (`10:44`).
6. **Curiositas: knowledge without a telos. [EST as the Augustinian concept]** *"Curiositas for Augustine was, you know, what lust is to the body, curiositas is to the mind."* (`11:11`) — *"a lust or thirst for unqualified knowledge. Like as if knowing more itself is the goal."* (`11:28`) — *"there's no teleology of the knowledge. Like, where is it heading? Right? What is the goal of the knowledge?"* (`12:18`). Its opposite is **studiositas**: *"Studiosity is the alternative or the opposite of curiositas… knowledge that actually is directed at something… towards some actual good"* (`13:16`).
7. **Acedia: restlessness, not sloth. [EST]** *"Acedia is kind of the disease of the mind and the soul according to Evagrius Ponticus"*; *"sloth is a really bad translation"* (`14:37`, `14:58`); it is the monk *"want to be anywhere except the place that they were at"*; and it is the age's *"fundamental disease"* — *"This restlessness of mind and soul is the fundamental disease of our time that technology is affecting. It has made accidie widespread and socially contagious."* (`16:49`, `16:56`). Causally: **acedia → curiositas → thin rites of passage** (*"accidie leads to this curiositas, this disordered desire for knowledge that doesn't necessarily matter. And then that leads to these counterfeit or thin rites of passage"*, `17:03`).
8. **The two selves, reprised and given a context theory. [EST]** The pseudo self is *"negotiable in real time"*; the solid self *"more intentionally formed"* — *"use the word I"*; and Bowen's **functional differentiation**: *"we can go to work and operate as a solid self. And then we can go into another environment with our old friends from high school and turn into a pseudo self."* (`17:34`, `23:34`). The modern world multiplies contexts (a "thousand different online subcommunities"; *"the average person has way more than eight or nine jobs in their lifetime"*, `24:31`) until *"we can find ourselves just negotiating our sense of self with each of them in real time"* (`25:22`) — the **adaptation trap**: *"we are rewarded for how well we can adapt and we're usually punished for not adapting"* (`25:28`).
9. **McLuhan + Aquinas: the senses are being scrambled. [EST for the sources]** *"any form of technology is kind of an extension of the human person"* (McLuhan, `18:43`) — it extends one sense and diminishes others; Aquinas's **sensus communis** = *"all of our sensory faculties working together to help us perceive reality as it is"* (`19:33`); its worked case is **Jacques Lusseyran** (blind French Resistance leader, `20:44`), and its modern analogue **synesthesia** (`21:58`) — the senses *"converge to give us kind of a unified sense of a thing or the truth of a thing"*.
10. **The diagnosis and the prescription. [EST diagnosis / HYP prescription]** *"the danger today is not that we're uncomfortable, it's that we're too comfortable"* (`26:12`); we must de-familiarise — *"make the familiar strange again in order to see it for what it really is"* (`26:47`).
11. **The demonic. [POETIC — his own theological vocabulary, explicitly flagged as such in the source]** *"that word is demonic"* (`26:59`) — *"demonic as something that acts outside of ourselves that affects or even takes over our will or ability to act"* (`27:35`); *"there is something demonic about social contagion"* (`28:03`); the Biblical **legion** (Mark 5 / Luke 8): *"the demonic is legion. Legion meaning many… the many can affect the one to the point where the one loses agency, loses the ability to act in one's own name"* (`28:40`, `28:47`); and the closing line: *"the will is subtly eroded until we think we're making decisions on our own, but we're not."* (`29:09`)
