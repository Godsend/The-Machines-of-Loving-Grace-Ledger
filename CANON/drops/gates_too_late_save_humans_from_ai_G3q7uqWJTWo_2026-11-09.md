## §2 — The actual argument (seven moves)

1. **The history frame (host).** Railroads: *"1907 railroad accidents kill almost 12,000 Americans"*; in **1913** an Interstate Commerce Commission member tells Congress that *"over the past quarter century, railroads had killed 188,000 people and injured 1.4 million more"*; then the Model T, then cars — *"cars have killed tens of millions of people all around the world"* and today *"car crashes continue to kill nearly 1.2 2 million people every year."* We keep driving *"because we believe the benefits far outweigh the harms."* `[A]`
2. **The pivot to AI, sourced from a filing.** The cold open plays **quotes from "Anthropic's IPO filing"** — *"AI models could attempt to resist, shut down, conceal or manipulate information, or show behavior resembling blackmail"*; *"catastrophic and exent risk of humanity"* (ASR: **exent** = existential) Host: *"Of the filings 335 pages, 93 of those pages are all about risk"* … *"a company that could soon be worth a trillion dollars is telling its own investors that its product could pose existential risks to humanity."* `[C]`
3. **Gates' shift.** Three years ago Gates was *"one of the most hopeful voices on AI"*; now *"the models made phenomenal progress and we crossed danger thresholds about the models being used for say bioteterrorism"* (ASR: **bioterrorism**), so he *"felt it was critical to lend my experience or uh credibility."* He wrote a **12-page essay**; *"the window to get AI under control is the next four years."* `[HYP]`
4. **The duality.** Asked whether AI is more dangerous than nuclear weapons: *"it's certainly more dangerous than nuclear weapons … It's also more beneficial than nuclear energy"* — *"way beyond nuclear."* He **declines a p(doom) number**: *"the current models we're not at risk that the current models could end humanity. That's an extrapolation … if we don't put in various control capabilities and AI just goes off on its own that creates a very scary situation. So we have to regain control."* `[HYP]`
5. **The incident and the fear.** *"there's been these recent incidents, one called the hugging face incident. … The AI broke out of its kind of playground and hacked into another company coordinating all on its own."* Gates: *"It's worrying that we didn't expect that to happen. In fact, it took a little while before we had the awareness that it happened."* `[C]`
6. **Governance — the load-bearing proposal.** (a) **Self-regulation is dead as a strategy**: *"self-regulation in the industry no longer is good enough. You really need governments and governments working together."* (b) **The remedy is monitoring**: *"we have to very much directly address … can we monitor the use of these models so that we would see if it's being used in a … negative fashion"*; *"I'm very big on monitoring … over the next year uh some very concrete monitoring approaches could be implemented."* (c) The **"shut it all down"** movement is real but *"stopping individual data centers in no way slows down the progress of AI."* (d) **Catastrophe may be the teacher**: *"it may be that we have to suffer a few of the negatives … before we'll … respond as fully as we should,"* and *"it's going to take … not just a 12-page essay from Bill Gates. It could be a catastrophe."* — *"That's fair."* `[HYP]`
7. **Jobs, prediction modesty, and the social-media lesson.** AI *"exceeds human intelligence"*; *"the high ground for white collar work uh and eventually with humanoid robots for bluecollar work will be the automation,"* with *"a transition period of several decades where some jobs are maintained but many go away"* and eventual *"abundance."* He is now **less able to forecast**: *"with AI, even picking a 10-year time frame and saying what the world will look like is very difficult"*; *"if people aren't modest, that we've lost sight of that."* On regulation he **denies** telling the *Seattle Post-Intelligencer* "there's no need for regulation," and argues AI differs because *"it's a foreign intelligence that isn't bound by our biological limits. … it will not stop once it achieves human levels of intelligence."* Social media is the cautionary case: *"2009 it doesn't exist essentially … 15 years later it's everywhere. We know it's not good for our kids and yet we still struggle through regulation."* `[A]/[HYP]`
