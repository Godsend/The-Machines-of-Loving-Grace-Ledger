## 1. Core Thesis: Emotions as "Measure & Motivate"
Robert Kurzban (evolutionary psychologist, author of *Why Everyone (Else) Is a Hypocrite* and *All the Feels*) and David Eagleman (Stanford neuroscientist, author of *Incognito*) lay out an algorithmic, computational taxonomy of human emotions:

1. **Every Emotion is a Dual Function:**
   - **Measure:** An internal/external sensor estimating a specific fitness-critical variable (blood $CO_2$, caloric deficit, relative social status, threat to bond).
   - **Motivate:** An action policy or behavioral recruitment program designed to shift that variable in ancestral environments.
2. **The Common Currency Problem:**
   - The body tracks thousands of incommensurate variables (hydration, social status, physical danger, mating opportunity).
   - Because physical behavior is mutually exclusive (you cannot simultaneously flee a predator, sleep, eat, and court a mate), the brain must convert multi-dimensional measurements into a **single common currency of valence and urgency**.
3. **The Pandemonium Architecture of Mind:**
   - Consciousness is not a unified executive CEO. It is an internal **bidding market** (Selfridge's *Pandemonium architecture*).
   - Diverse subconscious sub-networks campaign for control of the serial bottleneck (the organism's physical body).
   - The sub-network generating the highest emotional intensity wins the bid, capturing conscious attention and driving action.

## 2. Direct Intersections with EILT & Wetware Polycomputing

### A. The "Dashboard / Gauge" is Literally the GUI
Eagleman’s closing synthesis is word-for-word our **Internal Filter (GUI) Hypothesis**:
- Under the hood, massive parallel posterior networks ("the GPU") compute blood gases, face geometry, social hierarchies, and threat probabilities at millions of bits per second.
- The prefrontal serial conscious bottleneck ("the GUI", ~50 bits/s) cannot inspect the raw weights or firing rates.
- Instead, the backend compresses the entire computation into a single **gauge on the dashboard**—a *feeling* (anger, hunger, jealousy, awe).

### B. Why "Mean / Blunt" Signals Work Better Than High-Resolution Reasoning
- Eagleman and Kurzban explain why emotions feel so visceral, blunt, and non-negotiable:
- In high-stakes ancestral environments, nuanced deliberation at the GUI level is too slow.
- Emotional intensity acts as a **hard interrupts line**, seizing the executive bottleneck to force immediate action.
