## 1. What Friston actually says (his weighting, not the title's)

**[EST] Perception as prediction from the top down.** The thesis the title gestures at, in his words: the brain is *"a very constructive organ. It's an organ that generates predictions, hypotheses, fantasies, explanations that best explain the sensory impressions on our sensory organs"* `[74.1]`; the lineage is *"the days of Plato"* and, *"most beautifully articulated by Helmholtz in terms of the notion of unconscious inference"* `[60.2]`; and the modern framing is *"inside-out, a very constructive, active process of generating predictions as opposed to an outside-in process of passively absorbing data"* `[93.0]` — which he credits to *"people like Anil Seth and Andy Clark"* `[89.2]`.

**[EST] Predictive coding, plainly.** *"the imperative to minimize one's prediction errors, or more generally, the mismatch between what we predict should happen … and the sensory evidence that our predictions are correct"* `[126.8]`; and *"Your brain cells can literally subtract what's coming in and what you thought should be coming in, and then the difference is the news worthy information"* `[171.9]`.

**[EST] Why the inversion — the brain is skull-bound.** *"the problem it solves is the fact that we do not have access to the causes of our sensations"* `[221.4]`; *"the brain is skull bound. It lives in the dark."* `[230.4]`; and the scene-construction example: *"the way that we actually look at the world is by palpating it every 250 milliseconds, four times a second"* `[252.2]` (see §2 — flagged ASR). Dennett's label is quoted: *"Daniel Dennett calls this a strange inversion."* `[216.4]`.

**[EST] ★ "Controlled hallucination" — REFUSED, replaced by entrainment.** The host offers the slogan; Friston quotes it — *"this is captured in this phrase that perception is a controlled hallucination"* `[402.8]` — then **pushes back on the word "control":** *"the control, I think … gives a false impression. It's not that reality is controlling what's going on on the inside. This is genuinely a balanced dance."* `[415.7]`; **"I would replace control with entrainment."** `[446.8]`. His support is **Huygens**: *"Huygens' clocks … two clocks suspended from the same wall would … eventually come to swing in synchrony. And this is called generalized synchrony or synchronization of chaos."* `[451.8]`–`[479.7]`. The mechanism statement — the row §3 R2 turns on — is: *"the only … free energy minimizing solution for something that is loosely coupled to something else is this entrainment and synchronization."* `[484.6]`.

**[EST] Metzinger on transparency.** For the phenomenology he defers to *"people like Thomas Metzinger where there's different kinds of sense making … that can be opaque or transparent"* `[592.4]`; the capacity to *"go beyond transparent sensation … and actually know that you are looking at something"* `[620.4]` is *"an ability to actually act upon and render that which was transparent now opaque"* `[747.2]`. This is what gives the brain *"a kind of action on the inside"* `[721.2]`, and read by a psychologist, in his words, *"if you're a psychologist it'd be attention"* `[689.0]`.

**[EST] The two roads; FEP as least action.** He separates *"a sort of low road to understanding sense making"* `[774.4]` from *"the high road … a physicist would take"* `[780.2]`. On the high road: *"any system, including particularly the brain, is in the game of minimizing its free energy, where free energy is now read as this prediction error or surprise."* `[798.4]`. Mathematically *"it's just called the principle of least action"* `[806.0]`. The unification is stated as the point: *"two ways that I can do that"* — *"change my mind"* (perception) `[848.5]` or the *"active way"* (sampling different data) `[860.6]` — giving *"a seamless … unitary explanation for both action and perception"* `[884.1]`.

**[EST] Homeostasis and expected utility as the same phenomenon.** *"If I was a physiologist, it would just homeostasis."* `[909.3]` `[ASR: dropped "be" — see §2]`; *"exactly the same phenomena can be applied to physiology and homeostasis. You could even now apply to economics."* `[933.2]`; *"things like expected utility theory is just an expression of … acting upon the world in order to realize my predictions"* `[941.5]`.

**[EST] Range: "turtles all the way down", then a scale window.** General frame: *"it's turtles all the way down. So, as soon as you can disambiguate anything from its milieu … you … induce technically what's clinically called a Markov blanket, a boundary that separates me from everything that's not me."* `[975.6]`–`[990.7]`. He explicitly guards it: *"it's not saying that … existing means that I am perceiving and acting, but what it does allow you to say is that in existing as separate from my environment … I am licensed to describe this thing as engaging in a free energy minimizing process"* `[1007.1]` — *so it applies to anything, "if you've got a name for it"* `[1042.4]`. He then imposes a **window**: at the small end *"things like quantum mechanics would no longer apply"* `[1058.8]`, at the large end *"not so large that conservative dynamics and … the motion of the heavenly bodies, say, kick in"* `[1063.0]`; the interesting behaviour lives at *"this sort of intermediate scale"* `[1071.9]`.

**[EST] ★ Planning, and the thermostat.** When the interface to the world grows *"so removed from the inside of reasonably big things"* that *"action now becomes a cause of my sensation"* `[1094.8]`–`[1103.0]`, the system is *"actually inferring their own actions."* `[1120.1]`. That yields: *"planning is inference, inferring what is the most likely thing I am doing and what should I commit to"* `[1137.2]`; and *"planning would be definitive of the kinds of things that could possibly … be … called … a true agent."* `[1171.8]`–`[1178.6]`. Dreaming removes accuracy: *"if I had the opportunity to remove all the sensory data, I can take accuracy off the table … I can still minimize free energy … by minimizing complexity."* `[1463.7]`, and that entails *"getting rid of all the redundant associations … the wiring in our brains that I don't need"* `[1483.8]`, *"that is basically the argument for sleep for dreaming"* `[1508.0]` — *"a house cleaning that minimizes the complexity"* `[1574.2]`. The imperative he draws: *"to become wise and focused and sparse and non-redundant"* `[1448.2]`.

**[EST] Illusions cannot be talked out of.** *"even if you tell somebody … that's not going to make any difference."* `[1664.7]`; *"declarative knowledge … will not be able to penetrate these mechanisms."* `[1688.7]`; *"it's wonderful that you can't talk somebody out of an illusion … This is your reality and this is a gift not only of evolution, but all neurodevelopment."* `[1698.0]`.

**[EST] Psychiatry as false inference.** *"you can now read psychopathology as false inference … what … is basically bad belief updating."* `[1755.5]`. The two ways to be wrong: *"type one and type two errors"* `[1777.9]` — *"infer something is … there when it's not"* (hallucinations and delusions) vs *"inferring something is not there when it is"* (hysteria / dissociative / neglect) `[1781.2]`–`[1801.1]`.
