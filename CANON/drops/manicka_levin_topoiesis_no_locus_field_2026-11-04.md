## §1 · What the paper claims

**The empirical anchor (prior work, not theirs):** *Xenopus laevis* embryos survive teratogenic stress at significantly higher rates **in larger groups** — cross-embryo morphogenetic assistance (**CEMA**), Tung, Sperry et al. 2024.

**The proposed mechanism — "topoiesis":** a **constitutively secreted stress signal** (extracellular **ATP or adenosine**) that **diffuses and decays across the group**, accumulating more in larger groups **"where interior volume outpaces absorbing surface."**

That single clause is the whole idea. **The field is a geometric consequence, not a cellular one.** No cell stores it, no cell owns it; concentration is set by the ratio of volume to absorbing boundary.

**Results they report:**
- The rising field **carries more embryos to survival** and **demarcates the collective more sharply** from its surroundings as screening strength grows with size.
- **Intermediate sizes:** spatially organized — *"a rescued interior enclosed by a failing periphery"* — and it **reconstitutes after shuffling.**
- **Large groups:** the field restructures — peak concentration keeps rising while the interior gradient **"flattens into a near-uniform bulk bounded by a sharp rim, sharpening demarcation while blurring distinctions among interior members."**
- The pre-pattern **stabilizes well before individual fate commitment** — there is a window in which collective geometry acts on survival outcome.
- A biophysically calibrated minimal model **recapitulates CEMA rescue rates across all six group sizes (N=1–306; 0%–92%)** and the size-dependent craniofacial defect rate among survivors.
- **Prediction:** sealing a group's boundary against loss — *not* changing its size, *not* changing its biochemical regulation — **moves mean rescue from 0% to 100%** and reorganizes the rescue structure.

**Their conclusion:** *"diffusion and geometry alone may suffice to make a collective both spatially organized and controllable through **macroscale intervention rather than microscale engineering of its members.**"*

This is the sharpest attack on the house position yet, sharper than `F-LEV-SALIENCY`, because it does not contest *what* persists. **It shows that nothing needs to persist.**

You get **a coordinated, spatially organised, individually-demarcated collective**, which **reconstitutes after being shuffled**, and which is **controllable by moving one boundary** — from **diffusion and a decay constant.** No record. No representation. No memory. No bearer of history.

## §4 · What it strengthens for us

Two things, and they are real:

1. **Collective-level properties that no member has.** The field exists only at the group scale; no embryo carries it. That is the anti-reductionist claim, formalised, with a rescue curve.
2. **"Macroscale intervention rather than microscale engineering of its members."** Sealing a boundary moves rescue 0% → 100%. If that generalises, **the correct place to intervene in a multi-agent system is its boundary, not its agents** — which is an architectural claim the house can use, and it favours our *boundary* commitments over our *per-member* ones.

## §5 · What survives as a testable question

Addendum B was "does the variable have a locus?" That question is now answered *yes in this model, it does not*. The residual, sharper question:

**Which regime binds where?** The 2024 model needs local storage + transport to coordinate; the 2026 model needs neither. Both use "stress." They differ in what stress *is*.

**Proposed replacement test:** in a system exhibiting CEMA-like collective rescue, **ablate the secretory channel** (block constitutive release/decay) while **preserving per-cell error sensing**. If rescue survives, the field mechanism is not load-bearing and locality is doing the work. If rescue collapses while cells still sense their own error, **the coordination was never in the cells** — and the ledger's demand for a per-member witness is wrong at the root.

That is a clean perturbation, it uses their own system, and it can come out in the house's favour.

## §6 · Honest limits

- **Not peer reviewed.** Every number above is the authors' abstract; none is audited here. The recapitulation across N=1–306 (0%–92%) is *their* fit — a calibrated minimal model reproducing its own target data is weaker evidence than an out-of-sample prediction, and only the *sealing-the-boundary* result reads as a genuine forward prediction.
- The mechanism is specific: **extracellular ATP/adenosine**. Whether the finding generalises to other collectives (including artificial ones) is untested and is being asserted by analogy if we use it.
- 2024 (binary, local, transport) and 2026 (field, non-local, geometry) are **two models from the same lab eighteen months apart.** That is a group revising its own ontology — worth noting as a strength of theirs and as a reason not to over-read either paper as settled.
- The house's own §3 *"Stress Is All You Need"* is now under pressure from **three** directions: Barrett derives stress from prediction (ordering inversion), Shreesha–Levin treat stress as a scalar error signal, and Manicka–Levin treat it as a **geometric field**. Three incompatible ontologies of the same word, all from credible sources.

## §7 · Verdict

**Strongest point of the source.** *"Diffusion and geometry alone may suffice."* A genuinely minimal mechanism producing collective individuation, collective control, and shuffle-invariance — with a forward prediction (seal the boundary, 0%→100%) that is falsifiable and would be remarkable if it holds.

**Weakest joint.** A calibrated minimal model reproducing its own observational targets, in an unrefereed preprint, on one organism. The "blurring distinctions among interior members" claim is qualitative in the abstract and should not be repeated until the figures are read.
