## 2. The actual technical claims — per talk, with sim-vs-experiment preserved

**The single distinction the brief demands is preserved in every row: SIMULATION (a code computed something) vs EXPERIMENT (a device or sample measured something).** This symposium is heavily **simulation-weighted**; the device work is **prototype-scale**.

### 2.1 Dylan Schmelling `[ASR?]` — CSX: non-planar HTS magnets — **EXPERIMENT**
- **What it is:** **CSX = the Columbia Stellarator eXperiment**, a **quasi-axis-symmetric (QA) HTS upgrade of the existing CNT** stellarator at Columbia; keeps most hardware, replaces the two interlocked magnets with optimised HTS coils. `[QUOTE,L10-15]` "So CSX is going to be the quasi-axis symmetric high-temperature superconducting upgrade of an existing Stellarator at Columbia, that's CNT." `[VERIFIED]` against arXiv:2510.07042 ("Experimental Results from Early Non-Planar NI-HTS Magnet Prototypes for the Columbia Stellarator eXperiment (CSX)") and PPPL summer-school notes on CNT (four circular planar coils, interlocked).
- **Objective:** better confinement from QA symmetry + strong fields; and it is explicitly an **engineering** program: *"3D printing out of metal or non-planar coils, winding HTS onto non-planar coils due to the fragility of HTS… cooling the magnets"* `[QUOTE,L18-22]`.
- **Result (EXPERIMENT):** *"So far, we have built and tested three prototypes."* `[QUOTE,L25]` The second-generation low-field non-planar magnet ran *"40 turns of HTS… we operated this fairly successfully"* and finished testing last summer `[QUOTE,L31-34]`. The high-field prototype: *"we have not been able to operate this up to the full specification"* `[QUOTE,L41-42]`, reaching *"up to 100 amps… without any major problems"* `[QUOTE,L43-44]`, expecting *"about half a tesla on axis"* `[QUOTE,L38]`.
- **Timeline, in his words:** full-scale magnets *"sometime next year"* `[QUOTE,L71-72]`. **This is prototype-scale hardware, not a plasma result** — no plasma has been produced by this programme yet.

### 2.2 Rohan `[ASR?]` — engineering-informed coil-error modelling — **SIMULATION**
- Realistic errors (manufacturing, winding, insulation) fed through **SIMSOPT** `[they say "SEMSOPT", L293]`: "hundreds of random perturbations" `[QUOTE,L83-85]`, plotting **coil displacement vs quasi-symmetry-error**.
- **Finding:** two coil sets with *"essentially the same maximum coil displacement… have very different quality symmetry error"* → **"we need to care a lot about our spatial positioning of the coils"** `[QUOTE,L89-93]`. ### 2.3 Shreyas Sitala `[ASR?]` — stellarator coil optimisation — **SIMULATION (method claim)**
- Distinguishes tokamak (plasma current spins the plasma) from stellarator (complex coils twist the field) `[QUOTE,L132-136]`; the design problem is a **trade-off between coil simplicity and plasma quality** `[QUOTE,L143-150]`.
- **The method claim:** direct filament/coil optimisation is *"a local optimisation that can get stuck in some local minima"* `[QUOTE,L161-163]`; his alternative bunches a **surface current density** `[QUOTE,L164]` and is *"guaranteed to hit a global minimum"* `[QUOTE,L166]`, and is *"100 times faster or even a thousand times faster than the previous method"* `[QUOTE,L168-169]`. **This is a simulation-methodology claim about an optimiser, not a device result, and it is not independently corroborated in this source beyond SIMSOPT's existence as the house framework (Landreman et al. 2021).** `[EST]`

### 2.4 Daniel Burgess — tearing-mode onset — **SIMULATION validated against EXPERIMENT**
- Magnetic islands in tokamaks grow non-linearly, flush confinement, *"grow to such sizes that it actually locks with the wall of the device and leads to a disruption"* `[QUOTE,L181]`.
- **Hybrid approach:** splice expensive resistive physics in only at the rational/resonant surfaces, ideal solution everywhere else → *"a much more computationally tractable problem"* `[QUOTE,L196-205]`.
- **Result (the cleanest sim-vs-experiment correspondence in the set):** *"we've got discharges here that the hybrid model… is predicting are going to flip unstable"*; the growth rate *"flip[s] unstable right before the onset… of the actual measured tearing mode"* `[QUOTE,L210-214]`; **matched same-day/same-session discharges that did *not* tear are shown stable by the model** `[QUOTE,L215-218]`; uncertainty quantification (code "Bouquet") *"survives the error bar propagation"* `[QUOTE,L219-222]`. **DIII-D validation; appears in a data-validation phase, not stated as published.**

### 2.5 Sophia `[ASR?]` — coupled plasma + breeding-blanket dynamics — **SIMULATION**
- **The blanket is the tritium factory:** *"a blanket which is adjacent to the plasma. And its primary purpose is to breed tritium that you can feed back into the fusion reaction… And in a lot of cases, we're not quite sure what this blanket is going to look like"* `[QUOTE,L229-231]`. **This is the same bottleneck Cowley named *"that part we haven't done very much on at all"* — from the design side, and the uncertainty is stated by the researcher.**
- Concepts = electrically conducting fluids (liquid metals or molten salts) that respond to the strong background field; magnetohydrodynamic drag on pumping `[QUOTE,L232-236]`. **The interaction she studies is less-studied:** a plasma disruption changes the plasma's own field, inducing currents and flows in the blanket `[QUOTE,L237-240]`.
- **Quantitative SIMULATION results** `[QUOTE,L247-250]`: vertical instability can induce blanket flows *"up to about three meters per second"* and *"pressure variations up to almost a megapascal."* Two-way coupling matters: *"if you pretend the blanket is a solid and do not account for this fluid motion, you might actually over predict how stabilizing the blanket is for the plasma"* `[QUOTE,L252-257]`.   3. **ARC: *"a fusion power plant planned for the early 2030s in which the REMC would fulfill its full potential, which is really to prevent downtime"*** `[QUOTE,L295-296]`.

### 2.9 Elliot Felsky `[ASR?]` — cryogenic pellet fuelling — **EXPERIMENT (test stand)**

### 2.11 Thaddeus Kiker `[ASR?]` — neutron shielding for stellarators + spin-polarised fusion — **SIMULATION**
- **Stellarator-specific neutronics problem:** *"non-constant radial offset shielding… at every single profile… a different distance between the plasma and the coils"*, which *"set[s] constraints at particular points around your reactor where you want to add potentially more shielding"* `[QUOTE,L491-498]`.
- **Machinery built:** a stellarator-specific neutron source in **OpenMC** + automation of non-constant-radial-offset shielding, plus a **spin-polarised kernel** `[QUOTE,L501-503]`; speed work (single field period, tri-linear field grid vs analytic) `[QUOTE,L542-546]`.
- **The physics claim:** polarising fuel ions relative to the local field lets you *"redirect and steer where you want that neutron loading to go"* `[QUOTE,L506-509]`; validated kernel → *"when you polarize the ions… perpendicularly to the local magnetic fields, you produce **up to 50% more fusion power**, and… in the case of the variant of the parallel polarization, you're able to significantly reduce the center loading stack"* `[QUOTE,L551-553]`; source now *"a public pull request on OpenMC"* `[QUOTE,L549-550]`.
- **`[EST]` independent check:** the "≈50% more" figure is **real, established physics** — spin-polarised D–T raises the fusion cross-section by **a factor ≈1.5** (e.g. arXiv:2108.01211; Frontiers in Physics 2024; arXiv:2502.15941). **So he is reporting a known theoretical cross-section enhancement inside a simulation kernel — NOT an energy gain, NOT a device result.** `[VERIFIED]`
