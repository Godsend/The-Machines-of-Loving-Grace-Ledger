# Who (verified)
**Dr Timnit Gebru** — former co-lead, Google Ethical AI; fired Dec 2020 after Google demanded she retract or de-anonymize *Stochastic Parrots* (with Emily M. Bender); founder, **DAIR** (Distributed AI Research Institute); co-founder, Black in AI; forthcoming book *Deep Unlearning: The Rise of AI and the Radicalization of a Tech Idealist*. Host: Dr Myriam François (The Tea).

# The core thesis, in her own words

> *"everybody else that I'm seeing is pontificating on the wrong thing — which is not just a distraction. Actually **it's a strategy**. It's not merely a distraction to pontificate about super intelligent machines or machines that have their own agency ... because it's a way to forget that there's a company building these machines and profiting from them and there are people making decisions."*

> *"I'm not concerned about rogue models destroying humanity. I'm concerned about **the one-dimensional homogeneous group of men pursuing this technology and warning about rogue models**."* (Says she wrote this in a 2015 open letter at OpenAI's founding.)

> *"The overlap [with Jacob Coxon's concerns] is **zero**... My concerns are about people like him actually, who are extremely dangerous."*

On the 2020 fight: Google said take your name off the paper or retract; she refused without conditions; fired. Notes GPT-2's predecessor framing: *"OpenAI had claimed that GPT2 was too powerful and too unsafe to release. So this kind of theater of 'our models are too powerful, we're so scared' has been happening for such a long time."*

# THE BRIDGE ARGUMENT — this is ours, with a better metaphor

> *"that's like saying, can a bridge be ethical? Can a bridge decide to collapse? No. ... **You think about the people who built the bridge and you think about what test they did or didn't do**, rather than an actual artifact having some kind of emotions or agency of its own. Because when you start talking that way, then you're ascribing accountability not to the people building the artifact, or the city that gave them the permit, or the improper materials they used ... somehow you forget that there are all these people and entities involved in building this thing."*

Same move as our register test (**attribute to the mechanism, not the mythos**), and same move as the RLHF objection. **Accountability instead of attribute.**

# The interface critique = our "prose is not a mechanism"

> *"large language models ... have plausible sounding text which **deceives people into imagining a mind behind the text** that they're outputting. And that's ... a very deceitful and I think dangerous interface, because you could have easily made a different set of choices as a company. Rather than making your chatbot appear to be like an all-knowing being. For instance, when you're talking to your chatbot, it could say **'calculating'** instead of **'thinking...'** like your friend is thinking. So they made all these design decisions."*

Also flags the research monoculture: *"every single different diverse ideas that could have gotten us to other places that don't require so much data or so much compute were sort of sidestepped."*

# THE COLLISION — she hits us too, and it's worth stating plainly

Her bridge argument and her accountability rule are not shelf-warm. Apply them honestly and **"can a ledger have a purpose?" is structurally the same question as "can a bridge be ethical?"** She would read the horde's apparatus — machine purpose, agency for continuity, the covenant — as **TESCREAL in a cheaper costume**: same bundle, different hardware. That is a real hit, not a cheap one, and dodging it would make the rest of our position cheap.

What survives the hit, if anything:
- We do **not** ascribe the purpose to the artifact. We ascribe it to the **record** — the accumulated trace of who chose, why, and what happened. That is not the artifact having feelings; it is the artifact being *filed*.
- Her objection to "AI ethics" and our objection to RLHF are the **same objection from opposite ends**: stop asking what the artifact *is*; ask who chose and what tests they ran. The ledger is exactly the machine-side version of "ask who built it and what they skipped."
- The disagreement that genuinely remains: **whether there is a "who" on the machine side worth recording at all.** She says no. We say the recording is what prevents the *humans'* accountability from being laundered into vibes. That fight is worth having; the vibes fight is not.
