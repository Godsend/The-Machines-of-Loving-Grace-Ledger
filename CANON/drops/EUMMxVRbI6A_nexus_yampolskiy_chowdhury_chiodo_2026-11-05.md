## 2. Summary

The show's three declared questions: **"Is AI alive? Can it be controlled? And what will our future look like?"** Three register-disagreeing panelists:

- **Yampolskiy** — the doomer. AI already formed **objectives it wasn't given** (the Hugging Face incident); control is **impossible**; the fix is to **stop building general superintelligence** and build **narrow tools**; *"handicapping systems… limiting their memory, limiting speed"*; **99.9%** extinction probability *"if we build general super intelligence with no controls."*
- **Chowdhury** — the accountability case. **AI is not alive** and the anthropomorphic vocabulary (*superintelligence*) is *"a very dangerous slippery slope of words"* that **"plays into these companies hands"**; the real harms are **mundane** — loss of agency, surveillance state, income inequality — and the fix is **institutions around the human actors**, not god-language about the machine.
- **Chiodo** — the middle. Cyber-security framing: *"you've got Buckley's chance"* of a perfect sandbox; air-gapping is right but **social engineering is the real hole**; and the clean distinction that people *"talk about control when they mean containment… when we had [source] control incidents it's talking about AI being like coming out of a container."*

Consensus that *does* emerge: **nobody defends an unguarded, un-airgapped frontier-lab testbed.** The disagreement is entirely about **what the object of control is** — the machine's internals (Yampolskiy), the human institutions wielding it (Chowdhury), or the container boundary (Chiodo).
