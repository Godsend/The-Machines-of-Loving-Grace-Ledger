**Ch.2: Perversity**
- [O]/definition: perverse acts are deliberate wrongs done *because* they are wrong. "Not because we're making a mistake… but because we choose not to."
- [E] Augustine's pear theft (*Confessions* II). → **Accurate**. It matches his New Yorker essay "The Strange Appeal of Perverse Actions" (19 Jul 2019), which uses the same Augustine example, the same Joker line, and Sussman's "For Badness' Sake". - [A] "Hopeful monster" (Goldschmidt): when life is failing, big random jumps beat small tweaks. He correctly flags that biologists reject Goldschmidt's theory for evolution, and he uses it only as a life analogy.

- [E] Ironic-process slips: people worried about seeming prejudiced blurt prejudice, and people told to keep a secret leak it. → Wegner-style ironic process. He gave no citations, so the specific studies are **unverified** this pass.

It is distinguished from signaling, and peaks in toddlers, adolescents and midlife. **No discriminating test is offered.** The account is a taxonomy (autonomy / signaling / strategic irrationality / hopeful monster / ironic slips) rather than a theory with a falsifier.
- [E] Strategic irrationality: mixed strategies in poker and Nixon's "madman theory". Standard game theory (Schelling), accurately used.

## 2. The loneliness ≈ hunger claim: accuracy check

[E] **Bloom's source, Cacioppo, is represented accurately.** Cacioppo, Cacioppo & Boomsma 2014 (*Cognition & Emotion*, "Evolutionary mechanisms for loneliness") argue loneliness "may have evolved as an aversive state that, like hunger, thirst, and pain", motivates repair of social connection. Bloom's version of this is nearly verbatim: it is a signal, not a glitch; a child who cannot feel lonely would not thrive.

[E] **The strongest empirical support for the hunger analogy is the one he didn't cite:** Tomova et al. 2020 (*Nat Neurosci* 23:1597–1605; author correction 2022), n = 40.
- After 10 h of total social isolation, midbrain (SN/VTA) regions responded selectively to social cues, as they did to food cues after 10 h of fasting. The responses correlated with self-reported craving.
- **But** striatal and cortical regions *differentiated* social craving from food craving.
- So "like hunger" holds at the motivational/midbrain level and fails as an identity claim.

[E] **The social-pain ≈ physical-pain line (Eisenberger, Lieberman & Williams 2003, *Science* 302:290, Cyberball, dACC) is NOT invoked by Bloom.**
- His "painful" and "agony" language is phenomenological, not neural.
- Note for any downstream use: the shared-substrate reading is contested. Woo et al. 2014 (*Nat Commun* 5:5380, n = 60) found *separate* multivariate fMRI patterns for heat pain and for rejection (viewing an ex-partner), despite overlapping univariate activation.

**Verdict:** [O] Bloom's actual claim is accurate and appropriately modest: a hunger-like motivational signal, painful, evolutionarily functional. "Starvation" is Big Think's word. The neural-overlap story, which would make "starvation" literal, is neither claimed by him nor settled in the literature.

## 3. Spot-checks against primary sources

**3a. "Punish the company even if it causes mass harm": HOLDS, simplified.**
- Primary source: Baron & Ritov 1993, *J Risk & Uncertainty* 7:17–33 ("Intuitions about penalties and compensation in the context of tort law"), read in full text.
- Version B: if fined, the company stops making the vaccine and "there would be no vaccine available". Of 74 respondents who fined the company in version A, **only 23 reduced the penalty** and 48 were insensitive.
- Version D: payment secret and the company insured, so zero deterrence. 48 of 72 were insensitive.
- The companion study (Baron, Gowda & Kunreuther 1993) found only **15%** penalized more when penalties deter than when they perversely cause beneficial products to be withdrawn.
- [E] The direction holds; roughly two-thirds ignore deterrence. Bloom's "People say, put them out of business. Make them suffer" turns a two-thirds majority into "people". The "make them suffer" affect is his gloss; the paper measures penalty magnitudes, not stated motives.

**3b. Need for Chaos demographics and politics: PARTLY HOLDS.**
- Petersen, Osmundsen & Arceneaux 2023 (*APSR* 117(4); 8 US studies, N = 10,921). Arceneaux et al. 2021 (*Phil Trans R Soc B* 376:20200147; AU/CA/UK/US).
- [E] The item paraphrases are accurate: "Sometimes I just feel like destroying beautiful things"; "just let them all burn".
- [E] Young > old and men > women are **verified** (2021: "chaos-seeking tends to be higher among the young, men and those with less than a college degree").
- [E] "Extremes of both Right and Left" is **only partly** supported. The 2021 paper cites it as a *prior exploratory* finding, but its own analysis says "Right-wing ideology explains some of the variance" in the high-chaos profile and finds "an overlap between Right-wing populism and Need for Chaos". Bloom's "it turns out it's kind of balanced" is **an overstatement**.
- [E] **Omission that matters for this drop:** the APSR paper's *mechanism* is status-seeking × social marginalization, and **loneliness moderates it**: β(status-driven risk-taking × loneliness) = 0.06, p < 0.001.
  - Bloom treats loneliness (Ch.1) and destructive perversity (Ch.2) as unrelated topics.
  - The paper he cites ties them together: status-hungry people who feel ostracized want to watch it burn.
  - This is the most productive joint in the interview, and he walked past it.

**3c. Victimhood breeds callousness and selfishness: HOLDS.**
- [E] Wohl & Branscombe 2008 (*JPSP*): Jewish Canadians reminded of the Holocaust accepted less collective guilt for harm to Palestinians, and Americans reminded of 9/11 or Pearl Harbor did likewise for Iraq. This supports "more callous towards… those who they believe have caused them to suffer".
- [E] Zitek, Jordan, Monin & Leach 2010 (*JPSP* 98:245): recalling unfairness led to refusing to help the experimenter and to more selfish allocations, mediated by entitlement.
- Minor slip: Bloom's "being *seen as* a victim" should be "*feeling* wronged".

**Spot-check scorecard:** 3 of 3 load-bearing claims hold in direction. One (Need for Chaos) overstates left/right symmetry. One unflagged contested premise (loneliness not rising) is carried in from Thompson. One "uniquely human" claim is weakened by the great-ape teasing data. No fabricated or misattributed study found.

## 5. Falsifiers and testable joints

- [F] **F1 (on R1):** If perverse acts show the neural/behavioral signatures of error (post-act surprise, error-related negativity, corrective adjustment on the next trial) rather than goal attainment (reward response, no corrective update), Bloom's "chosen, not mistaken" definition fails and Hamartia-as-δ absorbs the class. If they show goal-attainment signatures, the house frame needs a separate term for chosen wrong.
- [H]→[F] **F2 (on R2):** Exploration-account prediction: perverse acts should rise with *recent sustained failure* (hopeful monster) and with *low perceived agency* (autonomy), and should not be predicted by boredom alone. If perversity tracks boredom or arousal and not failure or agency-deficit, the FEP/hopeful-monster mapping is decorative.
- [H] **H3 (from §3b, the unconnected joint):** Need-for-Chaos × loneliness × status orientation (Petersen 2023 β = 0.06) predicts that **AI companions that reduce felt loneliness should reduce Need for Chaos among status-oriented marginalized users.** [F] If loneliness relief via AI leaves NfC unchanged, or raises it (via a sycophancy-fed sense of entitlement), the palliative case in Ch.1 has a social cost Bloom didn't price.
- [F] **F4 (on R5):** If communities with persistent, public grievance records (feud-culture ethnography, permanent online reputation systems) show *lower* retaliation-cycle length than communities with forgetting/amnesty norms, the "ledger stabilizes punishment cycles" worry is wrong. If longer, the house needs an amnesty/decay primitive alongside append-only memory.

## 6. Verdict [O]

- **Strongest joint:** the "bad morality" segment.
  - The claim that most cruelty is done by people who feel righteous, that retributive intuitions ignore deterrence, and that victim identity licenses harm is backed by real, correctly cited literature. Baron & Ritov, Wohl & Branscombe, Zitek et al. and Gray & Wegner all check out.
  - It is consistent with his 2017 New Yorker thesis.
  - This is a credentialed researcher speaking accurately inside his field.
- **Weakest:** the perversity section.
  - It is a recycled 2019 essay plus a taxonomy of five unconnected explanations with no discriminating test.
  - It includes a "uniquely human" claim the great-ape teasing data weaken, and a "balanced left/right" Need-for-Chaos claim the source papers don't support.
  - The loneliness-not-rising premise is borrowed and contested (Buecker 2021).
- **The inflation is mostly Big Think's, not Bloom's:** "starvation", "Bloom argues", and "accelerated by AI" are all channel copy. On air he is hedged ("I don't know how severe this problem is").
- **For the house:** the most valuable thing here is that it **breaks Hamartia-as-prediction-error at its foundation.** Augustine, the source the house drop calls the corrupter, brought the counterexample of chosen wrong, and Bloom's whole research program is about it.
- R4 and R5 together say the house's suffering and Moloch frames are built from the victim's and the defector's side. They have no term for the **rewarding, righteous punisher**, who on Bloom's account does a large share of the world's harm.
- Recommended house action: add a "chosen-wrong / appetite" term next to δ, and an amnesty/decay primitive next to append-only memory. Then test F1 and F4 before either is promoted beyond [H].
