**Primary source checked** (not just the press release): Bezuidenhout, Peters, Kumar et al., "Deep

## 1. What it actually is

`[EST]` **Optical skyrmions** are topological structures in light (particle-like topologies in
structured light, building on a real and active subfield — the paper cites 29 prior references,
including work on topological robustness in turbulence and quantum-regime skyrmions). Their topological
invariant *N* is useful as a communications "alphabet" because it survives distortion, but **the states
aren't necessarily orthogonal**, so there was no deterministic way to READ *N* back out — that is the
actual problem this paper solves, not "building a brain."

**The method:** a deep diffractive optical neural network (D2NN-class device — the family Lin et al.
2018/Ozcan group established; not these authors' invention, the application is new) with **two
independent channels, five diffractive layers each**, that physically routes light to spatially
separated output detectors depending on its topological charge. Reading *N* becomes "which detector lit
up," not "process a captured image computationally."

**The genuine novel contributions, not in the press release at all:**
- **Real-time ("live") training**, not train-once-then-deploy.
- **Training parameters reduced by a factor of 1000** vs. prior diffractive-network training schemes.
- Demonstrated on **81 topologies** (vectorial Laguerre-Gaussian modes), working at noise levels that
  break conventional Stokes polarimetry.
- A working demo: transmitted and recovered an image encoded with a **15-level topological alphabet**,
  negligible crosstalk.

## 2. Press-release distortion caught (the exact discipline this house keeps applying)

`[CORRECTION]` The Wits press release says: *"After training on just 15 examples, the light-powered
brain successfully recognised 81 different light structures."* **This conflates two different numbers
from the abstract.** The "15" in the actual paper is the **15-level alphabet used in the communications
demo** (a separate experiment, image transmission), not a training-set size for the 81-topology
classifier. The abstract never states a training-example count for the 81-topology detector. This is
exactly the "secondary source miscounts/conflates the primary paper's own numbers" pattern this house
has caught repeatedly this week (Hoffman/ERC grant, the Astrum/Jon audit, etc.) — logged as another
instance, not a new class.
