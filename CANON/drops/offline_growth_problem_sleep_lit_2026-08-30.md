## Primary sources (both verified directly, open access)

### 1. Denis et al. (2021), J Neurosci 41(18):4088 — "Sleep Spindles Preferentially Consolidate Weakly Encoded Memories"
- **Key finding:** Across 6h awake, weakly encoded memories deteriorate FASTEST. With a nap, forgetting is attenuated to be EQUIVALENT across encoding strengths. **Consolidation of weakly-encoded items was uniquely associated with fast sleep-spindle density during NREM.**
- **Significance (their words):** "sleep was associated with better memory for weakly encoded information... fast spindles uniquely associated with consolidation of weakly encoded memories."
- **Why it matters here:** Weakly-encoded = high prediction-error / gap-tagged material (the canon's "unresolved gap"). It consolidates during NREM — the lowest-PCI state, frame offline. The gap is being processed WITHOUT the frame.
- URL: https://www.jneurosci.org/content/41/18/4088 · DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0818-20.2021

### 2. Denis, Chen, Kaur, Clayden, Schreiner, Cairney (2026), PLoS Biol — "Theta oscillations tag episodic memories for sleep-dependent consolidation"
- **Key finding:** Human EEG decoded "tagging" states at learning: memories tagged for consolidation across SLEEP (not wake) were linked to **3–8 Hz theta at encoding**. Tag magnitude predicted **slow-oscillation → sleep-spindle coupling during post-learning sleep**, which predicted better post-sleep memory.
- **Why it matters here:** This is the mechanistic spine for "the gap is tagged at encoding, then processed offline during sleep." Tagging = marking gap-significance; consolidation = the offline (frame-offline) work. The Living Frame is not in the loop during the actual consolidation.
- URL: https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3003938 · DOI 10.1371/journal.pbio.3003938
