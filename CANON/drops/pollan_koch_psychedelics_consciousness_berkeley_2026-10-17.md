## 2. Spot-checks against primary literature

**C1. Plant anaesthesia (Pollan P3). HOLDS, one detail swapped. [E]**
Yokawa, Kagenishi, Pavlovič, Gall, Weiland, Mancuso & Baluška, "Anaesthetics stop diverse plant organ movements, affect endocytic vesicle recycling and ROS homeostasis, and block action potentials in Venus flytraps," *Ann Bot* 122(5):747–756 (2018), DOI 10.1093/aob/mcx155, PMID 29236942, PMC6215046. **Full text read.** Findings:
- *Mimosa* leaves, Venus flytraps, Cape sundew and pea tendrils lost touch-induced and autonomous movement after 1 h of 15% diethyl ether in a sealed glass chamber.
- Flytrap action potentials were abolished and recovered ~900 s after the ether was removed.
- Lidocaine on *Mimosa* roots alone abolished leaf response.
- **Correction:** in this paper xenon (80:20 with O₂) was tested only on seed germination, chlorophyll, ROS and root vesicle trafficking. It was **not** tested on flytrap or *Mimosa* movement. Xenon on *Mimosa* is an older citation (Weigl 1968, cited in the paper). The flytrap result is diethyl ether.
- **Register limit:** the paper shows loss of excitability and movement. "Loss of consciousness" is an analogy the authors draw *from* human anaesthesia, not something measured in plants.
- **Circularity note:** the paper's discussion cites "Pollan, 2013" among its plant-cognition references. The literature Pollan cites back partly rests on his own popular writing. [E]

**C2. Plant memory "for 30 days" (Pollan P2). HOLDS for habituation. [E]**
Gagliano, Renton, Depczynski & Mancuso, *Oecologia* 175:63–72 (2014), DOI 10.1007/s00442-013-2873-7, PMID 24390479 (abstract read). *Mimosa* leaf-folding habituation persisted "even when left undisturbed in a more favourable environment for a month."
- **Scope:** this is habituation, i.e. non-associative learning.
- The stronger claim, associative learning in peas (Gagliano et al. 2016), **failed a larger blinded replication** (Markel 2020, *eLife* 9:e57614, PMID 32573434; Gagliano's comment and Markel's response are on record).
- Pollan's "you can teach a plant something" is true for habituation. It should not be read as established conditioning.

**C3. Alaska pilot (Koch K12). CORE HOLDS, details drift. [E]**
Joseph Emerson, off duty in the cockpit jump seat of Alaska/Horizon flight 2059 on **2023-10-22**, pulled the engine fire-suppression handles. Per the probable-cause affidavit he said "I thought I was dreaming and I just wanna wake up." He had taken psilocybin mushrooms ~48 h earlier at a memorial retreat for a friend and had not slept for >40 h (OPB 2023-10-24; CNN/NYT 2023-11-10; he later pleaded guilty and got probation in state court).
- **Drift in Koch's version:** "two years ago" (actually ~3), "from here to Seattle" (the route was **Everett → San Francisco**, the opposite direction), "the previous night" (he half-corrected himself; it was ~48 h).
- **Omitted confounds:** sleep deprivation, plus the depression and grief Emerson himself reported. Koch presents the case as pure psychedelic derealization. The anecdote supports "not harmless". It does not isolate psilocybin as the cause.

**C4. MEQ-30 → therapeutic benefit (Koch K9). HOLDS with caveats. [E]**
Ko, Knight, Rucker & Cleare, *Front Psychiatry* 13:917199 (2022), a systematic review (abstract read): 10 of 12 studies found a significant correlation, mediation or prediction link between mystical experience and symptom reduction. The authors flag small samples, low diversity and 6/12 open-label designs. "Nice correlation" is fair. Causation is not established.

**Not checkable from this talk:** no IIT technical claim and no 5-HT2A or REBUS mechanism claim was made, so none could be spot-checked.

## 3. How Koch handled Pollan's plant-consciousness claim

[E] **Koch did not qualify or reject it. He went further than Pollan.** In sequence:
- Pollan retreats from his in-trip conviction to *sentience without interiority* ("am I prepared… No, I'm not" [L124]; "I don't think they have that" [L129]).
- Koch pushes back the other way every time: "But it makes you think, doesn't it?" [L125]. "But it would still be conscious because it would still feel like something" [L127]. "It may feel like something to be a plant. And when the plant is anesthetized, it wouldn't feel like anything anymore" [L130].
- He then extends the claim unprompted to single-celled organisms: "many of us believe that ultimately [consciousness] is related to complexity. So it wouldn't be totally crazy… it would feel like something" for a bacterium [L134-L138].

[O] **Calibration verdict: the journalist was better calibrated than the IIT authority.**
1. Koch's stated premise is "complexity", which is **not IIT's criterion**. IIT ties experience to irreducible intrinsic cause–effect structure (Albantakis et al. 2023, *PLoS Comput Biol* 19:e1011465, IIT 4.0). By IIT's own lights a system can be very complex and have negligible Φ: Tononi & Koch 2015 (*Phil Trans R Soc B* 370:20140167) say complex feed-forward networks are not conscious. Koch gave a folk-panpsychist gloss, not an IIT derivation.
2. Nobody has computed Φ for a plant or a bacterium. Exact Φ is super-exponential in system size and has only been computed for toy systems (Mayner et al. 2018, PyPhi). So "it may feel like something" has **no measurement behind it**. He is on firmer ground with "may" than with "would".
3. He reads the anaesthesia result (C1) as loss of feeling. The primary paper licenses loss of action potentials and movement only.
4. It sits in tension with his own K2 ("no objective test… always an inference… more and more difficult" as we move to plants).

He is entitled to the *conjecture*. IIT 4.0 does predict graded consciousness in simple biological systems (Tononi & Koch 2015: "common among biological organisms and can occur in some very simple systems"). But on this stage it was delivered as near-assent without the theory's actual criterion, and the audience heard the IIT figure endorse plant feeling. **File K10 as [C], not as expert confirmation of Pollan's anecdote.**

## 6. Falsifiers

- **[F] F-1 (silent-substrate discriminator).** Take a cortical population that is quiescent but intact, compare it with the same population pharmacologically or optogenetically inactivated, and hold reportable activity constant. If experience reports differ (IIT's prediction), a commit-only account is incomplete: experience tracks structure that writes nothing. If nothing differs and experience tracks only active write/update events, IIT's inactive-vs-inactivated postulate is in trouble. *Status:* proposed in the IIT literature. Its feasibility in humans is disputed. Not run.
- **[F] F-2 (plant claim).** If plant "anaesthesia" is just generic membrane or excitability suppression, then non-anaesthetic agents that block action potentials to the same degree should reproduce the immobilization *and* the ROS/vesicle signature. If they do, the "same thing that knocks us out" framing loses its consciousness implication. Yokawa et al. did not include that control.
- **[F] F-3 (house debt).** Until the house gives a third-party-scoreable operational definition of "commit" that yields different verdicts from Butlin indicators and from PCI on at least one system, row 3 stands: IIT is ahead on measurability.

[O] **For the house:** the friction worth keeping is **state vs event (row 2)**, not "integration". IIT and bare-commit share a word and make opposite predictions about a silent, intact brain. On measurability (row 3) the honest scoreboard favours IIT. The house's answer has to be an operational definition of a commit (F-3), not another analogy.
