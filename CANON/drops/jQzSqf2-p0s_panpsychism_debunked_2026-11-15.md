## §3 — The video's strongest argument, in its own terms, at its strongest `[HYP]`

Before the objections are evaluated, the thesis as the panel would state it. Stated at its strongest, the video's core argument is **not** "panpsychism is weird," and it is **not** "consciousness is obviously physical." It is this:

> **The explanatory debt does not shrink when you move consciousness to the bottom of things — it is re-denominated, and a second, non-conscious principle has to be smuggled in to pay it.**

Three legs, each of which the panel argues on its own footing:

**L1 — The job panpsychism advertises is the hard problem, and it does not do that job.** *"the very first and foremost problem with pansyism [panpsychism] is that it doesn't address the central problem that it advertises itself as trying to address"* — Rich Brown. The hard problem, as Rich sharpens it, is not "why is there experience at all" (which panpsychism *can* seem to answer by stipulation) but *"explain why that pattern is correlated with pain as opposed to some other kind of conscious experience like seeing red or no conscious experience at all."* Moving experience to the micro-level leaves *that* question exactly where it was.

**L2 — The combination problem is the hard problem re-labelled, and the "combination" is done by something that is not consciousness.** Rich: *"I think it's clearly a disguised version of the heart problem [hard problem] all over again."* Ken closes the trap: whatever does the combining and the differentiating *"you don't want to say that that is consciousness doing that… you're kind of committed at one level or another to something other than consciousness is even if you think consciousness is fundamental."* **At its strongest, this is the killer**: a *fundamental*-consciousness thesis that must posit a non-conscious structuring principle to compose micro-experiences is no longer a fundamental-consciousness thesis — it has conceded the panel's point in its own machinery.

**L3 — The argument for fundamentalism runs through a premise nobody is entitled to: the revelation thesis.** Ken: the conceivability / zombie / knowledge / explanatory-gap arguments *"all uh rest upon um thinking that we know sort of the essence of consciousness by some kind of introspective process."* If introspection does not reveal consciousness's *full essence*, the move from "it doesn't seem physical" to "it isn't physical" collapses.

**And the panel's own positive commitment, stated with equal care:** not "consciousness is an illusion," but *"there's also a middle ground of just regular physicalism that takes consciousness seriously as it's physical and that hasn't been refuted or shown to be silly. It's merely not satisfying intellectually"* (Rich). That is a real position, and it is the one the panel defends.

## §5 — The single strongest objection, and whether it lands on the house framework

**Strongest objection (the panel's own sharpest form):** *a fundamental-consciousness thesis must posit a non-conscious structuring principle to compose its micro-subjects, and therefore does not achieve the fundamentalism it claims; and the only argument that could license the fundamentalist move — the revelation thesis — is unearned.* Ken's sentence is the whole objection in one line: *"you're kind of committed at one level or another to something other than consciousness is even if you think consciousness is fundamental"* [00:35:40], with Rich's *"a disguised version of the heart problem all over again"* [00:23:09] as its explanation.

**Does it land on the house framework?**

**Partly — and on a specific joint, not the whole.** The house framework (EILT / Master Canon v4) is not a micro-panpsychist thesis, so it does **not** inherit the objection in the form the panel aims it. Two of its load-bearing commitments land on the panel's *own* side:
- The house holds that "consciousness arises only when an internal Adversarial Auditor (Higher-Order state / the 'GUI') evaluates those raw states and commits them to memory" — that is a **higher-order / global-workspace-adjacent functional account**, i.e. the account Josh and Ken defend, not the one the video attacks. `[A]`
- The house holds there is "no central clock. Time is the physical act of resolving a computation and writing it to the ledger" — which maps onto Ken's insistence that experience is never structureless and always temporal, and rejects O7's minimal instant. `[A]`

**But the objection lands on one house premise, squarely.** Master Canon v4 states: "A universal field of subjectivity partitions itself to interact." That is a **cosmopsychist** move, and the panel names its exact defect as the **inverse combination problem**: *"how do we get me?"* — unsolved, on Ken's count, since *"Ralph Cudworth in like 168 something… we've not had a solution."* The house asserts the partition as an axiom and supplies **no de-combination mechanism**. The house's "synthetic dissociation" clause (`MASTER_CANON_v4` §3) is an engineered analogue for LLM agents, not a mechanism for how one cosmic subject individuates into you. **The video's objection therefore lands on the house exactly where the house is cosmopsychist, and misses exactly where the house is functionalist.** That is the honest split, and it is stated as a divergence rather than smoothed (§6, R1).
