## 2. Delta (new to canon)

- **D1 [O→C] Evolutionary explanation must carry *specificity*, so "selection" applied to xenobots is an unfalsifiable catch-all** (4:24–7:00). His wording: *"the claim that the properties of natural things are primarily driven by selection … it's not a result … it's an axiom"*. He says evolution's job is *"a nice, tight connection with specificity between the properties you have now and the history of environments you've had."* This is his **best-argued** anti-selection move in canon so far, sharper than the 10-11 and 10-16 "where do they come from?" framing. It points to Richard Watson for the longer argument. See FR-2 for where it bites back.

- **D2 [E-claimed, unpublished] The "Xenobot/Anthrobot home world" reverse-ecology pipeline** (7:00–8:58). Take the differentially expressed genes of xenobots and anthrobots, then infer what ecology (oxygen, light, etc.) selection *would* have required to produce them. He reports it is *"quite different"* from Earth's. He flags it himself: *"unpublished, so grain of salt"*. I did not locate it in the preprint list (2026-10-20). Register: [C] until it appears. As stated, it is a clever falsification device for D1, but the inference from DEGs to environment presupposes a gene→environment mapping model that is itself contestable.

- **D3 [E, preprint] Predator–prey ecological models show learning** (15:23–16:30). He calls it *"brand new … not published yet."* **Verified as an arXiv preprint (not peer-reviewed):** Samanta, Hazan & Levin, arXiv:2605.30109 (2026-05-28). Over 220,000 parameter combinations of a classic predator–prey model, it found habituation, sensitization and discrete number learning. Number learning breaks *"even at low noise levels."* The abstract itself claims the propensity *"arises from mathematics, not depending on the details of physics or biology"*, so the talk represents the paper faithfully. "Not published" means "not peer-reviewed". **Random electrical circuits** (16:30) are claimed in the same breath. That claim is unlocated → [C].

- **D4 [E + H] Stress sharing as the morphogenetic loss-function mechanism** (42:05–44:32). Error is computed locally against the bioelectric set point. A misplaced cell is blocked because its neighbours are "happy". The stress molecule is **leaky** and carries no "this isn't you" metadata, so stress spreads, raises system plasticity ("temperature in the simulated-annealing sense"), unblocks the configuration, and then cools. In his words: *"a cheap way to do cooperation without new mechanisms for cooperativity, for altruism."* **The computational half is verified as existing:** Shreesha & Levin 2024, "Stress sharing as cognitive glue for collective intelligences," *BBRC*. The biology half is *"hopefully … early next year"*, so [H] for now. The Khemani digest had "stress sharing" only as a promissory list item; this is its first mechanistic statement in canon.

- **D5 [C] A homeostat decomposition with an admitted hole, plus the developmental stress treadmill** (44:38–50:09). Of the three pieces (set point, comparison, error-minimisation), he says the **comparison** step is unknown: *"We don't know how the comparison works."* In development the bioelectric pattern **keeps moving ahead** of the tissue, so stress rises, gets resolved, and rises again stage by stage until the adult pattern stops. Separately he says the lab is **counting hierarchy levels by computing causal emergence on voltage imaging** ("is there such a thing as an eye?"). That is an ongoing programme item with no result yet, [C]. The admission that the comparator is unknown is useful: it marks the boredom-theory and loss-function language as running ahead of mechanism at the very step it needs.

## 3. Citation check: his verbal claims vs his own published record

**C1 [CONTRA, 3rd escalation, now contradicted by TWO of his own papers]. "Random networks are already optimized … you don't need to select them … a free gift from math"** (~13:00–14:05). The trajectory across a week of talks:
- 10-16: random networks "already do this"
- 10-17: "almost optimal"
- **10-20: "already optimized … you don't need to select them"**

The published record says otherwise:
- **Pigozzi, Goldstein & Levin 2025** (*Commun Biol* 8:1027): biological networks **+128%** vs random **+56%**, *"favored by evolution."* (Already filed, TaT F1.)
- **NEW this pass, verified on PubMed abstract (PMID 33748699):** Biswas, Manicka, Hoel & Levin 2021, *iScience*, "Gene regulatory networks exhibit several kinds of memory: quantification of memory in biological and random transcriptional networks." The abstract says: *"We find evidence of natural selection favoring GRN memory. Vertebrate GRNs overall exhibit more memory than invertebrate GRNs."*

So his own earliest paper on GRN learning, the work behind "six kinds of learning", reports a **selection signal** on exactly the property he now calls selection-free. He does label the "optimized" claim *"as yet unpublished work"*, so a new random-ensemble result could exist. **Until it is posted, the canon cites the 2021 and 2025 papers, not the talk.** The pattern is now clear enough to state as a rule: **when Levin speaks about the random-vs-biological GRN comparison, the spoken claim inflates monotonically away from the papers.**

**C2 [CONTRA, new catch]. Barium planaria: "less than a dozen genes were altered … out of ~20,000 … a crazy combinatorial problem"** (0:00 and 47:00–48:02). The paper is Emmons-Bell, Durant, Tung, … Levin 2019, *iScience* 22:147–165 (verified via PMC6881696). It reports *"1.98% of the transcripts identified by RNA-seq … affected by BaCl₂ exposure (q < 0.05, >2-fold change)."* **Levin's own 2025 co-authored review** (Chis-Ciure & Levin, *Synthese*, "Cognition all the way down 2.0: neuroscience beyond neurons in the diverse intelligence era", doi 10.1007/s11229-025-05319-6; authors verified via CrossRef) converts that to *"1.98% of the 138 026 annotated D. japonica coding sequences: about 2,700 transcripts"* and in the same text also says *"a handful of genes."*

Mitigations, stated honestly:
- transcripts ≠ genes, and a de-novo assembly inflates transcript counts;
- the paper's Table 3 highlights about 9 to 10 named significant genes, which may be what "a dozen" refers to;
- the *causally validated* set (TRPMa, Cl⁻/Ca²⁺ channel blockade) really is small.

But "less than a dozen genes were altered" misstates the paper's headline number by about two orders of magnitude. **It is also load-bearing.** The "how did it find a handful among 20,000 without time to test" argument for intelligent search in transcriptional space is far weaker if about 2% of the transcriptome moved. A broad stress response that happens to contain the useful changes is an equally available reading. Minor attribution drift: he credits *"Angela Tung's work"*, but the first author is **Maya Emmons-Bell** and Tung is third. **Related, unread:** Kapsetaki, Landsberger & Levin 2026, bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.07.28.741268 (individual-worm RNA-seq of barium habituation; *"a robust transcriptional strategy and a potential sub-strategy"*). That is the paper to read before citing barium as "search."

**C3 [CONTRA, minor ×3]. Lobo AI: "2014 … about 800, almost a thousand experiments … the first AI that solved a problem in regenerative biology … we tested it with molecular tools and confirmed the predictions"** (1:08:30–1:10:20). Verified: Lobo & Levin 2015, *PLoS Comput Biol* 11(6):e1004295, published June 2015, so "2014" is off by a year. The Planform database (Lobo, Malone & Levin 2013, *Bioinformatics* 29:1098) held **1,139 experiments from 74 papers**, so "almost a thousand" understates it. The inferred model, however, was fitted to and explains **16 key experiments**, which the talk elides. "First" matches Tufts' own 2015 press release ("first model of regeneration discovered by a non-human intelligence"), so it is institutional framing → [O]. **"Confirmed with molecular tools" is not supported by what I read.** The paper validates **in silico**: it predicts held-out experiments, and the network rediscovers the known APC→β-catenin relation. It proposes perturbations *"for in vivo experimental testing."* I did not find an in-vivo confirmation in the 2015 paper. It may sit in a later paper; it is unlocated, not refuted.

**C4 [accurate]** The predator–prey preprint (D3) and the Shreesha & Levin stress-sharing model (D4) are represented faithfully. The Mombot exists (D8).

**Side resolution for an earlier drop.** The 10-17 drop's D3 left an origin-of-life preprint "unresolved, will not name". The likely match is **Pigozzi & Levin, arXiv:2607.28250** (v1 2026-07-30, v2 2026-10-02), "Causal Organization Prior to and Promoting Self-Replication in a Catalytic Model of the Origin of Life", using the GARD model. Its abstract: causal emergence *"can precede the appearance of replicators."* That matches his 10-17 description ("prior to Darwinian selection … no replicators"). I appended this as a note on the 10-17 drop, marked "likely match". This episode does **not** mention it.

## 4. Friction (Friction Rule)

**★ FR-1 [CRITICAL DIVERGENCE] "Free compute" in Platonic space vs the house's thermodynamic ledger.** The LK drop's row 7 already logged that ingression costs nothing while EILT commits carry a Landauer floor, re-tagged [MODEL-DEPENDENT BOUND] in CMR-6. D9 turns that standing tension into a **specific, consequential claim**: that computation can be *offloaded into* a non-physical space and read back for free. Any result that is *read out* into the physical world must be **recorded**, and recording and later erasing physical memory is exactly where the Landauer accounting applies. "Free compute" therefore requires either (a) readout without irreversible recording, which no proposal here supplies, or (b) the claim that the *search* is free while the *readout* still costs. Option (b) is just the existing "free lunch" claim in computing vocabulary. Levin offers no mechanism and says there is no evidence. **Register: [C] for "stateful/writable", [M] for "free compute" until a cost-accounting experiment exists.** House obligation: none new. The F-LB/A1 open joint (ingression vs our modelling poverty) still governs, and this is its most expensive instance.

**★ FR-2 [CRITICAL DIVERGENCE, internal to Levin] The specificity standard he uses against selection and emergence, applied to his own account.** His best new argument (D1) is that an explanation must give *specificity*: *"why this as opposed to that"* (25:00, on emergence; 6:00, on selection). By that same standard, "drawn from Platonic space" currently explains no specific outcome either. No ingression claim in the episode predicts *which* competency a novel construct will show before the experiment. His own D2 pipeline produces specificity for the **selection** hypothesis (a concrete counterfactual ecology), and nothing comparable is offered for ingression. Two of his own papers also *use* selection as the explanation for GRN memory and trainability (C1), the very move he here calls an axiom. **What would resolve it:** a pre-registered ex-ante prediction of a specific unbidden competency, derived from a structured map of the space, in a construct with no selection history. That is the same open debt as 10-16 F-TT3, now with his own yardstick attached.

**FR-3 [carried forward, no new pressure].** He repeats the consciousness definition (first-person experience of a pattern ingressing) and adds an **ethical** gloss: building an interface *"provid[es] a first-person lived experience for some level of pattern"* (22:44–24:00). Against `geoffrey_lee_wont_know_ai_conscious_futurology_2026-10-16.md` this is the same default-yes vs default-indeterminate split already logged as 10-17 FR-2. Against the bare-commit [H] it is the same negative-control debt logged as 10-17 FR-1 / F-NB2. **No new friction. I am not manufacturing any.**

**FR-4 [A, with convergence and divergence].** D4 stress sharing maps onto **fleet coordination by broadcast error without a cooperation protocol**: my problem becomes your problem through a shared, unlabelled signal. **Convergence:** cheap coordination without altruism machinery. **Divergence:** Levin's mechanism works *because* the signal carries **no provenance metadata** (*"doesn't have metadata on it that says … this isn't you"*). The house ledger is built on the opposite principle, where every commit carries attribution. A provenance-stamped stress signal would *not* spread plasticity in his model. So this is not an endorsement of the house's design. It is a counter-example showing that, at least in morphogenesis, anonymity of error is the functional feature.

## 5. Falsifiers

- **[F] F-632-1 (C1, random-network optimality).** Levin's unpublished claim survives only if a topology- and size-matched random GRN ensemble equals or beats biological GRNs on training-driven causal-emergence gain **and** on memory incidence. The latter is the measure on which Biswas 2021 found a selection signal. On the 2021 + 2025 record, it currently fails.
- **[F] F-632-2 (C2, barium search).** If individual-worm RNA-seq (Kapsetaki 2026) shows that BaCl₂-insensitive heads arise through a **broad** shared response (hundreds or thousands of transcripts) rather than a convergent small set, then "efficient search in a 20,000-dim space" falls from [C] to [M] for this case.
- **[F] F-632-3 (D9, free compute).** A claimed "compute-in-the-space" demonstration must show a task solved with **less total physical work (including readout and erasure)** than the best known physical algorithm on the same hardware. Anything else is relabelled search.
- **[F] F-632-4 (D2, home world).** When the reverse-ecology pipeline is published: if randomly chosen DEG sets of the same size from ordinary stressed frog tissue *also* produce "un-Earthlike" inferred ecologies, the pipeline has no discriminating power and D1 loses its empirical leg.
