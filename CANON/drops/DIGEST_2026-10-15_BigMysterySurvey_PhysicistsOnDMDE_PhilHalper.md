## 3. Named-physicist reactions and **disagreements** (the highest-value output)

Panel: **Sean Carroll** (Johns Hopkins, "professor of natural philosophy"); **Niayesh Afshordi** ("Nesh"); **Ghazal Geshnizjani** ("Gazelle") — both University of Waterloo / Perimeter Institute. **The disagreements between them are the payload, so they are separated out.**

### 3.1 Where they AGREE
- **Big Bang is a nomenclature/physics-decoupling question, not a science question** — Carroll: *"this is not a science question really. This is just a nomenclature question"* (`[QUOTE, L50-52]`). Geshnizjani: *"I'm happy with that answer"* (`[QUOTE, L124-125]`).
- **Inflation is under-tested and its popularity is partly tenure/tradition.** Afshordi (co-author of the inflation critique book): *"it's been the longest game in town and it's been developed the most, so most people haven't really heard much about the alternatives"* (`[QUOTE, L191-197]`). Carroll names the two open problems: *"why did inflation start in the first place … a lot of working cosmologists just kind of brush under the rug"* and *eternal-inflation's failure to produce a firm prediction* (`[QUOTE, L260-277]`). Geshnizjani: *"I always come from a very skeptic camp … it relies on a pillar of assumptions that we still haven't been able to verify"* (`[QUOTE, L280-303]`).
- **We do not know what is inside a black hole.** Carroll: *"we don't know what's going on and that's what makes it fun"* (`[QUOTE, L1634-1635]`).

### 3.2 Where they DISAGREE — the useful friction
1. **Dark matter: is modified gravity dead, or merely unfashionable?**
   - **Carroll (hard):** *"a lot of the support for modified gravity and things like that [is] coming from the non-professional physics audience. Those of us who do astrophysics … are pretty aware of the huge number of constraints … it's basically ruled out in my mind. There's got to be some kind of dark matter particle out there."* — `[QUOTE, L435-445]` (`[00:16:56]`).
   - **Afshordi/Geshnizjani (softer on the demographic point, not on the physics):** Afshordi attributes part of the online-vs-in-person shift to the internet liking MOND *"much more than people in real life"* (`[QUOTE, L402-406]`) — i.e. he **concurs it is an audience artefact** but states it as an observation, not a dismissal. **Geshnizjani adds a *dissenting* frame:** the dark-sector options themselves may be an artefact of *"opaque language … mathematical formulation or descriptions that we come up [with] in order to explain a physical phenomena … this phenomenon doesn't quite fit in one category"* (`[QUOTE, L614-635]`, `[00:23:57]`). **This is the one place a panelist resists Carroll's "it's just a particle" framing** — Geshnizjani raises the possibility that the particle/gravity dichotomy is itself a description-layer artefact. **`[RIFF]` Note for us: this is a working physicist making a version of a description-relativity argument — but she uses it to *loosen* the option set, not to license a nameless driver. 2. **All three are surprised by the dark-energy result — and Carroll disputes its likely cause.**
   - **Geshnizjani:** *"I'm surprised. I would think if the majority of the responders were astrophysicists, they would have gone with the first one [cosmological constant]"* — `[QUOTE, L607-610]` (`[00:23:33]`).

3. **Hubble tension: a real bet, and a methodological split.**
