### 1. Executive Summary & The Core Mechanism

Why does the right hemisphere control and sense the left half of the body (decussation / contralateral wiring)?

For over a century, neurobiology treated contralateral organization as an evolutionary mystery or assumed it conferred some magical teleological advantage. Wessendorf & Wang formulate a clean, constraint-driven explanation based on **optical physics and conservative evolutionary reuse**:

1. **The Inversion Caused by Optics:**
   - The physics of a pinhole or convex lens intrinsically inverts the visual field: light from the right side of the organism projects onto the left hemiretina, and light from the left projects onto the right hemiretina.
2. **The Ancient Coordinate System (Optic Tectum / Superior Colliculus):**
   - The oldest sensory-motor integration structure in vertebrates (~500+ million years ago in Cambrian jawless fishes) is the **optic tectum**.
   - To make visual coordinates map smoothly across the midline without spatial discontinuities, the optic nerve fibers crossed at the **optic chiasm**, routing right-visual-field data to the left optic tectum.
3. **Multimodal Alignment in Registration:**
   - Rapid escape and targeting responses require sight, sound, and touch to align to the *same* spatial map. 
   - Because the optic tectum was already organized contralaterally, somatosensory (body touch) and acoustic maps had to be routed across the midline to match the visual map's coordinate system.
4. **Conservative Downstream Co-Option:**
   - When tetrapods and mammals later evolved complex descending motor tracts (rubrospinal and corticospinal systems for limb and finger manipulation), evolution did not re-invent an uncrossed coordinate frame from scratch. It built directly on top of the ancient midbrain tectal map. Fine motor control co-opted an ancient somatosensory routing pathway that was already cross-wired to compensate for the lens of the eye.
