## §1 · What the reel actually carries (the source's own words)

**Door 1 — destination-first "future backwards" planning.** The opener's frame:
> *"You can live your life thinking from the present forward, or you can live your life thinking from the future backwards. There's no experts in the future, it turns out. There's only experts of the present."* — and: *"you literally flip the time arrow."*
> The consequence he draws: *"you no longer have to fight over direction, you just have to fight over destination."*

**Door 2 — the "root node problem" and AI for science.** A founder-class speaker narrates moving from computer science to neuroscience *"in service of building general AI"*, then protein folding as *"a root node problem. If you solve it, you unlock many many other avenues of research"* — the AlphaGo moment as *"a lighthouse moment"*, and the targets left on the board: *"room-temperature superconductors, solve one of the Millennium Prize problems."* The relayed headline claim of the segment: *"it's going to be a new golden era of scientific discovery over the next 10 years."* `[HYP]`

**Door 3 — the AI co-scientist.** *"we developed this … tool called AI co-scientist, which essentially enables researchers to ask a research question. … it's an agent based system that can then sift through and do literature search, and then create hypothesis, and try to validate them, and rank them, and bring them back to the scientist."* A partner claim: on a superbug hypothesis, *"they could get to the same hypothesis in just about 3 days."* `[HYP]`

**Door 4 — world models vs LLMs (the sharpest architectural claim on the reel).** *"how can a system possibly plan a sequence of actions if it can't predict the consequences of its actions? … that's the concept the world models. You don't have that in LLMs."*
> *"The real world is way more complicated than the world of language."* — *"predicting the next word in the text is not that complicated."* — *"real intelligence comes from an an understanding of the real world"* `[sic: ASR doubled "an"]`.
> The prediction: *"the type of architecture that we use for LLMs, generative AI, does not apply to the real world … it's going to be the physical AI revolution, if you want."*

**Door 5 — scaled public-good systems (relayed numbers).** Flood forecasting: *"we now have a system that provides up to 7 days advance notice to sig- significant riverine floods covering … in about 150 countries, covering 2 billion people"* — immediately hedged: *"the problem is not solved yet. We still need to work on … flash floods and others."* `[HYP]` — the numbers are **the speaker's, relayed, not independently verified.**

**Door 6 — anthropic-report clip (augmentation vs automation).** *"people were being augmented to do the most difficult tasks"*; and *"challenge humans to do even better, harder, deeper thinking work."* `[HYP]` — self-reported by the lab that publishes the report.

**Door 7 — business transformation.** *"the AI transformation is not a technology transformation, it's a business transformation led by technology"* — illustrated with a loan-approval workflow whose real gain is *"a new product"* (a 10-minute loan), not a 10% cost cut.

**Door 8 — the safety-institution clip.** The **International AI Safety Report**: *"it's being modeled after the IPCC for climate change … bringing about 95 experts from around the world"*, to synthesize *"what are the risks, what do we know about them, … what are the mitigations"* — with an explicit firewall: *"it's really important to distinguish this scientific work of synthesis … from the political decisions."*

**Door 9 — value framing and the principal-agent aside.** *"AI as an amplifier of human capacity"* / *"AI as an amplifier of human ingenuity"*, used *"not … as a prediction … as a design principle, design goal"*, and the plea *"agree on what's worth building."* Against which the consumer-agent aside: *"I want an AI that works for me"* — the "Charlie" blog frame, set against Siri/Alexa's corporate principals.

**Door 10 — creative economy.** *"With AI, I can produce those ideas in seconds"* — interviewer and artist agree the tool collapses years of production to seconds but that *"it's going to always come down to … a creative director, or the taste"*.
