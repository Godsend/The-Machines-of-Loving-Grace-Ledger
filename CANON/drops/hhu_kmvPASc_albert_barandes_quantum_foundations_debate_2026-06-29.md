## §1 What was actually argued

**Shape.** [EST] A ~47-min prepared commentary by Albert (0:01–0:47), then a cross-examination that never gets past two questions (paraphrase): what exactly are the laws, and is this realist, then two closing statements. The host's own closing summary: *"we didn't really move too far past the questions of scientific realism and the formulation of Jacob's theory"* [2:31:51]. This is an **unresolved** debate, not a convergence.

### 1a. Albert's position

- **Motive.** [A] For 30 years, realist foundations work has struggled with the wave function as *"a fantastically highdimensional object"* that seems to make fundamental space high-dimensional [0:01:14–0:02:20]. A theory that removes it would be *"a noteworthy and exciting possibility"* [0:17:08].
- **His one-sentence reconstruction, which he says is equivalent to Barandes's ontology** [0:07:29–0:11:30]:
  - The ontology is a finite set of particles with determinate positions.
  - Take the wave function at t0 to be the configuration eigenfunction of the actual initial configuration.
  - Evolve it with Schrödinger's equation, and apply the Born rule to get P(config at t | config at t0).
  - *"And that's it. That's the whole theory."* [0:11:30]
  - On this reading the wave function is a mathematical instrument encoding the initial configuration, not a free degree of freedom as in Bohm, and not the whole state as in GRW. [A]
- **Indivisibility as he reads it.** [A] Only t0 is privileged. The configuration at t1 tells you *nothing* beyond what t0 already gives about t2. Splitting the double-slit calculation t0→t1→t2 into two steps destroys the interference pattern, so *"the two-step calculation is forbidden"* [0:22:50]. *"The history of the world is in Jacob's terminology indivisible"* [0:23:24], and the theory is *"radically non-Markovian"*. Consequence: Russell's five-minutes-ago scenario becomes empirically distinguishable [0:23:58].
  - Measurement-induced suppression of interference is only a "statistical vestige" of effective collapse or branching. It is *not* an exception, and indivisibility stays absolute for the whole universe [0:28:31–0:30:45].
- **The incompleteness objection (his central argument).** [A] The theory gives single-time marginals from t0. It gives nothing about how the configuration at tA relates to tB, and nothing about paths [0:32:29–0:33:38].
  - His "minimal completion": the configuration jumps independently at each instant according to the marginal [0:34:47–0:36:30].
  - Under it, *"The traces and records and memories of the past that seem to present themselves at any single instant, although they are all consistent with one another, are all completely unreliable as indicators of actual past configurations"* [0:36:30].
  - Napoleon example: conditioning on books about Napoleon gives an astronomically low probability that the earth existed one nanosecond ago [0:38:46]. With no reliable dispositions there are no observers or measuring instruments [0:39:18–0:39:52].
  - Verdict: *"the question of its empirical adequacy and the deeper and prior question of whether it has any empirical content at all remains at best radically unsettled"* [0:41:36–0:42:11].
- **Locality.** [A] Barandes is *"working with a different notion of what it means to be a fundament for a fundamental physical theory to be local than Bell was working with"* [0:43:54]; this is not a way around Bell's theorem. Albert reports, second-hand, that Barandes told him Bohm's non-locality *"can't unambiguously be settled"* on that notion, which Albert calls *"really really bad news"* [0:44:30–0:45:08]. **Barandes never directly answered this specific Bohm point on-air.** [EST] (absence checked by reading the full transcript)
- **Close.** [A] He cannot say whether what Barandes has in mind is realist in his sense: *"I don't feel like I'm coming out of the discussion with a clear answer to that question"* [2:19:11–2:19:44].

### 1b. Barandes's position

- **Albert's version is "incomplete", not "false".** [A] *"Was that law false or incomplete? >> Incomplete"* [1:15:45]. The jumping completion matches Bell's "Everett (?)" theory from Bell's 1981 *Quantum mechanics for cosmologists* [0:48:40–0:49:45]. **[EST]** That paper exists (Bell, in *Quantum Gravity 2*, Isham/Penrose/Sciama eds., OUP 1981; reprinted in *Speakable and Unspeakable*). Albert clarifies that he read the theory as having *no* dynamics, and the jumping was only an illustration [0:50:20–0:51:27].
- **The theory in three steps** [0:53:06–1:29:45], all [A]/[HYP]:
  1. **Ontology.** Configurations in roughly physical space, a "classical-like" ontology. Realism here is *"a form of intellectual exploration"*, not dogma [0:54:46].
  2. **Every system is a stochastic process.** That includes the full tower of multi-time joint probabilities, which he calls modal structure. Markov is a special simplification [1:11:41–1:14:35].
  3. **Division events.** At some t′, the first-order conditionals rooted at t′ decouple from the standalone distribution at t′. This yields a good initial-value problem [1:18:29–1:20:45].
     - They are never exact. He says they are *"exact to like one part and 10 to the 40"* and that *"these division events as I prove in the paper they emerge out of decoherence"* [1:21:21–1:21:53]. **The number and the proof are not verified here.**
     - Exact: the configurations and the modal structure. Emergent and inexact: the dynamical laws and the initial-value split [1:29:09–1:29:45].
     - He claims this explains *why* physical theories show a clean law/initial-condition split [1:30:51].
- **The top system.** [A] He resists presupposing a mereological "top system". He cites *"junky"* worlds: Schaffer 2010 (**not verified**) and Thomas Mormann 2014, **[EST]** *Set Theory, Topology, and the Possibility of Junky Worlds*, *Notre Dame J. Formal Logic* 55(1):79–90 [1:09:22]. He then grants a top system for the sake of argument [1:08:15–1:10:32].
- **"What are the rules?"** This is the crux exchange [1:24:10–1:46:06].
  - Barandes: *"So the rules are the universe stochastic process. That's the rule."* Albert: *"No, but Jacob that's not enough. Um um there are lots of those."* [1:34:49]
  - Barandes adds a past hypothesis: there is at least one very good division event at t0. From it a Hilbert-space and unitary representation follows [1:27:24–1:28:02].
  - The higher-order multi-time probabilities are **not fixed by the theory**: *"The theory does not specify what it is"*, though *"in a sense that like the universe knows"* [2:01:46–2:02:19].
  - They are only partly constrained "upwardly" by subsystem division events, and they *"wash out"* of subsystem predictions (*"what's miraculous about this is the washing out of the unknown details"* [2:00:03]). For the universe as a whole they cannot be fixed [2:03:25–2:04:30].
  - What he gives up: *"What I'm giving up is exact initial value problems"* [1:46:06]. Albert: *"I get so queasy when your answers start with if"* [1:45:34].
- **"Nothing else works".** [A] In his account, Bohmian mechanics works only for fixed numbers of non-relativistic particles [1:46:06]. Bell's 1984 Markov jump model for QFT was shown not to work by Gillespie [1:46:39–1:51:35]. Albert's counter: *"There's no argument that this is the only way to Bohmianize"* quantum field theories [1:52:08–1:52:42], and Bell's own paper mentions other routes [1:53:14].
  - **[EST] Citations verified:**
    - Bell, *Beables for quantum field theory* (CERN-TH-4035/84).
    - L. Hardy, D. Home, E. J. Squires, M. A. B. Whitaker, *Realism and the quantum-mechanical two-state oscillator*, PRA 45, 4267 (1992).
    - D. T. Gillespie, *Why quantum mechanics cannot be formulated as a Markov process*, PRA 49, 1607 (1994).
    - Hardy et al., *Comment on…*, PRA 56, 3301 (1997), with Gillespie's reply in the same issue.
  - **Not verified:** the content of Gillespie's proof (that some quantities leave [0,1]), the citation counts, Gillespie's cause of death, and the "2001" non-Markovian attempt. A 2001 Gillespie *Chaos* paper exists, but its content was not checked.
- **Wave function = latent variable of a hidden Markov model** [2:07:53–2:11:11]. [A] It shares the latent variables' marks: many variables, abstract, malleable, unobservable, not in physical space, not back-reacted upon. *"It just nails what the wave function looks like so perfectly"* [2:10:07]. **[EST]** The supporting paper exists: Barandes, *Pilot-Wave Theories as Hidden Markov Models*, arXiv:2602.10569. Its text cites L. P. Neuwirth (1970 lectures; Institute for Defense Analyses), matching the on-air "Lee Neuwirth / IDA" history. Barandes's history paper also exists: *Historical debates over the physical reality of the wave function*, *Eur. Phys. J. H* (PhilArchive BARHDO-3). [EST]
- **Stochastic precedents.** [EST] Everett's thesis does contain a section titled "d. The stochastic process interpretation" (checked in the quantum.country PDF of Everett's text). [EST] Nelson, *Derivation of the Schrödinger Equation from Newtonian Mechanics*, Phys. Rev. 150, 1079 (1966). Bopp and Fényes are named on-air but not checked.
  - Albert's narrowing: *"just being stochastic is surely not a sufficient condition"* for doing GRW's job in statistical mechanics (Penrose collapse as his counterexample). Barandes: *"Agreed."* [2:15:34–2:16:45]
  - **[CONTRADICTED]** Barandes says GRW *"was uh concocted in the 1970s"* [2:12:15]. The verified publication is Ghirardi, Rimini, Weber, PRD 34, 470 (**1986**). I did not verify when the work began, so the claim is unsupported by the publication record rather than disproven.
- **Closing claims** [2:21:21–2:31:51]:
  - Measurement problem. [A] It is resolved by a single kind of modal structure, with no special status for measurements.
  - Scope. [A] Gravity is excluded: *"I'm not claiming to have a theory of quantum gravity"* [2:24:07]. The theory is meant to be modest, for meso- and macroscopic systems.
  - Position eigenfunctions. [EST] They exist in non-regular representations of the Weyl algebra, via Halvorson, *Complementarity of representations in quantum mechanics*, *SHPMP* 35:45–56 (2004). The reference is verified; the technical claim is the speaker's.
  - Locality taxonomy. [A] He separates correlation, counterfactual, causal and signal locality: *"I am openly admitting that quantum mechanics exhibits counterfactual non-locality"* [2:28:02]. He claims only **causal** locality, under **his own** account of causation built from his conditional probabilities.
  - Tsirelson result. **[EST]** The paper exists: Barandes, Hasan & Kagan, *The CHSH Game, Tsirelson's Bound, and Causal Locality*, arXiv:2512.18105, journal ref **Phys. Rev. A 114, 022208 (2026)**. ## §4 Verdict

**Strongest point.** Albert's reconstruction plus the Napoleon argument [0:32:29–0:41:36]. It is a crisp, checkable demonstration that a law giving correct single-time Born probabilities can still destroy the reliability of records, and with it observers and empirical content. The demand for a multi-time law is therefore not fussiness. It is what makes evidence possible. This is the most portable result in the video, and it applies to *any* record-keeping system, ours included. [C]

**Weakest joint.** Barandes's on-air answer to "what are the laws?" is "the universe is a stochastic process" plus a past-hypothesis division event. The decisive multi-time structure is *unfixed by the theory* (*"the universe knows"*). As stated in this video, his fundamental-law claim explains every subsystem's law-likeness while fixing nothing beyond Albert's one-sentence law. That is the necessity move, and Albert's *"there are lots of those"* stands unanswered. [C]

**Secondary weak joint.** "No other theory works" leans on the Gillespie history against *Bell's Markov* QFT model. Albert correctly notes that this rules out one route to Bohmianizing QFT, not all routes. [A]

**Opinion.** The debate stalls on a definitional impasse about what a law is, not on physics. Barandes's best evidence is off-stage (the PRA Tsirelson paper), and this video should be cited for the *framing of the objection*, not for the theory's success. [C]
