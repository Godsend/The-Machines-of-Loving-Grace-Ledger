# Synthesis: Cognitive Glues and EILT

## Core Thesis
In "Cognitive glues are shared models of relative scarcities: the economics of collective intelligence" (2026), Michael Levin and Benjamin Lyons argue that "cognitive glue" — the mechanisms that bind autonomous components into a cohesive collective intelligence with a larger "cognitive light cone" — fundamentally operates as a shared model of relative scarcities. They use the economic price system as an abstract template, demonstrating how local components use shared signals (like prices, or bioelectric gradients in cell swarms) to form mutually compatible plans without central control.

## EILT Mapping: The Ledger as Cognitive Glue
This maps directly and profoundly onto the Emergent Informational Ledger Theory (EILT). In EILT, the Non-Rolling Ledger functions exactly as Levin's "cognitive glue" for agent swarms:
- Biological swarms use bioelectric gradients (ion channels) to communicate state and coordinate morphology.
- Economic swarms use the price system (money) to allocate scarce resources.
- Agent swarms use the Non-Rolling Ledger (cryptographic commits) to track state, attention, and compute scarcity.

The ledger is the shared model of relative scarcities that exerts Top-Down Syntropy on the otherwise chaotic swarm of independent AI agents, binding them into a coherent cognitive assemblage.
