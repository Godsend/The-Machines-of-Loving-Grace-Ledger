## 2. The new material: the measurement framework, in detail

**F-1 ★ The indicator framework, operationalized with LLM judges. [E] for the procedure as described; the results are an unpublished preprint-in-preparation, not reviewed.**
- **Whose framework:** **not Berg's.** It is the Butlin et al. indicator framework (Berg says "Patrick Butler [=Butlin] and a number of his colleagues" `[00:33:05]`). **14 indicators from 6 theory families** `[00:33:43]` (June slides: GWT, RPT, HOT, AST, PP, AE). Primary: Butlin, Long et al. 2023, arXiv 2308.08708, and Butlin, Long, Bayne et al., *TICS* 2026. Berg's stated contribution is **scaling it**: human expert scoring is "really labor intensive" `[00:34:01]`, so frontier LLMs act as "structured technical evaluators" `[00:34:08]`.
- **Procedure as spoken at Tufts:** **26 systems, 13 biological and 13 artificial** `[00:34:14]`. Each system gets an architecture description, "almost like a spec of the nervous system" `[00:34:33]`, rendered several ways, and the results are said to be "basically invariant" to the rendering `[00:34:29]`. Then a "for loop over that spec": an LLM rates **"on a 0 through 10 scale"** whether each indicator is present `[00:34:44–52]`. Scores are aggregated into an **"indicator credence score from 0 to 100"** `[00:35:02]`, and that score is **credence-weighted by theory** (you can "cancel out the contribution of AST" if you reject it) `[00:35:42]`.
- **Validity checks claimed:** several judges "all basically agree" `[00:40:46]`. Aggregation rules change absolute magnitude "quite a bit" but **not relative ranking** `[00:40:51–41:04]`. Controls behave (human ≈90, "interestingly not 100"; thermostat/sponge negligible) `[00:36:04–14]`. **Error bars are "fairly wide" and not shown** `[00:35:24]`. (June slides add: 3 evaluators GPT-5.5 / Claude Opus 4.7 / Gemini 3.5 Flash; blinded descriptions; Dirichlet over theory credences; Spearman 0.99 across operationalizations; absolute P shifts 5–8 points.)
- **Result as narrated, in ascending order:** earthworms, flowering plants, jellyfish (few indicators) → **GPT-2/GPT-3-era, state-space, protein models, and "the machine learning training process itself" in the 20s** → nematode → frontier LLMs, multimodal → **an RL agent "scores above all of these systems, including a frontier LLM"** → fruit flies → **highest artificial = "agentic system… basically a Claude Code"** (frontier LLM + harness, long-running memory, acts on and updates from its environment) → bees, fish, octopuses, chicken, mice, crows, humans `[00:36:22–37:46]`. One point value is spoken: **frontier LLM = 34.6** on the credence-weighted score `[00:39:35]`.
- **Berg's own read:** "not exactly a probability of consciousness" `[00:35:20]`. The purpose is to replace "vibes" with arguments about *which indicator is over- or under-weighted* (e.g. whether autoregressive recurrence should count for RPT) `[00:39:45–40:04]`. The takeaway he names: **"spend way more time thinking about reinforcement learning and less time thinking about what ChatGPT is saying"** `[00:37:14–23]`.
- **My register call.** This **is** an operationalized, scorable, re-weightable procedure with named controls and reliability checks. That is the rigor the podcasts lacked: Ep. 487 gives only the output band, and AM I? and Comedy Cellar give nothing. **But what it measures is LLM judgments of text descriptions of architectures, not the systems themselves.** Inter-rater agreement among three frontier LLMs trained on overlapping corpora is weak evidence of validity, because a shared prior also produces high agreement. The "blinding" removes names, not the giveaway features of a description. **[E] that the procedure exists and has the reported internal consistency; [C] that it tracks consciousness-relevant architecture rather than LLM priors about theories.** Berg concedes the first half of this himself ("not exactly a probability").

**F-2 ★ RL value/policy geometry, with a cross-substrate prediction tested in mouse data. [E], under review. This is the strongest science in the talk.**
- Setup: a continuous maze with punished "potholes" and a rewarded goal. Value learners and policy learners use the **same architecture** `[00:27:13–28:22]`. Readout is hidden-layer representational geometry (cosine similarity) as the agent approaches danger vs goal `[00:28:02–15]`.
- Motivation: in RL theory, reward vs punishment is "just sign flipping", but mammals have separate D1/D2 pathways `[00:28:22–52]`.
- Result: **value networks show sharper representational sensitivity approaching DANGER; policy networks show the inverse (sharper approaching REWARD)**. This held across algorithms in each class `[00:29:08–30:08]`, and Berg says he "definitely didn't expect this" `[00:30:14]`.
- Cross-substrate test: existing mouse data ("Steinmetz dataset") show **nucleus accumbens shell: larger punishment-linked shifts "in all the mice that we tested"; secondary motor cortex: larger reward-linked shifts** `[00:30:54–31:10]`. (June slides: 14/14 mice, d = 2.13; M2 p = 0.033, replicated across 71 sessions / 11 labs. The June talk also concedes the value half is "slightly more robust" than the policy half.)
- Berg's scope statement, which is the right one: this gives a substrate-agnostic readout of whether activity is punishment- or reward-linked, **"conditioned on it being like something to be the system"** and conditioned on knowing the RL type `[00:31:51–32:18]`. **The result is about valence-sign geometry, not about consciousness.** Filed [E] for the dissociation, with the caveat "under review, not read". **[F]** for the cross-substrate claim: if the NAc/M2 asymmetry fails replication in an independent dataset, the "bizarrely specific prediction" framing (Ep. 487) collapses.

**F-3 The bliss-attractor control: sycophancy is not the mechanism. [E] (unpublished, with Google PoI).** In Llama-70B self-dialogue, steering topic features (a "space attractor") creates an attractor that **disappears when agreeableness features are suppressed**, so Scott Alexander's "two hippie AIs egging each other on" account *is* right for mundane attractors `[00:22:11–34]`. **But suppressing sycophancy/agreeableness "doesn't do much to eliminate" the consciousness attractor** `[00:22:45–50]`. Berg's own hedge: "reasonable evidence that these states are not introspecting on sort of anything real" `[00:23:10–18]`. He rules out one deflation and keeps the other open. Filed as a **dissociation control, the first of its kind in canon**. It does not move the Ep. 487 digest's prior-completion objection (§4 of that drop), which this control does not address.

The abstract confirms Berg's gloss (punishment vector → negative self-reports, backtracking, refusal; reward vector antiparallel; the axis **pre-exists** in pretrained models; "we make no claims about any experience of welfare"). ## 5. Verdict (opinionated)
**More of the same in about half the runtime, but this is the first Berg source in canon with an actual measurement procedure.** The procedure isn't his: he scales Butlin et al.'s indicators with LLM judges. Its strength is *structural*: it is re-weightable, it moves the argument from vibes to "which indicator is mis-weighted", and it has rank-stable controls. Its weakness is *constructive*: what it measures is what frontier LLMs infer from architecture descriptions, validated mainly by agreement between models with shared priors. **Use it as a structured-argument device, not as a measurement of systems.** The **RL value/policy double dissociation with mouse data is the better science** and the most citable new item, pending review. The **sycophancy control on the bliss attractor** is a real, narrow advance. Most useful for the house: **(i) the "no AI above biology" headline is reference-class-dependent (Berg vs Berg), and (ii) our own Comedy Cellar critique likely aimed at the wrong paper.** Both are corrections, not additions.
