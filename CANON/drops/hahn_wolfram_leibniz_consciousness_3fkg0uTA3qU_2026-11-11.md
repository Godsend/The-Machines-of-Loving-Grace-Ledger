## 1. What the source actually says

**The frame [EST·relayed].** Recorded at the MIT Media Lab; the host opens by pointing at a prior Hahn appearance and at a then-just-recorded Jacob Barandes episode (both linked on screen), then asks the guest to expand an idea he had raised off-air: *"You had some ideas as to how to unify liveness [**Leibniz**, `[CLEANED]`] with Stephen Wolfram's work."* [00:00:23].

**★ The ladder of representations [HYP] — the interview's spine.** Hahn's proposal is that consciousness is **not a switch**: *"there's an idea attributed to liveness [**Leibniz**, `[CLEANED]`] where it's really a hierarchy. It's kind of a ladder of different capabilities. And it revolves around the ability to represent your ideas, to think about your thinking, and then to think about that thinking, and so on."* [00:00:45]. He sets the discontinuity explicitly — *"where it's not just on or off, but it's kind of this discontinuum"* [00:01:49] — and applies it to machines: *"do they instantiate something like consciousness? And if so, is it like human consciousness?"* [00:02:09]; *"Or does it have some way of knowing what it knows and thinking about what it's thinking about?"* [00:02:27]; and he forecasts the loop closing — *"we will kind of close the loop on this thing. That the snake is going to bite its tail, and we will bootstrap this thing we call consciousness inside these machines."* [00:02:41] **[POETIC/HYP]**.
The host immediately supplies the strongest pressure on the ladder — a **threshold objection**: *"I think you can always translate a continuum to an on or off by saying, is it zero or non-zero?"* [00:03:14], by analogy with a *"chargeless"* photon. Hahn's answer is that the threshold question is **open**, and that a GPU is now the awkward case: it is *"essentially what the classical world would say is a fancy crystal"* [00:04:11] that is nonetheless running the intelligence.

**★ Wolfram's computational equivalence, as relayed [EST·relayed].** *"So Wolfram has this fantastic idea of computational equivalence. The idea is that the natural world stumbles upon computers quite often."* [00:04:29]; *"what Wolfram showed with his cellular automata research is that it's relatively easy to stumble upon a system that's complex enough to support universal computation"* [00:05:17]; the four-class breakdown is attributed to Wolfram, and generalised — *"any system that's not obviously simple, any system that's sort of interesting in its dynamics, the majority of the time that's going to support universal computation"* [00:05:42]. The ceiling is the point: *"you smash into this sort of upper ceiling of computation relatively quickly, right? It's sort of a low speed limit"* [00:07:56]; *"that's the ceiling. There's nothing more powerful than that."* [00:08:11]. Hahn then **transposes the question to representations**: computers are easy to find in the wild, so *"what's the chance they have any interesting programming to go with that?"* [00:08:33] and *(host)* *"is there a universal representation at level six?"* [00:08:44]. Hahn's answer is hedged — *"I would like to hope that we're not at that universal level yet."* [00:09:31] — and his closing image is blunt: *"we're angels with assholes. Right? That we're still these corporeal beings that eat sandwiches"* [00:10:08–00:10:12] **[POETIC]** — he explicitly flags the quote as **unattributed**: *"I forget who says it, we'll have to look it up"* [00:10:08].

**★ Unthinkable thoughts and the perceptual window [HYP].** *"I do think it's the case there's unthinkable thoughts."* [00:11:23]. Evidence by relaying **Richard Hamming** — *"He says there's smells that dogs can smell that we can't smell"* [00:11:48] — and by arithmetic: *"there are numbers that we can never conceive of. At least we can write them down like Graham's number."* [00:12:07]. The philosophical label is given **hedged and without its originator** — *"The philosophical term, I believe, is umwelt."* [00:12:30].

**★ The mental immune system [HYP] — the second load-bearing claim.** Hahn's answer to *why* the window is closed is not just capacity: *"it's kind of an immune system that we mentioned before. That our mind itself is trying to protect us from a variety of thought patterns that would essentially destabilize."* [00:13:29]; *"this barrier that evolution has instantiated to prevent us from going nutty"* [00:14:01]; and, more provocatively, *"Intelligence could be kind of a Fermi-style filter that's actually preventing a lot of progress."* [00:14:23]. The host's counter-move is to **turn the filter into an autoimmune pathology** — *"Is it the case then that there would be autoimmune disorders of the mind?"* [00:19:09] — which Hahn accepts as *"a fantastic idea"* he would *"have to think about"* [00:19:19].

**★ Language as organism — the "divine parasite" [HYP/POETIC].** Language is not for communication but is *"As an organism. Something that's alive."* [00:20:11]; *"I've referred to it as a divine parasite. But it's something that lives on the brain. But it's not the brain."* [00:20:31]; hence the startling claim about what a conversation is: *"I'm not talking to your brain. I'm talking to this entity that lives in your brain."* [00:20:57]. Dissociative identity is offered as the evidence — multiple *"operating system, software, stack"* on one hardware, *"multiplexed. Like we run more than one app on our phone at a time."* [00:21:03–00:21:09]. The umbrella question is stated directly — *"is our brain a programmable object? And if so, is language, is natural language, a programming language?"* [00:21:37]. The organism framing is credited **in passing to a colleague** — *"I've been thinking about it more with my colleague, Dr. Behrenholz [**Barenholtz**, `[CLEANED]`]"* [00:20:11].

**★ Virtual machines all the way down [HYP].** *"this idea of a virtual machine"* [00:23:37] — the emulator example (Super Mario on a MacBook by simulating a Nintendo chip [00:24:19]), then recursion: *"you can have an emulator running on an emulator"* [00:24:40], *"it could be virtual machines all the way down in some sense"* [00:24:49]. Conclusion for neuroscience: *"there could be multiple layers between the thing doing the talking and the neurons doing the firing"* [00:25:07], against *"a one-to-one mapping"* [00:25:15]. The host presses hard on whether this is literal — *"the turtles all the way down, I take it extremely seriously."* [00:25:27]; *"do you actually mean that? Or do you mean that it's multiple levels of virtual machines, but there's a finite end?"* [00:25:31] — and Hahn splits the answer: in the brain there may be a bottom, **but in physics not necessarily** — *"when we get down below the atoms, there might be software again"* [00:26:05]; *"we have information at the bottom, which somehow instantiates the atomic reality. And then on that, we build computers out of the atoms, and then we get software again."* [00:26:12]; *"in the modern world, we never actually talk to the hardware directly. We build a virtual hardware that's much easier to use."* [00:28:24], with **PyTorch** as the example — *"PyTorch is an imaginary machine that has things like vector operators and matrix multiply"* [00:28:10] — and the conjecture that evolution did the same [00:27:51–00:28:35].

**★ The bat, infant amnesia, and the representation argument for experience [HYP/A].** *"the classic example is, what is it like to be a bat? A classic paper from 74, I think."* [00:29:06] — **the author is never named on tape.** Hahn's own position is the strong one: *"I take a strong approach to this that maybe it's not like anything to be a bat"* [00:29:39], grounded in **infant amnesia**: *"this idea in psychology of what's called infant amnesia"* [00:29:45]; without language there is no episodic memory because there is no **label** — *"without language, right, we install language into children between the ages of one and two"* [00:30:24]; *"which is like the Dewey Decimal catalog, without having this card, I can go, that's the one I mean"* [00:31:03] **[A]**. Applied to ourselves: *"the vast majority of our so-called experience is outside this thought window"* [00:32:13]; *"We don't even know that we have gallbladder, let alone what they do"* [00:32:20]; *"it would seem to me that we should be skeptical that it's like anything to be those systems"* [00:31:56].

**★ Madness, lethal ideas, and the retreat [HYP/C].** The second half turns personal. Losing your mind is *"leaving everyone else behind"* [00:34:19]; the price of the frontier is stated as a rule — *"you have to be willing to go crazy"* [00:33:30] … *"you can't get past these barriers that your mind has built"* [00:33:59] (the cue boundary sits over the 29 s hole, §0). Then the drop is described from the inside: *"I've had an experience. Let's just say that. And I'll be somewhat vague about it."* [00:45:10]; *"I felt like I was losing my mind"* [00:45:15]; the mechanism he blames is **emulating guests** — *"I have to take them on, first emulate them, but also think that they could be correct"* [00:45:45]; recovery was **ACT** — *"I went through ACT therapy. So acceptance, commitment therapy"* [00:47:15], a practitioner named only as *"this lady named Lillian Dindo [**Lilian Dindo**, `[CLEANED]`]"* [00:47:21] (the channel description links a Lilian Dindo TOE episode, §5). The host's formulation of the technique is the cleanest line in the segment — *"it strikes me as sort of reading the code, but not running the program"* [00:48:00] — which Hahn calls *"a brilliant way of phrasing it"* [00:48:05].
**"Lethal text"** enters here, and **without a source**: *"this, this concept of lethal text and so on. When I first came across that, just the concept of an idea that would do harm"* [00:48:18]. He names his own hesitancy *"to even share that idea"* [00:48:24], and the general law he draws — *"they are lethal to our previous self, right? Your adulthood is lethal to your childlike self"* [00:49:02]. The thesis he lands on is **moral, not mechanistic**: *"the real scary idea is that those that have gone mad are not wrong"* [00:50:47], with the moon/balloon-of-cheese illustration [00:52:00–00:53:32] of a correct experience mislabelled by its token, cut by the 25 s hole [00:52:21→00:52:51].

**The close [HYP/C].** On AI: *"we're approximating already, not just artificial general intelligence, but artificial superintelligence"* [00:40:38] — with **no metric, benchmark or date given**; the recipe is *"we don't even need a new recipe"* [00:41:13], simply more data and embodiment — *"but it hasn't watched YouTube yet"* [00:40:51]; *"Little kids will slosh water around for hours on end, just learning how fluids work"* [00:41:09]. Scaling is cited as the phase-transition evidence: *"this is exactly what we've seen with these large language models with the so-called scaling laws"* [00:40:04], up through *"it can do a theoretical physics"* [00:40:28]. For culture: the future mental space is *"farther out than just the linear 25 years"* [00:56:23], and the tools *"are either going to drive us mad or they're going to open up a new renaissance"* [00:56:36]. Salient **external** anchor in the same passage: if you brought someone from 200 years ago into a modern city *"they would think we're a million years into the future, not 200 years"* [00:55:47].
