## 1. What it actually is

`[EST]` A **purely theoretical/modeling** paper (zero new experiments) from three named, real PPPL
physicists. It unifies five previously-separate pieces of fusion-ignition theory (Lawson power balance,
Cordey accessibility, the marginal ignition ridge, ignition entry points, Mills thermal-runaway dynamics)
into one 0D framework that treats ignition as a **constrained dynamical pathway**, not a fixed threshold.

**The actual finding:** the **Cordey saddle** (lowest-energy route to ignition) sits where **heating the
plasma first, then raising density** beats the conventional density-first approach — a real optimization
result, not metaphor. Radiation damping (synchrotron losses, impurities) pushes that saddle toward
**higher** density/temperature requirements than the idealized Lawson picture, which in realistic 3D
geometry translates into higher required plasma β (pressure), tying ignition accessibility directly to
MHD stability limits. Also: the same loss mechanisms that make ignition *harder to reach* provide a
previously-unrecognized **stabilizing feedback against thermal runaway** once ignited — a genuine
double-edged result, not spin. Specific numbers from PPPL's own release (plausible, consistent with the
mechanism, not independently re-derived here): tungsten at ~1 part in 10,000 can roughly double the
pressure needed for ignition; liquid-lithium walls and spin-polarized fuel both partially reopen the
ignition window.
