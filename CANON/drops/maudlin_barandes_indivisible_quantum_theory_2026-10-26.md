## §2 Barandes's actual core claims, as stated in this video

**2.1 The problem list [HYP — his framing].** Barandes enumerates four problems with the Dirac–von Neumann axioms [00:16:50–00:21:33]:
- the **measurement problem** — two categorically different time-evolutions (unitary vs. collapse) with the theory not defining what "measurement" is;
- the **category problem** — the axioms deliver *only* measurement-outcome averages, so early-universe/cosmological probabilities have no home;
- the **mereology problem** — no non-contingent rule for how a subsystem of a composite evolves without helping yourself to the measurement axioms a second time;
- the **ontology problem** — the theory presupposes external observers and outcomes without saying what exists.
[EST] These are recognisably the house's own "provenance/observer" anxieties in physics vocabulary. Barandes's own favours: collapse or hidden-variables-style "new physics" — [EST] see Maudlin (1995) abstract below for the same conclusion.

**2.2 "Indivisible" = non-Markovian, as a *dynamical* (not causal-model) property [HYP, on-air; CORROBORATED].** Barandes carefully separates **[EST]** the **dynamical Markov property** of a stochastic process (the next state / its distribution is fixed by the *present* configuration alone) from the **causal Markov condition** of a Pearl causal model (a node is screened off from non-descendants once its *parents* are fixed). A process can be non-Markovian yet obey the causal Markov condition, and vice-versa [00:43:59–00:52:04]. **Indivisibility** is the *dynamical* failure: the process "**fails to divide**" between certain times — you cannot insert a valid conditioning time in between and recover the statistics by cutting the process there [02:11:57]. He calls it "a failure of iterativeness."
> **Independent confirmation:** his own paper *A Deflationary Account of Quantum Theory…* (arXiv:2602.01043, 2026-01-31) states verbatim: *"Indivisibility is a simple and remarkably new idea. One can think of it as 'a failure of iterativeness.'"* **[EST]** — the on-air definition matches the published one.

**2.3 The construction [HYP — his model; MATH-CLASS].** Start with the simplest **discrete, deterministic, logically reversible** process on N configurations (he uses N=17; then N=2 for a "qubit"); represent configurations as standard-basis vectors; the dynamics is a **permutation matrix** (hence **unitary**); take **fractional powers** of that unitary to interpolate to continuous time; **mod-square the entries entrywise** to get a real non-negative matrix; that matrix is **unistochastic** (definition: a stochastic matrix whose entries are the modulus-squares of the entries of a unitary) and hence (doubly) stochastic → a genuine stochastic process that **interpolates with an ordinary evolving probability distribution**, has **no complex numbers**, and is **not Markov** [01:59:44–02:12:15]. Alternatively the *unitary* interpolation gives a Markovian-looking picture with complex amplitudes, Born-rule mod-squaring, a Hamiltonian generator, eigenvalues, a Schrödinger equation — but *only as a representation*. **[EST]** (both interpolations are described consistently; the term "unistochastic" is defined the same way in his papers).

**2.4 Interference as non-Markovianity [HYP; the load-bearing claim].** For the 2-configuration "qubit" (a coarse-grained double-slit), computing the final distribution the *indivisible* way vs. the naive cut-the-middle **Markov approximation** differ, and *"the discrepancy is mathematically exactly the same as the formula for the interference effects"* [02:44:02–02:46:17]. His gloss [02:49:35–02:50:31]: **interference = the discrepancy between a genuinely non-Markovian evolution and its convenient Markov approximation** — "not a question of waves." He explicitly notes this is where Maudlin balks. **[HYP]** — this is Barandes's claim, presented on air as surprising to *him*; I did **not** independently re-derive the identity (verification debt).

**2.5 Measurement & collapse without collapse [HYP].** Add a second "qubit" as a **detector**; model the whole thing as one big stochastic process; **classically marginalize** over the detector. The interference terms **vanish** (exactly, if the detector coupling is ideal; partially otherwise). At the level of the stochastic law the indivisible matrix **factorizes** across the two times — *"the spontaneous generation of a new division event that wasn't there before"* [02:52:21–02:53:11]. Crucially he stresses: **"It's not a global statement. We're not saying the whole universe has a division event, but at a mereological level … it has this division event"** [02:53:11]. Other observables ("**emergables**," his coinage; the non-commuting ones) arise by *changing the detector's dynamics*; measured probabilities *"accord with the predictions of the Born rule"* [02:55:24–02:56:18]. **[HYP]** — claimed, not re-derived.

**2.6 Time direction & the second law [HYP; SMALL BUT HOUSE-RELEVANT].** Because the detector interaction is **directed** (it evolves differently forward vs. backward), Barandes says you get **spontaneous breaking of time-translation and time-reversal invariance** at that level: *"the evolution is Marov to the past but not to the future"* — and explicitly: *"there's no time irreversibility or violation of time translation invariance at a global level except maybe the very first initial"* [02:53:39–02:54:32]. Separately [02:54:32]: because unistochastic matrices are **doubly stochastic**, they are **entropy-non-decreasing** → *"something like the second law."* He repeatedly hedges: *"I'm not going to make a metaphysical statement about this. This is just a math statement"* [02:53:39]. **[HYP]** — his claim, self-flagged as mathematical, not ontological.

**2.7 The deflationary upshot [HYP].** The picture: *"just a configuration of the world … and the laws are indivisible … but they can be represented in a quasi-Markovian sense using a Hilbert-space picture."* The Hilbert-space "regalia" is *"like a hidden Markov model with complex numbers and lots of arbitrariness."* Therefore wave function / quantum state are **optional** — *"the wave function or quantum state is the hidden variable, not the local beables"* [03:02:23]. He says he is **not** claiming you *must* avoid reifying them, only that you *can*. **[HYP]** — an explicitly optional-deflationary reading, not an eliminativist proof.

## §3 Maudlin's own position — agreements and disagreements (not flattened)

**3.1 His trinity [EST — his own well-known device, stated here 00:29:00–00:29:58].** On whether the wave function "represents nothing / something-not-everything / everything": ψ-Bayesians = *nothing*; many-worlds = *everything*; Bohmians & collapse theories = *something, non-local, and partly local*. He places Barandes in the **"represents nothing"** camp.

**3.2 Maudlin's actual commitment [EST via explicit "I just believe" 00:40:40–00:41:07]:**
> *"I just believe the wave function really does represent something physical. Yes. Not something local, not something that's regular space, but it's there and it evolves continuously through time … That's a deep part of the explanation of how earlier events can be causally relevant to later events."*
He is a **ψ-realist**: a non-local, non-backreacting, continuously wave-equation-evolving entity. His evidential anchor is **two-slit interference** — something is in contact with both slits; classical wave physics is the known home of interference; the wave equation is (in his reconstruction) a Markov process [00:32:27–00:41:45].

**3.3 Where he agrees with Barandes [EST].**
- Both agree the **Dirac–von Neumann measurement axioms are broken/categorically mismatched** (decoherence cannot be a *deductive* bridge from probability-free to probability-bearing evolution) [00:17:17–00:22:52].
- Both agree on the **necessary-vs-sufficient error** in treating a decohered density matrix as epistemic [02:29:39–02:31:38] — Maudlin even names his own 1995 paper, which Barandes recommends by name.
- Both are precise-terminology maximalists and enjoy the meta-point that "**rigor doesn't stop when the symbols dry up**" [02:28:30–02:29:11].

**3.4 Where he disagrees — the hard fault lines [EST].**
- **Directly, on the deflationary core.** Maudlin: *"…you're telling me you can account for [interference] without that by this. I can't absorb. There's just no conceivable way you could explain or I could absorb how any of that is supposed to work. And … I have to say I couldn't follow"* [03:00:16–03:01:01]. He calls the account "very suggestive" but *not* explanatory *to him*.
- **On giving up the Markov condition.** Maudlin frames it as **"temporal action at a distance,"** *"a really big deal,"* and says you'd need to be *"pushed into that in a very strong way … even stronger than aesthetic preferences"* [00:39:25–00:40:40]. He accepts **spatial** Bell non-locality (Bell *forced* it) but is much warier of a *temporal* analogue.
- **On "interference = non-Markovian discrepancy."** He objects that a *two*-configuration model cannot by construction host an interference *band* pattern, and that the wave-analogy is not "some aesthetic preference" but *"a straightforward physical problem"* [02:34:52–02:47:19].
- **A sharp side-disagreement with Feynman [EST].** Maudlin: Feynman's textbook double-slit argument *"is dreadful. It's just wrong,"* because probability theory alone doesn't license the two-slit average; Barandes agrees ("preaching to the choir").
- **Barandes's counter-question to Maudlin [EST, unresolved on-air]:** in Bohm's theory, transformations that change ψ's phase without changing |ψ|² alter the guiding equation and hence the trajectories with no change in empirical output (he attributes a 1950 spin-½/Dirac-field origin and a 1999 re-derivation to Harvey Brown) → **the Bohmian trajectories look "radically unfixed."** Maudlin's reply: known, but it's a gauge-vs-physical question with *no algorithm* — you must argue case by case; the phase may be a gauge degree of freedom. **He concedes this is *"one of the things that has made me skeptical that the pilot wave could be physical, but it's not an open-and-shut case"* [03:03:03–03:08:41].** This is a rare Maudlin-side admission of doubt about his own preferred ontology, and it matters for any pitch built on "Maudlin is a committed pilot-wave realist."

**3.5 The Shoemaker story — the single most outreach-relevant passage [EST].** Barandes retells Shoemaker's *"Time Without Change"* (three civilizations that each periodically "freeze" for a year — 7/11/13-year cycles, least-common-multiple year = 1,001) [01:01:00–01:07:24]. Uses: (i) it motivates a **hidden-Markov model** whose extra "clock" variable is **non-local, non-backreacted, malleable, and unobservable in principle** [01:14:16–01:16:30]; (ii) it is the *intuition pump* for the indivisible construction ("a lot like the kind of process that Shoemaker's universe had") [02:11:57]. **Maudlin's own gloss [01:07:24–01:10:21]:** *"time is the measure of change"* (Aristotle) **but also "time is the measure of stasis"**; and, on the frozen year, *"I personally think that we were frozen for a year would be the better [hypothesis]"* — i.e. **a duration with no physical change is, in Maudlin's view, the more plausible reading** (under Quinean holism). **This directly bears on EILT's "no update = time flatlined" slogan (see §5 row 5 and §7).**
